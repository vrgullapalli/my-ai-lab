### Currently settled

- Venkat has final decision and approval authority.
- Repository canon governs the agents and runtimes operating inside that repository.
- Alfred does not outrank Chief of Staff repository governance.
- Gabriel does not outrank `AGENTS.md`.

## 13. What must always require Venkat’s approval?

Under the current Chief of Staff rules, approval is required for:

- Creating or changing consequential commitments or decisions.
- Creating, deleting, or materially changing an agent.
- Changing an agent’s responsibility, authority, or permissions.
- Granting persistent tools or access.
- Adding global rules.
- Changing governance.
- Cross-repo reads or writes that cross an existing boundary.
- External communication, publication, purchases, deployment, or client action.
- Crossing client, privacy, sensitivity, or Foundry boundaries.
- Promoting an inference into consequential canonical truth.
- Continuing beyond a declared budget or stopping rule.
- Destructive or difficult-to-reverse actions.

Memory is the important exception:

- Routine, reversible capture, classification, linking, summarization, correction, and expiration may run within approved boundaries.
- Memory changes affecting decisions, authority, behavior, privacy, other Foundries, or public claims still require approval. See [D-130](/Users/venkatgullapalli/Documents/my-ai-lab-v2/chief-of-staff/DECISIONS.md:778).

This has not yet been adopted as one global approval contract across every future Foundry.

## 14. Should governance itself be governed?

### Already established

- An agent cannot expand its own authority.
- Authority does not transfer through a name, handoff, recommendation, acknowledgement, or silence.
- Agents cannot treat their own output as approval.
- Governance changes require Venkat’s approval.
- Released decisions are changed through new decisions, not silent rewrites.

### Not yet fully designed

We have not created a cross-repo structural rule stating:

> A governance agent cannot modify its own governing policies.

That principle is consistent with current authority boundaries, but the global enforcement mechanism has not been designed or adopted.

We also have not decided:

- Who evaluates the governance agent.
- Where its governing policy lives.
- Whether it can propose changes to itself.
- Which independent check approves a new version.
- How changes propagate across repos.
- How rollback works when a governance change causes harm.

## The important systems distinction

Do not treat these as the same thing:

- A logical Foundry.
- A Git repository.
- An authority boundary.
- A deployment boundary.
- A data-isolation boundary.

We have discussed the logical Foundry network. We have not completed the repository, authority, monitoring, and enforcement architecture for that network.
