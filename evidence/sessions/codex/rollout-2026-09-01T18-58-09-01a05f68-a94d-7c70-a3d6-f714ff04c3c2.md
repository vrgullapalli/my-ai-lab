> I bring a physician’s evidence discipline, a founder’s product judgment, and two decades of experience building customer-data and decision systems across healthcare.

You’re right. “Trustworthy decision foundations” is abstract consultant language. It makes you sound impressive without telling anyone what you do.

A more honest description is:

> You’ve spent your career making messy healthcare data usable.

The recurring situation is concrete:

- Different systems describe the same customer differently.
- Teams disagree about what the data means.
- Leaders cannot tell whether an AI idea will work with what they have.
- Vendors recommend more technology before anyone understands the underlying problem.
- Nobody has a defensible answer about what to fix, build, or stop.

Your role is:

> You find where the data, technology, and organization stop matching reality. Then you help the responsible leader decide what will work and what to do next.

Your audience is the person accountable for that mess but unable to inspect every part personally:

> A pharma commercial, customer-engagement, data, or AI leader—or an agency executive serving pharma—who must make an expensive decision about customer data or AI and does not trust the answers they are currently getting.

The clearest audience-facing explanation I can currently support is:

> I help pharma leaders figure out what their customer data can support, where AI will genuinely help, and what needs to change before they spend more.

Why you:

> I’m a physician and founder who has spent more than twenty years working on healthcare customer data—from identifying the same person across disconnected systems to determining whether today’s AI can use that data reliably.

That language is less grand, but it lets someone picture the problem, recognize themselves, and understand why you belong in the room. That is the standard we should hold every positioning sentence to.
