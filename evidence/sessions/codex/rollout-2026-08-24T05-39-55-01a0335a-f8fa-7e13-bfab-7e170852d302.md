### Primary-system categories

### Problem types

- Already completed but never closed.
- Decision or clarification needed.

- Implementation plan required.

Every loop must have exactly one current closure route:

- **Evidence-close:** the required outcome already happened.

- **Human/external action:** confirmation or receipt closes it.
- **Park:** a real owner and trigger are recorded; it leaves the active queue but is not falsely called completed.

## The second distinction: commitments versus options

The tracker appears to mix two fundamentally different things:

- **Commitments:** something is owed, blocked, promised, required, or awaiting a real decision.
- **Options:** something might be useful later—a feature, pipeline, method, integration, or capability.

- A named outcome depends on it.
- A plan is approved to build it.
- A repeated failure demonstrates the need.
- A date or observable event activates it.

This is probably one of the largest hidden sources of inflation: **the tracker treats option value as workload.**

## Group by position in the dependency graph

Some loops matter far more than others even when they look similar.

- **Unlockers:** closing one releases several downstream records.
- **Dependents:** cannot move until an unlocker closes.
- **Independent actions:** can close without affecting anything else.
- **Carriers:** already contain the surviving work of another loop.
- **Cycles:** A waits on B while B waits on A.
- **Orphans:** no owner, trigger, dependent work, or closure condition.

1. Completed records needing reconciliation.
2. Root unlockers.
3. Independent high-consequence actions.
4. Everything else.
