The whole system is meant to turn messy information into useful work—without mixing everything together or giving every AI access to everything.

Think of it as a company with one front desk and several specialist rooms.

### The front desk: Chief of Staff OS

The Chief of Staff OS receives things such as:

- Notes and ideas
- Meeting transcripts
- Research
- Problems
- Client information
- Signals that something has changed

It works out what the information is about, what should happen next, and which specialist Foundry should handle it.

### The specialist rooms: Foundries

Each Foundry has a specific job.

- **Decision Foundry:** turns evidence and competing choices into a clear decision.
- **Content Foundry:** turns useful ideas into content.
- **Marketing Foundry:** turns positioning and market evidence into marketing work.
- Other Foundries could be added later.

Each Foundry owns its own work. They can cooperate, but they do not freely browse one another’s information.

### The shared foundation

The Foundries use the same basic plumbing:

- A common way to describe information
- Rules about who can access what
- Small context packages containing only what a task needs
- Records of what happened
- Links back to the original evidence
- Approval checks before important actions
- Receipts showing what was produced and why

This lets the Foundries work together without becoming one giant, messy database.

### A simple example

Imagine you record this observation:

> Buyers keep asking whether AI work will replace their employees.

The system could:

1. Save it as an observation—not immediately treat it as truth.
2. Send the relevant evidence to the Decision Foundry.
3. Explore several possible responses.
4. Produce a decision about your position.
5. Give the approved position to the Content Foundry.
6. Create an article or presentation based on it.
7. Keep a record connecting the final content to the evidence and decision.

Each step knows what it received, what it produced, and what it was allowed to do.

We built and tested the shared plumbing using fake information. The tests showed that information can move through the system, stay separated, require permission, and leave a useful record.

We have not yet built the complete everyday product. There is no finished interface, no real Foundry connection, and no client use.

In one sentence: **the system gives the right information to the right specialist AI, with clear limits, evidence, approvals, and a record of what happened.**
