> ---

> ## 1. Preserve the current analytical baseline
> 
> Treat the completed Phase 2 report as the baseline under review.
> 
> Do not reopen findings merely because a new methodological control exists.
> 
> A finding should change only if the new control:
> 
> - exposes a confound
> - changes attribution
> - narrows scope
> - strengthens cross-context support
> - weakens causal interpretation
> - changes confidence
> - reveals a reasoning-to-capability gap
> - changes whether evidence is person-level, tool-level, or configuration-level
> 
> If none applies, leave the finding unchanged.
> 
> ---
> 
> ## 2. Context-control audit
> 
> For every major capability and explanatory finding, test whether recurrence could be explained by context rather than by a stable person-level capability.
> 
> Where evidence exists, consider:
> 
> - time period
> - domain/topic
> - role
> - internal vs external work
> - paid/client vs self-directed work
> - solo vs collaborative work
> - audience
> - stakes
> - purpose
> - exploratory vs committed work
> - AI-assisted vs non-AI work
> - installed skill/tool dependency
> - source reliability
> - attribution certainty
> 
> Do not invent missing metadata.
> 
> For each major finding classify context robustness as:
> 
> - CROSS_CONTEXT
> - CONTEXT_BOUND
> - PARTIALLY_CROSS_CONTEXT
> - UNKNOWN
> 
> If the current wording is broader than the evidence supports, narrow it.
> 
> If the finding survives materially different contexts, record that as stronger support.
> 
> ---
> 
> ## 3. Reasoning evidence, where available
> 
> The separate VOICE research identified direct reasoning episodes as a potentially valuable evidence class.
> 
> Where the existing evidence estate already contains:
> 
> - direct speech
> - raw writing
> - edit history
> - corrections with explanations
> - decision records
> - disagreement or reframing episodes
> - repeated questioning
> - explicit explanations for accepting, rejecting, sequencing, or changing something
> 
> you may use those sources only when they bear directly on a capability or causal mechanism already under review.
> 
> Examples:
> 
> - reframing a presented problem before acting
> - distinguishing real constraints from inherited choices
> - making an unknown affect the decision
> - changing direction after contradictory evidence
> - identifying prerequisites before downstream work
> - rejecting an attractive interpretation after verification
> - changing the decision axis
> 
> Do NOT infer VOICE traits.
> 
> Do NOT write claims such as:
> 
> - "this is Venkat's voice"
> - "this is his communication style"
> - "this belongs in his brand"
> - "Venkat is inherently X"
> 
> The only question here is whether observed reasoning behavior strengthens, narrows, or fails to support a professional capability or mechanism.
> 
> ---

> ## 4. Reasoning-to-capability bridge
> 
> For every Phase 2 finding that depends materially on reasoning behavior, explicitly test:
> 
> reasoning behavior
> → consequential decision or action
> → work performed
> → resulting capability
> → evidence of value or outcome
> 
> If the chain breaks:
> 
> - retain the reasoning behavior as a supporting mechanism if justified
> - do not call it a demonstrated capability
> - lower or narrow the claim where necessary
> 
> A repeated reasoning pattern is not automatically a professional capability.
> 
> A professional capability requires evidence of effective performance.
> 
> ---

> ## 5. Attribution audit
> 
> Phase 2 already distinguishes:
> 
> - Venkat
> - AI/tooling
> - human-plus-AI configuration
> 
> Re-test only the major claims where attribution materially matters.
> 
> Use these categories:
> 
> ### PERSON_LEVEL
> Depends primarily on Venkat's judgment, accumulated knowledge, question selection, standards, decisions, or accountability.
> 
> ### TOOL_LEVEL
> Primarily supplied by current models, software, automation, or installed skills.
> 
> ### CONFIGURATION_LEVEL
> Exists because Venkat's judgment is combined with advisory/execution models, installed capabilities, or other tooling.
> 
> ### COLLABORATIVE
> Depends materially on another human participant or team.
> 
> ### UNCLEAR
> 
> For each important AI-era capability ask:
> 
> - What disappears if AI is removed?
> - What disappears if Venkat is removed?
> - What disappears if the installed skill/component is removed?
> - Is the result portable across models/tools?
> - Does the capability belong to the person or to the assembled operating configuration?
> 
> Do not over-attribute AI-generated execution to Venkat personally.
> 
> Do not under-attribute judgment merely because execution is machine-produced.
