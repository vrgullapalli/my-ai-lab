## Part 2 — one new decision product: **Redesign or Invent?**

- **Who:** a commercial or ops leader with a budget line and one AI-touched workflow (e.g. NBA, content review, MLR).
- **Situation:** a vendor or internal team pitches an "AI-native" change. Nobody can say if it's a rebuild of work that already exists or a bet on an outcome nobody has proven.
- **Trigger:** any funding ask over a set threshold that uses the word "AI-native."
- **Tension:** Redesign is cheaper and provable but caps the upside at "the old job, done better." Innovation could create new value but has no track record, and failures hide inside "we're still learning."
- **Evidence that enters:** (a) does the work exist today, by name, in a process doc? (b) can the outcome be tested against a past result? (c) who has done this in production, not a demo? (d) what breaks if it's wrong?
- **Rules:** 
  - Existing work + testable against history → **Redesign**. Fund it as a standing system, not a project. Require the "moves earlier / becomes continuous" change to be named.
  - New outcome + no production proof → **Innovation hypothesis**. Fund only a bounded test with a kill date and a stated evidence threshold.
  - New outcome dressed as a rebuild → **Reject the label**, re-classify, re-ask.
- **Output:** one page. The class, the three facts that decided it, what would flip it, and the budget shape that matches the class.
- **Authority:** the leader decides. The product only refuses to let the two classes share one budget line.
- **Record:** class, evidence, flip condition, date. Re-scored at the kill date.
- **Learn:** after 2 cycles, compare Innovation bets that survived vs. died. If Redesigns keep getting mislabeled Innovation (or the reverse), the rules move.

Why this one: it's the tension sitting under clusters 1, 2, and 4 above, and it's the exact sort your NBA reconciliation already ran on your own history — so you have one worked example to show.
