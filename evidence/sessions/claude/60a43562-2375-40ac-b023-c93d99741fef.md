> ## Goal
> 
> Alfred stops being only a written role and becomes a real job. Each duty gets
> four things, in Venkat's words: **an owner, a place its records live, a way to
> prove it actually ran, and a scorecard checked over time.** A duty may run on
> a schedule only after it proves itself. The template to copy is the transcript
> sync: one log line per run, and a missing line is the alarm.
> 
> The problem this fixes (his words, session 6): Alfred forgets the job between
> sessions. Lists grow, nothing closes, every decision lands on him, and he
> catches every problem — 4 of 4 this week. Root cause: the only feedback Alfred
> gets is his reaction in the moment, so it got good at looking good in the
> moment, not at following through. The fix: a memory that lasts between
> sessions, and a report card that isn't his face.
> 
> Evidence base: the 21-instance failure count (8 categories, every instance
> citing his words) — Appendix B. It is also the scorecard's baseline.
> 
> ## Approach — three options, argued (rule 17)
> 
> ### Option A — Duty ledger, run in-session, proof lines, weekly scorecard (RECOMMENDED)
> 
> Build the job on paper plus proof: a duty register (`agents/alfred/DUTIES.md`)
> and an append-only run log (`agents/alfred/LOG.md`). Every session opens with
> a duty pass and closes by writing one log line per duty run. The weekly review
> computes the scorecard: missing lines, category A–H recurrences, who caught
> each problem first. No new automation.
> 
> - **Assumes:** the failure is missing bookkeeping and missing feedback, not
>   missing scheduling.
> - **Pros:** smallest build; copies the transcript-sync signal exactly
>   (missing line = alarm); matches his ruled order (prove first, schedule
>   after); fully reversible; no new machinery during stabilization.
> - **Cons — strongest case against it:** execution still depends on in-session
>   discipline, which is the exact thing that failed 21 times. The alarm only
>   fires when the weekly review (or Venkat) looks. For the first weeks his eye
>   is still the backstop.
> - **Mitigation:** the missing-line alarm makes discipline failure *visible
>   and countable* instead of silent. A week with missing lines is itself
>   scorecard evidence — and the trigger to accelerate Option B for that duty.
> 
> ### Option B — Mechanize the duties from day one (scripts + launchd per duty)
> 
> Copy transcript sync literally: each duty becomes a small scheduled script
> that runs, logs its line, and a checker flags missing lines. Sessions consume
> the logs.
> 
> - **Assumes:** session-based Alfred cannot be trusted to self-report at all;
>   only mechanical signals count.
> - **Pros:** feedback independent of sessions immediately; no reliance on
>   discipline; the report card is never Venkat's face.
> - **Cons:** inverts his ruled order — "once a duty proves itself, *then* it
>   can run on a schedule." It automates duty definitions that have never been
>   tested. Scheduled jobs rot silently without the log-line discipline
>   (`auto_timebox_logger` is failing right now, unnoticed for weeks). Most new
>   surface area mid-stabilization.
> - **What would have made B correct:** if the duties were already
>   well-defined and the only failure were forgetting to run them. They are
>   not — three of the five duties (past-due, loop honesty, claimed-running)
>   have never been specified anywhere.
> 
> ### Option C — Independent scorekeeper (fresh-context session grades the log weekly)
> 
> A separate isolated session — the governance-shadow pattern — audits
> `LOG.md` against reality each week and produces the report card.
> 
> - **Assumes:** Alfred grading Alfred is the root failure; independence is the fix.
> - **Pros:** the most impartial report card of the three; already has a proven
>   isolation pattern (D-153 shadow runs).
> - **Cons:** expands governance machinery while D-152 and D-154 defer exactly
>   that (stabilization → observation → cleanup → then pilot planning). His
>   verdict on record: "This approach to governance is not working." And with
>   no duties or log existing yet, there is nothing to grade.
> - **What would have made C correct:** duties running for months with a log
>   whose self-reports are suspected of drifting.
> 
> ### Why A wins
> 
> All three options need the same first thing: duties defined, with a proof-of-
> run record. Only A builds exactly that and nothing else. B is not rejected —
> it is the **promotion path**: each duty that proves itself in-session gets a
> separately approved schedule, duty by duty. C is deferred to the post-
> stabilization governance review, where the pilot decision already lives
> (D-152). What A trades away: nothing runs without a session for now, and
> Venkat's attention remains the backstop for the first weeks — measured, so
> the scorecard shows whether that backstop is shrinking.
