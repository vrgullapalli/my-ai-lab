> ## Operating artifact definition
> 
> An operating artifact is:
> 
> > A reusable, bounded embodiment of operating know-how that can be invoked by a person, agent, skill, workflow, product, or system to perform a defined piece of work without acquiring agency of its own.
> 
> It is crystallized know-how that can run.
> 
> A qualifying artifact:
> 
> - Encodes a recognizable method, heuristic, decision rule, evaluation method, transformation, diagnostic, or operating sequence.
> - Performs a bounded operation.
> - Serves a goal owned elsewhere; it does not establish, maintain, reprioritize, or independently pursue that goal.
> - Can normally follow `Invoke → Perform → Return or Update`.
> - Does not require a persistent persona or continuing organizational responsibility.
> - Can travel beyond the situation that produced it.
> - Can be embedded in skills, agents, workflows, harnesses, reasoning graphs, products, instruments, human processes, or other artifacts.
> - Retains its identity when its implementation changes.
> 
> Do not confuse the artifact with its implementation or container. Content, knowledge, frameworks, prompts, templates, outputs, skills, agents, and workflows are not automatically operating artifacts. They may contain or implement bounded operating know-how that should be extracted and named separately.
> 
> Reject or decompose candidates that require broad goal ownership, arbitrary subgoals, indefinite execution, an ongoing identity, unrestricted exploration, vague “good judgment,” the entire historical corpus on every invocation, one exact historical situation, or an implementation-specific identity.
