> ## My read
> 
> The two briefs point to one main conclusion:
> 
> > **You do not need more machinery. You need the lab to use what it already knows.**
> 
> The orientation scan shows strong evidence, control, and observability, but weak retrieval, unclear context ownership, duplicated material, and execution machinery that is ahead of actual use. 
> 
> The AI-native brief reaches almost the same conclusion from the other direction: a living profile, standing reasoning, continuous learning, and proactive work all depend on being able to retrieve the right evidence and context across the lab. 
> 
> ## One thing I would change
> 
> I **would not build the standing watch first**, even though the possibility brief ranks it #1.
> 
> That is too early.
> 
> A standing reasoner that cannot reliably answer:
> 
> > **What do we already know about this?**
> 
> will become another layer of shallow monitoring.
> 
> The brief itself says retrieval across the whole lab does not exist, there are 1,061 orphan files, and the living profile depends on retrieval. 
> 
> ## What I think the architecture now is
> 
> Keep these separate:
> 
> * **Current drivers:** what you are trying to move forward now.
> * **Jobs:** what the lab actually has to do.
> * **Capabilities:** what the lab must be able to do reliably.
> * **Operating model:** deterministic mechanisms + AI reasoning + human authority.
> 
> The seven jobs in the possibility brief are useful observations. I would **not turn them into seven new systems**.
> 
> And the three-ring model is useful, but I’d treat it as the **operating model**, not a replacement for your capability families.
> 
> ## Next steps
> 
> ### 1. Make three small rulings
> 
> Only resolve what blocks the next layer:
> 
> * Is `DONE.md` still authoritative, historical, or partly both?
> * What is the canonical source for “who I am / how I work / how I think”?
> * What is actually running in the routine system: 2 or 29?
> 
> The orientation scan shows all three are currently unclear or conflicting. 
> 
> Do **not** turn this into a full cleanup project.
> 
> ### 2. Build retrieval across the lab
> 
> This should be the next real capability.
> 
> Its job:
> 
> > **Given a question or task, find what the lab already knows and return the strongest relevant evidence with its source.**
> 
> Start with:
> 
> * markdown
> * decisions
> * receipts
> * transcripts
> * seeds / points of view
> 
> No new agent.
> 
> No elaborate context schema.
> 
> No standing watch yet.
> 
> ### 3. Prove retrieval with real questions
> 
> Use questions you genuinely ask, such as:
> 
> * What have I already said about AI-native systems?
> * Why did we stop using the foundry model?
> * What have I decided about corrections becoming rules?
> * What do I believe about authority vs agency?
> * What work already exists on Telegraph belief revision?
> 
> Success means it finds the right material, shows the source, surfaces conflict, and knows when evidence is weak.
> 
> ### 4. Then build dynamic context assembly
> 
> Once retrieval works:
> 
> > task → infer what it needs → retrieve → resolve authority/conflict → assemble minimum useful context
> 
> That unlocks the living profile and much better session context. 
> 
> ### 5. Then make reasoning standing
> 
> Only then move from:
> 
> > **“I ask Alfred.”**
> 
> to:
> 
> > **“Alfred notices something changed.”**
> 
> At that point the watch can reason over your current drivers using trustworthy context rather than just sensor outputs.
> 
> ## So the sequence I’d use
> 
> **Resolve 3 blockers → Retrieval → Context assembly → Standing reasoning → Learning loop → More proactive action**
> 
> Everything else can wait.
> 
> **Deeper idea:** proactivity is probably **not a capability you build directly**. It is what emerges when the lab can retrieve what it knows, understand the current situation, remember what matters, and notice when reality changes. Building “proactivity” first risks rebuilding the checklist problem in a smarter form.
