> What AI-native structure actually means here
> Telegraph+'s rule: structure follows the agent contract, not the flow of data.

> Folder	Holds	Why it's not optional

> Note what changes: evidence/ and reference/ stop being top-level. They're inputs that agents draw on, not organizing principles. Putting them at the top is what makes it a pipeline.
> 
> How you double-check, every time
> Four tests. Apply them to any new folder, agent, or plan — including anything I propose:
> 
> The forbidden-shape test. Does the design produce judgment only at the end? If the reasoning step comes after the collection and storage steps, it's a pipeline.
> The earning test. Is anything agentic being deferred until it's "proven"? Authority may be limited from day one. Agency may not be postponed.
> The nine-questions test. Can each agent answer all nine — job, authority, state, tools, collaboration, challenge mechanism, how it stops, when it escalates, how you'd know it exceeded authority? If not, it's a recipe, not an agent.
> The first-output test. Telegraph+ ruled that its first demonstrable output is a belief that changed, with its history — not a report. What's the lab's equivalent? If the first thing it can show you is a document, you built a filing system.
> Test 3 is the one that would have caught native-lab. Test 2 is the one that would have caught the original.
