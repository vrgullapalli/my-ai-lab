# Process Is the Product
## Operational Specification for AI-Native Commercial Capabilities

## 1. Purpose

Define how an AI-native commercial capability should move work from a need, question, signal, or opportunity to a useful commercial outcome.

The capability may operate:

- inside a pharma company
- inside an agency
- across client and agency teams
- across commercial, analytics, medical, legal, regulatory, field, media, data, and technology functions
- across multiple systems and organizational boundaries

The process is not simply a sequence of automated tasks.

**The process is the reusable operating mechanism through which the capability understands the situation, applies judgment, earns permission, acts, learns, and adapts.**

---

# 2. Core Thesis

> **The output is not the product. The repeatable way the capability produces a defensible outcome is the product.**

A deck, recommendation, audience, analysis, content asset, next-best action, or decision may be an output.

The durable product is the mechanism that produced it.

That mechanism should preserve:

- what was known
- what it meant in context
- what remained unresolved
- what evidence supported proceeding
- who or what could act
- what happened
- what changed
- what was learned

---

# 3. The Six Connected Layers

Every AI-native commercial capability should operate across six inseparable layers.

| Layer | Core question |
|---|---|
| **Process** | How does the work move from need to outcome? |
| **State** | What does the capability know at this point? |
| **Context** | What does that state mean in this situation? |
| **Trust** | Is there enough support to proceed? |
| **Authority** | Who or what may make the next move? |
| **Experience** | What does each person need to see, know, or do now? |

These should not be designed separately.

They work together at every meaningful transition.

---

# 4. Relationship to Trust Is the Product

Process and Trust are not parallel systems.

They are intertwined.

### Process asks:

> What should happen next?

### Trust asks:

> Has the capability earned the right to do that next?

### Authority asks:

> Who or what may do it?

### Context asks:

> Does the answer change because of this situation?

### State asks:

> What do we currently know and where does the work stand?

### Experience asks:

> What does the relevant person need from the capability right now?

Therefore:

> **Process determines possible movement. Trust governs permitted movement. Authority determines the actor. Context changes interpretation. State preserves continuity. Experience makes the system usable.**

---

# 5. Process Is Not Workflow

A traditional workflow usually assumes:

**Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3 → Step 4**

An AI-native process should behave more like:

**State → Evaluate conditions → Determine next move → Act → Update state**

Different situations may produce different paths.

For example:

**Evidence sufficient**
→ proceed

**Evidence incomplete**
→ investigate

**Novel situation**
→ involve human judgment

**Known, low-risk situation**
→ act automatically

**Material conflict**
→ stop or escalate

**Dependency changed**
→ reopen earlier work

The process is therefore **adaptive rather than fixed**.

---

# 6. Core Operating Loop

Every capability should support some form of:

**Need → Understand → Gather → Interpret → Decide → Act → Observe → Learn**

At each transition, evaluate:

**State + Context + Trust + Authority + Experience**

before proceeding.

This creates a continuous operating loop rather than a chain of disconnected AI tasks.

---

# 7. The Process Transition Contract

Every meaningful transition should be able to answer:

| Element | Question |
|---|---|
| **Current state** | Where are we? |
| **Desired change** | What are we trying to accomplish now? |
| **Context** | What matters about this situation? |
| **Inputs** | What evidence, data, knowledge, and prior decisions are available? |
| **Trust condition** | What must be true to proceed? |
| **Authority** | Who or what may make this move? |
| **Action** | What happens next? |
| **Resulting state** | What becomes true after the action? |
| **Experience** | What does each relevant actor see or do? |
| **Exception path** | What happens if the expected conditions are not met? |
| **Propagation** | What must travel downstream? |
| **Record** | What needs to remain observable or reconstructable? |

This is the core operational object for Process Is the Product.

---

# 8. Product Principles

## Principle 1: Start from the job, not the existing workflow

Do not automate existing steps simply because they exist.

Ask:

> What should this way of working become now that AI exists?

Existing workflows are evidence about current constraints, not automatically the future design.

---

## Principle 2: Design transitions, not just tasks

A task tells the capability what to do.

A transition defines:

- when it should happen
- what must be true
- what changes afterward
- what happens if it cannot proceed

This is what turns automation into an operating capability.

---

## Principle 3: State must persist

The capability should not repeatedly rediscover what the organization already established.

Preserve:

- decisions
- evidence
- assumptions
- unresolved issues
- approvals
- changes
- restrictions
- prior judgment

The next step should begin from the current state, not from zero.

---

## Principle 4: Context changes the process

The same information may require different actions depending on:

- brand
- market
- audience
- client
- role
- workflow stage
- channel
- regulatory environment
- commercial objective
- consequence

Context should alter routing, judgment, trust requirements, and experience.

---

## Principle 5: Trust governs every meaningful transition

Trust is not a final QA gate.

If evidence weakens, sources conflict, context changes, or a boundary is reached, the process itself should change.

It may:

- narrow
- investigate
- pause
- reroute
- escalate
- require judgment
- stop

---

## Principle 6: Human judgment should be intentionally placed

Do not add humans everywhere.

Define:

- which decisions genuinely require judgment
- whose judgment matters
- what that person needs to see
- what their decision changes

AI should handle work that does not benefit from human judgment.

Humans should enter where their judgment changes the outcome.

---

## Principle 7: Authority should determine orchestration

The capability should know not only what could happen next but who is authorized to make it happen.

Authority may belong to:

- the AI
- a specific person
- a role
- a team
- another organization
- an approval body

Authority can change as trust changes.

---

## Principle 8: Exceptions are part of the product

Do not design only the happy path.

Common exceptions should have explicit behavior.

Examples:

- missing evidence
- conflicting evidence
- ambiguous identity
- outdated brief
- client strategy change
- unauthorized data use
- unexpected model output
- rejected recommendation
- failed execution
- changed approval condition

An exception should not force people to abandon the capability and invent a manual workaround.

---

## Principle 9: Handoffs must preserve meaning

Work often crosses:

**brand → agency → analytics → creative → review → media → field**

A handoff should preserve:

- evidence
- context
- assumptions
- decisions
- trust state
- authority
- restrictions
- unresolved issues

The next team should not need to reconstruct why the work exists.

---

## Principle 10: Process should be observable

The organization should be able to understand:

- where work is
- why it is there
- where it is getting stuck
- where humans intervene
- where AI fails
- what gets overridden
- which exceptions repeat
- whether outcomes improve

Observability is not only for audit.

It is how the process itself gets better.

---

## Principle 11: Learning should improve the process

Repeated use should reveal:

- missing context
- unnecessary steps
- recurring exceptions
- poor handoffs
- misplaced judgment
- weak trust conditions
- unnecessary human intervention

The process should evolve from this evidence.

But learning must not silently change authority or governance.

---

## Principle 12: Reuse the mechanism, preserve the context

The goal is not to create one universal process.

Separate:

**what should travel**

from:

**what must remain contextual**

Reusable elements might include:

- transition logic
- trust rules
- evidence patterns
- escalation mechanisms
- state structures
- review patterns

Brand-, market-, client-, or role-specific context may remain local.

---

# 9. Process State

Every capability should maintain enough persistent state to answer:

### Where are we?
Current stage or condition.

### What do we know?
Relevant evidence and facts.

### What have we decided?
Human and AI decisions.

### What remains unresolved?
Gaps, conflicts, questions, exceptions.

### What has changed?
Material differences since previous work.

### What is permitted?
Current authority and restrictions.

### What should happen next?
Available transitions.

State is what allows the process to survive time, handoffs, people, and systems.

---

# 10. Context Layer

Context gives state meaning.

Relevant context may include:

- business objective
- brand strategy
- market
- customer
- indication
- audience
- client
- contract
- current campaign
- approved content
- policies
- decision history
- organizational norms
- role
- channel
- local constraints

Context should be assembled based on the job.

Not every capability needs everything.

---

# 11. Trust Layer

At every consequential transition, determine:

- Is the evidence sufficient?
- Is it current?
- Are important conflicts unresolved?
- Are assumptions acceptable?
- Does this use fit the conditions under which the capability was validated?
- Has anything material changed?
- Is human judgment required?

The result should change what the process can do next.

---

# 12. Authority Layer

For each transition define:

### AI may act
Routine, supported, bounded.

### AI may propose
Human judgment still required.

### Human must decide
Consequence, ambiguity, or organizational responsibility requires it.

### Another role or organization must decide
The capability has reached its decision boundary.

### Nobody may proceed
Required trust conditions are not met.

Authority is part of the process state.

---

# 13. Experience Layer

The process should not feel like operating a workflow engine.

People should experience:

- a clear current state
- an obvious next move
- relevant context already present
- AI handling routine work without unnecessary interruption
- human involvement only when useful
- clear explanation when the process changes course
- evidence available when judgment is required
- unresolved issues that remain visible
- easy resumption after time away
- handoffs that do not require re-explaining everything

The desired experience is:

> **I do not have to manage the process, but I can understand and intervene when it matters.**

---

# 14. Role-Specific Process Experience

Different actors interact with the same process differently.

### Commercial lead
Decisions, unresolved issues, implications, next moves.

### Agency team
Brief, context, evidence, assumptions, client boundaries.

### Analyst
Data, methods, evidence gaps, interpretation.

### Creative
Strategy, constraints, approved inputs, open creative space.

### MLR or other reviewers
Only what requires their judgment plus relevant evidence and history.

### Field
Approved actions or content appropriate to the current situation.

### Capability owner
Performance, exceptions, bottlenecks, overrides, trust degradation.

### Audit / compliance
Reconstructable history when required.

One process.

Different experiences.

---

# 15. Cross-Organization Process

When the capability spans organizations, explicitly preserve:

- ownership
- permissions
- confidentiality
- IP rights
- client-specific context
- reuse conditions
- decision rights
- approval boundaries
- responsibilities
- handoff state

The process should know when work has crossed an organizational boundary and what that changes.

---

# 16. Failure Behavior

Failure should change the process.

Possible responses:

- retry
- gather evidence
- narrow scope
- reroute
- request clarification
- involve another role
- rollback
- suspend
- stop
- reopen an earlier state

Do not silently continue after a material process failure.

---

# 17. Process Observability

Measure specific process behavior.

Useful measures include:

- time spent in each state
- repeated exceptions
- human interventions
- override patterns
- rework
- failed handoffs
- stalled transitions
- missing-context events
- blocked actions
- unnecessary approvals
- outcome quality
- changes between initial and final decisions

Avoid measuring only speed.

A faster bad process is still a bad process.

---

# 18. Process Learning Loop

The capability should periodically ask:

> Which parts of the process are creating value?

> Which steps exist because of old constraints?

> Where does human judgment materially improve outcomes?

> Where does AI routinely succeed?

> Which exceptions are becoming normal?

> What context is repeatedly missing?

> Which controls are unnecessary?

> Which trust conditions are too weak or too strict?

The goal is continuous redesign of the operating mechanism.

---

# 19. Minimum Capability Process Spec

Every AI-native commercial capability should define:

### Desired change
What should become better or newly possible?

### Job
What must the capability accomplish?

### Actors
Who participates or is affected?

### Process states
What meaningful states can the work occupy?

### Transitions
How does it move between them?

### Context
What affects interpretation?

### Trust conditions
What must be true to move?

### Authority
Who or what may make each move?

### Experience
What does each actor need at each state?

### Exceptions
What can go wrong?

### Propagation
What must travel downstream?

### Observability
What needs to be monitored?

### Learning
How will the process improve?

### Outcome
How do we know the process created value?

---

# 20. Acceptance Criteria

Process Is the Product is operational only when:

1. The process begins with the job, not the existing workflow.
2. Meaningful states are explicit.
3. Transitions have clear conditions.
4. Context changes interpretation and routing where appropriate.
5. Trust determines whether consequential transitions may occur.
6. Authority is explicit at each meaningful decision.
7. Human judgment is intentionally placed.
8. State survives handoffs and time.
9. Important context travels with the work.
10. Exceptions have designed behavior.
11. Cross-organizational boundaries are preserved.
12. The process is observable.
13. Failures change behavior.
14. Learning improves the process without silently expanding authority.
15. Reusable mechanisms are separated from local context.
16. The outcome can be traced back through the process that produced it.
17. The process itself becomes better through use.

---

# 21. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- automating the existing workflow step for step
- calling a prompt chain a process
- losing state between agents or tools
- asking humans to approve everything
- adding trust checks only at the end
- treating context as static metadata
- using one workflow for every situation
- hiding exceptions outside the system
- handoffs that strip away rationale
- measuring success only through speed
- capturing logs without learning from them
- allowing AI learning to silently change decision rights
- creating so many branches that the process becomes impossible to understand

---

# 22. Canonical Definition

> **Process Is the Product means the AI-native commercial capability is designed as a persistent, adaptive way of working rather than a collection of AI tasks.**
>
> Process moves the work.
>
> State preserves what is known.
>
> Context gives that state meaning.
>
> Trust determines whether movement is justified.
>
> Authority determines who or what may act.
>
> Experience makes the right part of the system visible and usable to each person.
>
> Together, they create a commercial capability that can reliably move from need to outcome, survive handoffs and change, incorporate human judgment where it matters, and improve through use.
