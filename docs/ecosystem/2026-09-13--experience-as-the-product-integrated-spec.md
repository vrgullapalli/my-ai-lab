# Experience as the Product
## Integrated AI-Native Commercial Capability Specification

## 1. Purpose

Define how people experience and shape an **AI-native commercial capability** across pharma companies, agencies, partners, teams, and systems.

Experience is not just UI.

It is the live connection between the person and the capability.

> **Experience as the Product means the capability turns what it knows, remembers, believes, trusts, and is allowed to do into the simplest useful experience for the person and situation, while making sure that person's actions correctly change the capability in return.**

---

# 2. Core Outcome

The person should experience:

> **It knows where we are.**  
> **It understands what matters here.**  
> **It remembers what matters from before.**  
> **It shows me what changed.**  
> **I can tell what I can rely on.**  
> **It handles routine work without bothering me.**  
> **It brings me in when my judgment matters.**  
> **When I act or correct it, the system actually changes.**

The goal is not more interaction.

The goal is **less unnecessary coordination and better judgment when interaction matters**.

---

# 3. Integrated Capability Model

Experience sits across the full capability.

| Capability layer | Core question |
|---|---|
| **Process** | What should happen next? |
| **State** | What is true now? |
| **Memory** | What from the past should still matter? |
| **Context** | What does this situation mean here? |
| **Evidence** | What supports what we believe or claim? |
| **Trust** | What can safely be relied on? |
| **Authority** | Who or what may decide or act? |
| **Experience** | What should this person see, know, decide, or do now? |
| **Evaluation & Learning** | Did it work, and what should improve? |

Experience should not duplicate these layers.

It **projects and connects them**.

---

# 4. Integrated Runtime

At every meaningful moment:

**Person / event / task**  
↓  
**Determine current Process + State**  
↓  
**Retrieve relevant Memory**  
↓  
**Assemble current Context + Evidence**  
↓  
**Evaluate Trust**  
↓  
**Resolve Authority**  
↓  
**Determine what this person needs now**  
↓  
**Create the smallest useful Experience**  
↓  
**Person acts, decides, corrects, or does nothing**  
↓  
**Write consequences back to the capability**  
↓  
**Update State / Memory / Context / Trust / Process**  
↓  
**Observe outcome and learn**

This loop runs continuously.

# 5. Experience Is Two-Way

## Capability → Person

The capability may:

- act quietly
- inform
- recommend
- explain
- ask
- warn
- request judgment
- restrict
- block
- escalate

## Person → Capability

The person may:

- approve
- reject
- correct
- add context
- challenge
- defer
- override
- narrow
- delegate
- stop
- teach

Both directions must have real consequences.

---

# 6. Experience Contract

For every meaningful interaction, answer seven questions.

## 1. What does this person need to understand now?

Usually some combination of:

- where things stand
- what changed
- what matters
- what is unresolved
- what needs attention
- what happens next

Do not show everything merely because it exists.

---

## 2. What should the capability already know?

The person should not need to repeat:

- settled decisions
- current strategy
- known constraints
- prior corrections
- ownership
- active work
- established preferences
- known evidence
- previous outcomes

Ask again only when something is missing, stale, conflicting, or important enough to verify.

---

## 3. What changed?

Show meaningful change plus impact.

Not:

> 12 files updated.

Instead:

> Customer segmentation changed. Three active recommendations may need review.

---

## 4. What can safely be relied on?

Make meaningful differences visible:

- established
- supported
- provisional
- inferred
- disputed
- stale
- unknown

The person should not have to guess.

---

## 5. What actually requires human judgment?

Bring people in for:

- strategic choices
- consequential ambiguity
- material exceptions
- competing reasonable options
- new authority
- high-impact actions
- decisions the organization has reserved for people

Routine work should remain routine.

---

## 6. What control should the person have?

Depending on consequence:

- inspect
- correct
- approve
- reject
- defer
- override
- undo
- narrow
- escalate
- pause
- stop

Control should match real authority.

---

## 7. What should change because of this interaction?

The interaction may change:

- State
- Memory
- Context
- Trust
- Authority
- Process
- downstream work
- future behavior
- Evaluation evidence

A meaningful interaction should not end with:

> Thanks for your feedback.

---

# 7. Interconnection with Process

## Process → Experience

Provides:

- current stage
- available transitions
- blocked work
- exceptions
- next possible actions

## Experience → Process

Human action may:

- advance
- pause
- redirect
- reopen
- cancel
- complete

## Operational rule

> **Show the person the decision or action that matters, not the workflow machinery behind it.**

---

# 8. Interconnection with State

## State → Experience

Provides:

- current status
- active decisions
- unresolved issues
- important changes
- available actions

## Experience → State

Interactions may:

- confirm
- reject
- correct
- supersede
- resolve
- reopen

## Operational rule

> **Every important status shown to the person should represent real system state and change behavior.**

---

# 9. Interconnection with Memory

## Memory → Experience

Provides relevant:

- prior decisions
- corrections
- rationale
- outcomes
- exceptions
- useful history

## Experience → Memory

Preserve durable lessons such as:

- why a decision was made
- why something was rejected
- a repeated correction
- an important outcome
- an exception likely to matter again

## Operational rule

> **Memory should remove repetition without making old experience silently control new situations.**

---

# 10. Interconnection with Context

## Context → Experience

Determines what matters for:

- this user
- this role
- this brand
- this client
- this market
- this task
- this moment

## Experience → Context

People may:

- supply missing context
- correct wrong context
- clarify scope
- identify an exception
- tell the capability what changed

## Operational rule

> **The experience should feel specific to the situation, not like a generic AI response with more data attached.**

---

# 11. Interconnection with Evidence

## Evidence → Experience

Supports:

- claims
- recommendations
- explanations
- uncertainty
- comparison
- review

## Experience → Evidence

People may:

- add evidence
- challenge a source
- reject weak support
- flag contradiction
- confirm interpretation

## Operational rule

Use **progressive evidence disclosure**.

### Normal work
Show little.

### Important judgment
Show enough to decide.

### Review
Show deeper evidence and provenance.

### Audit
Make the full chain reconstructable.

---

# 12. Interconnection with Trust

## Trust → Experience

Determines:

- how strongly something is presented
- whether uncertainty is visible
- whether the capability proceeds
- whether review is needed
- whether something is blocked

## Experience → Trust

Human:

- validation
- correction
- override
- rejection
- outcomes

may change the evidence supporting future reliance.

## Operational rule

> **Trust problems must change capability behavior, not just add warnings.**

---

# 13. Interconnection with Authority

## Authority → Experience

Determines:

- what AI may do quietly
- what AI may recommend
- what requires approval
- what the person may change
- what nobody may do

## Experience → Authority

Authorized people may:

- approve
- delegate
- narrow permission
- revoke
- override

## Operational rule

> **Never ask for approval where approval changes nothing, and never hide a consequential action behind apparent automation.**

---

# 14. Interconnection with Evaluation & Learning

## Evaluation → Experience

May surface:

- repeated failure
- weak outcomes
- unusual overrides
- degraded performance
- emerging patterns

## Experience → Evaluation

Interactions provide evidence through:

- acceptance
- rejection
- corrections
- intervention
- abandonment
- outcomes

## Operational rule

> **Do not confuse interaction with success.**

The user clicking “accept” is not enough.

Ask whether the capability helped produce a better outcome.

---

# 15. Core Experience Moments

Design these moments before designing screens.

## A. Return

The person comes back after time away.

Show:

**Current**  
Where things stand.

**Changed**  
What materially changed.

**Open**  
What remains unresolved.

**Needs you**  
What requires judgment.

---

## B. Normal work

The capability has enough context, trust, and authority.

Experience:

> **Stay out of the way.**

Do routine work quietly.

---

## C. Uncertainty

Something material is weak or unknown.

Experience:

> Here's what I know.  
> Here's what's missing.  
> Here's what that changes.

Offer the appropriate next move.

---

## D. Judgment

Human judgment matters.

Present:

- the decision
- relevant context
- evidence
- unresolved issue
- realistic choices
- likely consequence

Do not make the person reconstruct the problem.

---

## E. Material change

Something changed that affects active work.

Show:

> **What changed → what it affects → what needs attention**

---

## F. Correction

A person corrects the capability.

The system should:

1. understand the correction
2. update relevant State or Context
3. determine downstream impact
4. preserve useful Memory
5. reassess Trust if needed
6. tell the person what changed

---

## G. Handoff

Work moves between people, teams, or organizations.

Transfer:

- current state
- relevant context
- evidence
- decisions
- assumptions
- restrictions
- open questions
- authority

The next person should not need the previous person's brain.

---

## H. Failure

Explain:

- what failed
- what remains safe
- what was affected
- what the capability already did
- what now requires human attention

---

# 16. Core Experience Surfaces

Do not start with a large dashboard.

Start with six reusable surfaces.

## Now

> What matters now?

- Current
- Open
- Blocked
- Next

## Changed

> What is different since I last interacted?

- change
- impact
- affected work

## Needs You

> What actually requires my judgment?

For each item:

- decision
- why it needs this person
- evidence
- options
- consequence

## Why

> Why is the capability saying or doing this?

Reveal:

- context
- evidence
- assumptions
- trust limits

## History

> How did we get here?

- previous state
- decisions
- changes
- corrections
- outcomes

## Control

> What may I do?

Show only controls available under current authority.

---

# 17. Experience State Should Expand and Contract

The interface should become more detailed only when conditions require it.

### Routine
Minimal.

### User asks why
Expand evidence and context.

### Uncertainty
Expose limits.

### Change
Surface impact.

### Judgment required
Create a decision experience.

### Failure
Increase explanation and control.

### Audit
Expose full history.

This avoids both:

- hiding too much
- overwhelming everyone with governance machinery

# 19. Cross-Organization Experience

Commercial capabilities may span:

**pharma → agency → analytics partner → media → field**

Experience should preserve meaning across boundaries without exposing everything.

The capability must know:

- what may cross
- what stays internal
- who owns the information
- who owns the decision
- which context remains valid
- what restrictions travel with the work

A good handoff reduces coordination without removing responsibility.

# 20. Experience Write-Back Contract

Every consequential interaction should define:

### Interaction

What did the person do?

### Meaning

What did that action mean?

### State impact

What changes now?

### Context impact

Does any assumption, scope, or condition change?

### Memory impact

What should remain useful later?

### Trust impact

Does reliance increase, decrease, or remain unchanged?

### Authority impact

Does permission change?

### Process impact

What happens next?

### Downstream impact

What other work is affected?

### Learning

What evidence should Evaluation capture?

This closes the experience loop.

# 21. Example: Agency Recommendation

An agency capability develops a recommendation.

### System underneath

**State:** Recommendation proposed.  
**Memory:** Similar recommendation previously failed because of payer access.  
**Context:** US launch, current brand strategy, present payer conditions.  
**Evidence:** Research + client data + field signals.  
**Trust:** Strong overall, one unresolved assumption.  
**Authority:** Agency may recommend; client must decide.

### Client experience

> **Recommendation ready**
>
> This fits the current launch strategy.
>
> The main evidence is consistent across three sources.
>
> One payer-access assumption remains unresolved.
>
> **Accept provisionally · Investigate first · Reject**
>
> **Why this recommendation?**

The client chooses **Investigate first**.

### Write-back

**State**
→ Decision pending evidence.

**Process**
→ Investigation branch begins.

**Memory**
→ No durable decision yet.

**Context**
→ Payer access becomes required context.

**Trust**
→ Recommendation remains provisional.

**Authority**
→ No downstream execution allowed.

**Experience**
→ Client no longer sees this as ready to implement.

That is Experience as the Product working across the full capability.

# 22. What Good Feels Like

The person should rarely think about the architecture.

They should experience:

- fewer repeated questions
- fewer unnecessary approvals
- fewer status meetings
- fewer lost decisions
- less digging through history
- less uncertainty about what is current
- clearer decisions
- better handoffs
- fewer surprises
- easier recovery when something goes wrong

The capability should feel:

> **aware enough to help, restrained enough to trust, and clear enough to control.**

---

# 23. Experience Failure Modes

Avoid:

- asking for context already known
- forgetting important prior decisions
- showing stale state as current
- treating AI inference as fact
- evidence hidden behind confident recommendations
- warnings that change nothing
- approvals that have no effect
- corrections that disappear
- endless confirmation requests
- unnecessary notifications
- generic experiences across different roles
- dashboards showing system activity rather than what matters
- handoffs that strip away context
- AI taking consequential action without clear authority
- human judgment requested where simple automation would suffice

---

# 24. Experience Observability

Measure conditions that expose friction or failure.

Examples:

- repeated questions
- unnecessary approvals
- ignored alerts
- corrections
- overrides
- rework
- failed handoffs
- abandoned tasks
- decisions delayed waiting for context
- lost write-backs
- unexplained state changes
- user interventions caused by missing context

Do not optimize for engagement.

A capability requiring **less interaction** may be working better.

# 25. Evaluation

Evaluate Experience across:

### Clarity
Does the person understand where things stand?

### Continuity
Can they resume without reconstructing the past?

### Relevance
Are they shown what matters now?

### Trust
Can they tell what can safely be relied on?

### Control
Can they intervene appropriately?

### Judgment
Are humans involved where their judgment matters?

### Restraint
Does the capability avoid unnecessary interruption?

### Write-back
Do decisions and corrections change the system correctly?

### Handoff
Can another person continue without rebuilding context?

### Outcome
Does the experience improve decisions, action, or work?

# 27. Acceptance Criteria

Experience as the Product is operational when:

1. Experience reflects current State.
2. Relevant Memory prevents unnecessary repetition.
3. Context changes the experience when the situation changes.
4. Evidence is available when needed.
5. Trust changes capability and interaction behavior.
6. Authority determines real controls.
7. Process determines meaningful next actions.
8. Routine work creates little unnecessary friction.
9. Important changes become visible.
10. Human judgment is requested intentionally.
11. Decisions and corrections write back correctly.
12. Important consequences propagate downstream.
13. Different roles receive appropriate projections.
14. Handoffs preserve usable understanding.
15. Failures produce clear recovery experiences.
16. Interactions create evidence for learning.
17. People can return later and quickly understand where things stand.
18. The overall experience reduces organizational coordination burden.

---

# 28. Canonical Definition

> **Experience as the Product means an AI-native commercial capability continuously turns its Process, State, Memory, Context, Evidence, Trust, Authority, and Learning into the simplest useful interaction for each person and situation, while ensuring that each meaningful human interaction correctly changes the capability in return.**

The capability should:

- know enough not to keep asking
- remember enough not to repeat mistakes
- understand enough to be situational
- show enough evidence to support judgment
- know when it can proceed
- know when it needs a person
- provide real control
- preserve the consequences of human decisions
- improve through use

The experience is not the interface sitting on top of the capability.

**It is how the capability becomes usable by the organization.**
