# Specimen: "What lawn care can teach you about data strategy" (LinkedIn, ~2024, pasted by Venkat 2026-09-09)

I never gave much thought about lawns.

Until recently, I didn't even have a lawn to worry about! But then I moved to the suburbs, and suddenly, I went from not caring to being somewhat obsessed with having the best looking lawn on the block.

Along the way, I was pleasantly surprised by what the experience taught me about the similarities between lawn care and data strategy.

Initial Indifference

At first, I thought lawn care was basic: mow, water, maybe throw some fertilizer around. I didn't pay much attention beyond that. Many businesses do the same with data. They focus on the basics until they can't ignore it anymore. Then it's a rush to fix things. Just as a well-maintained lawn doesn't happen by accident, neither does a solid data strategy.

The Neighborhood Pressure

All my neighbors have near-perfect lawns, and I can't help but feel the need to make sure mine is just as good (if not better). Businesses react the same way when they see competitors excelling with data. They realize they need to step up their game, gaining a competitive edge and staying relevant.

Overcompensating

Feeling the pressure, I bought top-of-the-line tools,..mower, edger, leaf blower, etc. I went full throttle, and in doing so, made key mistakes like mowing too low and over-fertilizing. My lawn ended up brown, patchy, and lumpy. Businesses often make the same mistake, investing in expensive tools without understanding their use, which can lead to tech debt and inefficiencies. The solution? Start with a solid plan and gradually integrate new tools.

Why This Matters

Just like my neighbors weren't impressed by my patchy lawn, your clients won't be impressed by a messy data strategy. Start with the basics: understand your needs, assess your current data, and make a plan. Skipping these steps is like expecting a green lawn without watering it. A well-executed data strategy enhances client satisfaction and drives business growth.

Getting It Right

Four months in, and I feel like I have a handle on it. I follow a lawn care schedule and best practices: grass height, how often to mow and water, and even following the city water restrictions (drought, ugh). Similarly, success in data strategy comes from understanding your environment, following best practices, and maintaining consistency.

The Hard Truth

The truth is that this stuff isn't easy, but it's important. I ended up wasting a lot of time fixing my lawn because I didn't do it right the first time.

So, before you dive headfirst into data, remember this: Take a step back, embrace simplicity, and start with a plan. Think of it like taking care of your lawn. Patchy grass and messy data are both unimpressive.

Plus, do you really want your neighbors or competitors talking about how your data strategy is as bad as your lawn care?
