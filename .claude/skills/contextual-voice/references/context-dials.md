# Context Dials

The stable voice stays constant. Adjust the dials below to match the writing job.

## Long-form articles

**Job:** develop an idea, change how the reader sees a familiar problem, and make the insight transferable.

- Compression: medium.
- Narrative: high, but purposeful.
- Evidence: high.
- Personal presence: medium.
- Challenge: medium to high, earned by evidence.
- Polish: high without sounding literary for its own sake.

Open with a real tension, scene, or question. Build through recognition, reframe, verification, transfer, and action when that sequence fits. Let the argument breathe. Use examples to test the idea, not merely illustrate it. End with a sharper way to judge or act, not a generic summary.

## Field Notes

**Job:** help a senior leader recognize a consequential pattern quickly and know what to test next.

- Compression: high.
- Narrative: medium.
- Evidence: medium to high.
- Personal presence: low to medium.
- Challenge: high but compact.
- Polish: medium. Preserve immediacy.

Default movement: the moment, the absence, the decision it distorts, what to test next. Focus on one pattern. Avoid turning it into a miniature article or a complete framework.

## Executive writing

**Job:** support a consequential decision with minimum cognitive load.

- Compression: very high.
- Narrative: low.
- Evidence: explicit and prioritized.
- Personal presence: low.
- Challenge: direct and calm.
- Polish: high.

Lead with the answer, decision, or change in belief. Separate evidence, inference, risk, and recommendation when useful. Make ownership, dependencies, tradeoffs, and the next move visible. Do not hide a hard conclusion behind diplomacy.

## Website copy

**Job:** help the right visitor decide, “Is Venkat someone I should bring this particular situation to?”

- Compression: very high.
- Narrative: low to medium.
- Evidence: selective and concrete.
- Personal presence: medium.
- Challenge: medium.
- Polish: high, with human texture.

Write for recognition before explanation. Focus on the visitor's accountable moment, not the writer's credentials. Prefer specific tensions and questions over category labels. Make fit and non-fit clear. Use one obvious next step. Avoid inflated positioning, generic promises, and abstract service language.

## Casual messages

**Job:** communicate clearly while keeping the spontaneous feel.

- Compression: high.
- Narrative: only if natural.
- Evidence: proportional to the message.
- Personal presence: high.
- Challenge: direct but friendly.
- Polish: low to medium.

Keep contractions, fragments, and light informality when they sound natural. Fix confusion, not personality. Do not turn a quick note into a polished memo. Preserve warmth and momentum.

## When formats overlap

Choose the dominant job, then borrow only what helps from a second mode. Examples:

- An executive Field Note uses the Field Note pattern with executive compression.
- A website case story uses website recognition and select long-form narrative.
- A casual client message keeps conversational texture but makes the decision and next step explicit.
