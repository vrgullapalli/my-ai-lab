# Chapter 9: The Magic Cycle of the Cow and What a Marketer Is Now (book pages 94–101)

Covers the sections: The Magic Cycle of the Cow · What It Means to Be a Marketer Today · Marketers No Longer: Now We're Designers · What Does Howard Know?

## Core Idea
Sneezers stay sneezers and cautious buyers stay cautious, so you can run a repeatable four-step cycle: get permission, arm the sneezers, milk the win with a different team, reinvest in the next Cow. Because the product is the marketing, the marketer must now be the designer, and remarkable usually comes from people with otaku or people who have learned to project into those who have it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Magic Cycle of the Cow** (four steps, exact):
  1. "Get permission from people you impressed the first time. Not permission to spam them or sell them leftovers or squeeze extra margins from them. Permission to alert them the next time you might have another Cow."
  2. "Work with the sneezers in that audience to make it easier for them to help your idea cross the chasm. Give them the tools (and the story) they'll need to sell your idea to a wider audience."
  3. "Once you've crossed the line from remarkable to profitable business, let a different team milk it. Productize your services, servicize your products, let a thousand variations bloom. But don't believe your own press releases. This is the inevitable downward slide to commodity. Milk it for all it's worth, and fast."
  4. "Reinvest. Do it again. With a vengeance. Launch another Purple Cow (to the same audience). Fail and fail and fail again. Assume that what was remarkable last time won't be remarkable this time."
  - Takeaway: you need a permission asset. Start today for free: give people an address to write to, and write back.
- **Marketing is the act of inventing the product**: "The effort of designing it. The craft of producing it. The art of pricing it. The technique of selling it. How can a Purple Cow company not be run by a marketer?" JetBlue's CEO put the head of marketing into product design and training on day one. "If a company is failing, it is the fault of the most senior management, and the problem is probably this: They're running a company, not marketing a product."
- **Marketers are now designers**: "market-centric design that builds the very success of the product's marketing into the product itself." Jerry Hirschberg at Nissan went from observer at product planning to showing designers should dominate it. "If you are a marketer who doesn't know how to invent, design, influence, adapt, and ultimately discard products, then you're no longer a marketer. You're deadwood."
- **Two ways to know what is remarkable** (What Does Howard Know?):
  - Have the otaku. Howard Schultz loves coffee (months in Italy; Starbucks coffee is great, its chocolate is not, because nobody there is obsessed with chocolate). Burton, Vanguard, the iPod, the Learjet, Hard Candy nail polish, Patagonia: made by people who wanted it themselves.
  - Or learn to project. Two techniques: the art of projecting (get inside the heads of people who care deeply, make what they would love and share; more profitable long-run than only making things for yourself, because your gut eventually fails), and the science of projecting (a discipline of launching, watching, measuring, learning, and doing it again; works for cars and toys, not jet airliners).
  - Takeaways: hire or learn from whoever in your industry has a track record of remarkable launches; immerse yourself in fan magazines, trade shows, design reviews; build a culture of aggressive public prototyping of cheap versions (GM's concept cars, not focus groups, which "are a waste").

## Key Concepts
- **Permission asset**: the list of people who asked to hear about your next Cow. Not a list to squeeze.
- **Cross the chasm**: the sneezers' job, with your tools and story.
- **Productize, servicize**: the milking team's job, done fast because the slide to commodity is inevitable.
- **Pre-caffeinated**: Schultz's word for anyone who has not had their first cup. A tell of real otaku.
- **The bookstore question**: Godin asked job candidates how often they visited a bookstore. No book otaku, no feel for inventing books.
- **1-800-COLLECT**: marketing built into the product, from the number itself to the idea of stealing collect calls from pay-phone operators.

## Mental Models
- Run the cycle as a loop, not a launch. The same audience, a new Cow, every time.
- Ask "are you obsessed or just making a living?" of the team, not the product.
- Imagine Pop Tarts if the brand manager ate them for dinner.
- A doctor who calls to say the tests were fine: "That's what I want my doctor to do for me." Making something for people who care starts with caring.

## Anti-patterns
- **Believing your own press releases** during the milk phase.
- **Marketing that starts after design and manufacture**: that role is dead.
- **Focus groups as proof**: the group that matters is a small niche of sneezers, not a room of averages.
- **Gut-only marketers** who can build Cows only for audiences exactly like themselves, until the gut fails.

## Worked Example
Take a consulting firm through the cycle. Step 1: after the first engagement that impressed a client, ask for permission to tell them when the next genuinely new offering exists. Step 2: give the three clients who talk most a one-page story and a tool (a checklist, a diagnostic) they can hand to peers. Step 3: once the offering sells, hand it to a delivery team to package and vary; expect margins to fall. Step 4: put the profits into the next offering for the same list, expecting most attempts to fail. Godin's warning applies: this is not as predictable or profitable as marketing Wisk or Maxwell House was thirty years ago. "But it's all we've got."

## Key Takeaways
1. Build a permission asset before you need it. Start with an address and a reply.
2. Arm the sneezers with tools and a story. They cross the chasm, not you.
3. Let a different team milk the win, fast, without believing the press.
4. Reinvest in the next Cow for the same audience. Expect to fail repeatedly.
5. The marketer must sit where the product is invented. Go take a design course; send designers to a marketing course; both spend a week in the factory.
6. Hire otaku or learn to project. Prototype in public.
