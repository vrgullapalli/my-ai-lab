# Chapter 5: The Problem with the Cow Is Fear (book pages 45–54)

Covers the sections: The Problem with the Cow · Follow the Leader · Case Study: The Aeron Chair

## Core Idea
Remarkable is rare not because ideas are scarce but because people are afraid of criticism. In a crowded market, fitting in is failing. "Safe is risky." Boring always leads to failure, and the unpredictability of the outcome is the point, not a flaw.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Safe is risky**: "In a crowded marketplace, fitting in is failing. In a busy marketplace, not standing out is the same as being invisible." And: "Boring is always the most risky strategy."
- **The catch-22 of the Cow** (from Jon Spoelstra's Marketing Outrageously): in tough times your boss says you cannot afford to be remarkable. In good times the same people say relax, play it safe. Either way, no Cow.
- **You do not equal the project**: "Criticism of the project is not criticism of you." When you launch a clunker, the criticism is real, but it is about the idea. "It's people who have projects that are never criticized who ultimately fail."
- **Nobody gets unanimous praise**: "If you're remarkable, it's likely that some people won't like you. That's part of the definition of remarkable." The best the timid can hope for is to be unnoticed.
- **Follow the leader (the flock)**: geese fly in formation because followers get less wind resistance. But the flock rotates leaders. People and companies that never lead pick the wrong bird and get laid off in the ten thousand, or get trapped when technology reshuffles the deck (the record labels lobbying to keep the world the same).
  - Takeaway: "What tactics does your firm use that involve following the leader? What if you abandoned them and did something very different instead? If you acknowledge that you'll never catch up by being the same, make a list of ways you can catch up by being different."

## Key Concepts
- **School as a factory**: rows, batches called grades, no defective parts. It teaches that being noticed is bad. That pattern runs most bosses.
- **The good news of fear**: since almost everyone is petrified of the Cow, "you can be remarkable with even less effort."
- **The seventy-four restaurants**: a fourteen-block stretch of Amsterdam Avenue. Many cuisines, all dull, because after spending all that money the owner will not take one more risk. Ray's Pizza is average and never gets a bad review.
- **Unpredictability**: "You can't know if your Purple Cow is guaranteed to work... That's the point. It's the very unpredictability of the outcome that makes it work." Minimize, do not eliminate, the risk.
- **Footnote**: boring leads to failure, "except, of course, when being boring is, in and of itself, remarkable."

## Mental Models
- Think of the Cadillac CTS: called the ugliest car outside the Soviet bloc, criticized everywhere, selling fast. The official critics did not matter. The buyers loved it.
- Think of My Big Fat Greek Wedding, Bob Dylan going electric, Bloomberg booed then elected, Palm persisting single-minded and cheap against conventional wisdom. Each was criticized, each won. Palm only stumbled when it played safe (three years of feature creep).
- Contrast the Buick: boring for fifty years, hard to criticize, not successful. Drugstore.com: boring site, boring stuff, nothing to criticize, no Cow, few new customers.
- Andrew Weil took a path his Harvard Medical classmates called reckless. They are overworked and frustrated. He is having a blast. "Being safe is risky."

## Anti-patterns
- **Hiding from criticism**: it guarantees you will not succeed, since the only way to cut through is to be remarkable.
- **Staying in the back of the flock**: no practice leading means no way to lead when the flock fades.
- **A thousand imitations of your own first hit** while the market dries up.

## Worked Example
The Aeron chair. Before Herman Miller, desk chairs were invisible: spec'ed by purchasing or HR, chosen for safety. In 1994 Herman Miller launched a $750 chair that looked different, worked differently, and cost a lot. They knew it was not a safe purchase for an ordinary purchasing agent and that they might not sell many. But sitting in one sent a message about who you were. Seth Goldstein spent his first venture check on a dozen and made the front page of the Wall Street Journal. Millions sold; it is in MoMA's permanent collection. Mark Schurman of Herman Miller: "The best design solves problems, but if you can weld that to the cool factor, then you have a home run." Godin's reading: "making a safe chair was the single riskiest thing they could do."

## Key Takeaways
1. Expect criticism. It is part of the definition of remarkable.
2. Separate yourself from the project. Failed projects are how great work gets made.
3. When times are bad or good, the excuse is the same. Ignore it.
4. List every follow-the-leader tactic you use. Replace each with a way to be different.
5. Boring is the riskiest strategy. Reduce the risk of remarkable; do not avoid it.
