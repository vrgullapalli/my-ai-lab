# Public asset brief — template

The five decision lines open every brief. Nothing may precede them in
the body, and none of them may contain an ID, a file path, or a state
name.

---

## What we're proposing
One sentence.

## Why someone would care
One sentence.

## What they get
One sentence.

## Recommended form
One sentence.

## My recommendation
Approve / Revise / Hold — Strength ★–★★★★★

---

Then the detail, in this order:

### Proposed asset
What exactly is it?

### Classification
AI-native or AI-assisted. If AI-native: what AI materially does in the
user's experience. If AI-assisted: how AI materially assists while the
person gets the central value without AI participating.

### Intended person
Who specifically.

### Recognizable moment
What happens in their world that makes this useful now.

### Job
What progress it helps them make.

### Core message
The one idea that should remain a week later.

### User value
What becomes clearer, easier, safer, faster, or more defensible.

### Result
The tangible thing they leave with.

### Canonical asset
The reusable judgment or capability actually being created.

### Primary experience
The first public form, and why that form fits the job. Interactivity is
never chosen merely because it is possible.

### What stays out
What deliberately does not belong.

### Visible judgment
How someone experiences the judgment instead of reading claims about
expertise.

### Corpus relationship
Reuse / customize / extend / merge / build, in one line, from the
corpus-match record.

### Related expressions
The supporting forms that could follow (web, PDF, post, article,
outreach excerpt), as possibilities, not commitments.

### Why this deserves public existence
Never "because we need content."

### What success looks like
Observable behavior, not invented conversion metrics.

### Recommendation
Approve / Revise / Hold — Strength ★–★★★★★, strongest reasons, main
downside, what would change the view.
