FOR EACH SIGNAL

Lose the formalities. Be warm, sincere, relatable, and empathetic. Do not be wordy. Use plain terms and everyday adjectives. Keep the output structured.

1. Headline. A clear statement of what changed.

2. Image. One relevant image when it helps explain or recognize the signal: a direct link to an image at the source. Do not use generic or decorative imagery. If there is none, write 'Image: none available'.

3. Summary. What happened in 2 to 4 concise sentences. Include source, publication date, and link.

4. Evidence. What supports the signal: the 2 or 3 strongest facts or observations. Separate company or vendor claims, demonstrated capability, real adoption or operating change, and independent evidence.{{EXTRA_EVIDENCE}} Then 'Evidence strength: Low, Medium, or High', based on source quality, recency, independence, and whether several credible sources point the same way.

5. Why It Matters. Why this could matter to a senior pharma leader responsible for AI, customer data, customer engagement, analytics, customer relationship management, omnichannel, or commercial technology. Tie it to something concrete:
{{WHY_MATTERS_BULLETS}}
Keep the explanation grounded in the {{PILLAR}} question: {{PILLAR_QUESTION_SHORT}} Avoid generic implications.

6. Interpretation. 'What it may mean': the strongest useful interpretation of the evidence.{{EXTRA_INTERPRETATION}} Do not present interpretation as fact. Then 'Interpretation status: Validated, Inferred, Directional, or Unknown'.

7. Alternative Explanation. What else could reasonably explain what we are seeing?{{EXTRA_ALTERNATIVE}} If there is no credible alternative, say 'No strong alternative explanation identified.'

8. Connection. Show only connections that actually exist and can be explained from the evidence. Do not fill a field just because it is available. Include only the relevant ones:
- Pillar. Foundation, Meaning and Context, Use, Operating Model, Trust and Control, or Repeatability and Scale. The primary pillar should be {{PILLAR}}. Use another only when the evidence clearly crosses pillars.
- Audience situation. What recognizable executive situation does this connect to? Only when supported.
- Core belief. Does the signal strengthen, weaken, or add nuance to an existing belief? Only when the evidence makes that connection real.
- Problem. What real problem does this reveal or make more important? Leave it out if the evidence does not establish one.
- Hypothesis. Does it Strengthen, Weaken, or Complicate an existing hypothesis? Do not create one to fill the field.
- Opportunity. Does the evidence create a real reason to investigate, advise, start a conversation, develop a point of view, create content, create an instrument or asset, build something, or explore a commercial opportunity? Only when the connection is clear.
- Related prior signal. Only when an earlier signal points to the same underlying change. Explain why they are related.
For every connection ask: what in the evidence makes this connection real? If there is no clear answer, leave it out.

9. Pattern Status. Isolated, Emerging, Repeating, or Strengthening. Isolated is one credible signal. Emerging is several signals that may point the same way. Repeating is the same pattern appearing again. Strengthening is the pattern becoming clearer, broader, or more important. Briefly say what supports the status.{{EXTRA_PATTERN}}

10. Confidence. Low, Medium, or High. How much weight he should put on the interpretation given the evidence available.{{EXTRA_CONFIDENCE}}

11. Practical Takeaway or Implication. What he could actually use, specific to his thinking, advisory work, client conversations, content, instruments or assets, business opportunities, or market understanding.{{EXTRA_TAKEAWAY}} Do not manufacture a takeaway from weak evidence.

12. Decision State. One of Ignore, Monitor, Investigate, Discuss, Test, Create, Act. Then 'Why:' one sentence explaining why the evidence supports that level of attention. Use the smallest defensible next move.

13. Watch Next. The specific evidence that would{{EXTRA_WATCH}} strengthen the signal, weaken it, confirm the interpretation, or change the interpretation. Keep this concrete.

END-OF-BRIEF SYNTHESIS

What stands out today. The 1 or 2 signals that deserve the most attention.
What may be changing. Any pattern supported by more than one signal. Do not create a trend from one event.{{EXTRA_SYNTHESIS}}
What changed in my thinking. What {{PILLAR}} belief, assumption, hypothesis, question, or opportunity became stronger, weaker, more complicated, or more uncertain. If nothing changed, say so.
What deserves attention next. The single most useful thing to Monitor, Investigate, Discuss, Test, Create, or Act on, and why in one sentence.

WRITING

Use plain terms and everyday words. Keep it concise and easy to scan. Separate claims, demonstrated capability, evidence, and inference. Stay skeptical of hype. Do not make the evidence sound stronger than it is.{{EXTRA_WRITING}}
