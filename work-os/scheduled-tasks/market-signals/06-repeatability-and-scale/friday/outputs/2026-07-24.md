## Signal 1: The reusable unit is a method plus data, and data reuse is contested

**What happened**
- Observed: Bristol Myers Squibb says its "Predict First" method "now informs every small molecule program and most large molecule programs" ([PharmExec, July 20](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/bristol-myers-squibb-collaboration-nvidia--ai-)). A daily summary the same day said it spans "oncology, hematology, cardiovascular disease, immunology, and neuroscience" ([PharmExec Daily, July 20](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/daily-bristol-myers-squibb--partnership-nvidia)).
- Observed: deal makers say data reuse is now a main term. "Previously, biotech platform companies would hand off most of the data generated during a licensing deal." That has "changed as AI companies need that information" ([BioSpace, July 22](https://www.biospace.com/business/ai-data-becomes-sticking-point-in-biopharma-dealmaking)).
- Observed: Roche's July 23 release restated its purchase of PathAI, to combine "Roche's strong position in companion diagnostics and PathAI's advanced AI platform" ([Roche, July 23](https://www.roche.com/media/releases/med-cor-2026-07-23)). The purchase itself was in May, so this is not new this week.
- Inferred: all three are about owning the reusable layer (a method, a data right, a platform) rather than a single tool.

**Unit of analysis**
- Research and diagnostics AI at large drugmakers, and data terms in AI drug-discovery deals. Not commercial or customer data.

**Evidence**
- The Bristol Myers Squibb items are one event reported twice. The July 24 PharmTech roundup covered it a third time (from a search snippet, not verified at the source; the page refused to open).
- BioSpace is independent and quotes named people at Xaira, Bayer, and Sofinnova.
- Counterevidence: "most" large molecule programs, not all. The method does not cover everything.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** One company claim with no outcome numbers, one independent trade story, one restated old deal.

**Obvious explanation**
- Big companies buy compute and platforms and call it a factory.

### Hypothesis update
- Hypothesis affected: territory T05, Transferable Operating Intelligence. Secondary dependency: Foundation, meaning who owns the data.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** A method can spread across programs, but its edge shows at "most." And reuse now depends on contract terms, not just on technology.

### Pattern basis
- Two different kinds of evidence point the same way: internal method reuse, and a market argument over external data reuse.
- No evidence that outcomes repeat. Tool reuse is visible. Result reuse is not.
- Too early to tell. Interesting signal, no pattern yet.

## Signal 2: Live is not the same as lasting

**What happened**
- Claimed: "one 2026 study found 74% of organizations have been forced to shut down or roll back a live AI customer communications agent, a rate that holds across every region and industry and does not improve with experience or investment" ([PharmExec, July 17](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/from-dr-google-claude-important-patient-support-innovation-human-connection)). The study is not named. **I could not verify it.**
- Claimed: pharma research adoption "drops sharply in the domains that matter most for discovery: 42% for generative design, 40% for biomarker analysis, and 29% for ADME prediction." ADME means absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion. The writer, from Chemical Abstracts Service, says scientists need "traceability, reproducibility, and defensibility" ([PharmExec, July 22](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/investing-ai-just-not-where-matters-most)).

**Unit of analysis**
- Customer-facing AI agents across industries, and AI use in pharma research. Two units, linked only by inference.

**Evidence**
- Two opinion pieces in one outlet. Both numbers are secondhand, without links. The second writer sells data services.
- Nothing on Thursday or Friday added to this.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**

**Obvious explanation**
- Early AI is brittle, and rollbacks are normal.

### Hypothesis update
- Hypothesis affected: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **NOTHING CHANGED.** If real, "does not improve with experience" would cut against the belief that the second deployment is easier. But an unnamed study cannot move a belief.

### Pattern basis
- Same idea in two settings: reaching production does not mean people keep acting on it.
- Not independent measurement. Stable, too early to tell.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: The portable unit may be smaller than the tool.**
Bristol Myers Squibb did not say a model travels. It said a method "informs" programs. What moves may be a way of asking questions, with local science still doing the work. For commercial leaders, this suggests asking "which step travels?" rather than "does the platform travel?" Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: Reuse rights may decide who gets the learning loop.**
If data from each deal stays siloed, each partnership starts from zero. Pharma's instinct for "strict control" protects the asset but may block the compounding. The same trade-off likely exists between brand teams and agencies over customer data. Inferred, not shown this week.

**KEY INSIGHT: If rollback does not fall with experience, reuse of technology is not reuse of conditions.**
Each new setting would be a fresh trust test. The cost of the next deployment would not shrink the way scale plans assume. This rests on an unverified number, so hold it lightly.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **What else could explain this?** A normal summer news cycle. Earnings weeks crowd out operating stories. Commercial reuse may be happening and simply not reported.
- **Counterevidence:** Bristol Myers Squibb's claim, if accurate, is real reuse across five disease areas. That is a positive case.
- **What would make us wrong:** a pharma company showing the same commercial AI result holding across two brands or markets, with what it had to change. Or the 74% study turns out to be narrow or weak.

## Trigger

- A named, dated study behind the 74% rollback figure. That would move Signal 2 to INVESTIGATE.
- Any disclosed number showing a second deployment took less time or cost than the first, in pharma commercial.
- A signed AI deal that discloses data reuse terms.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE.** Two questions a leader can ask about any "it's ready to scale" claim: "What did the second deployment reuse unchanged, and what did it rebuild?" and "Did the result repeat, or only the tool?"
- **DEEPER ASSET.** None yet. The evidence does not support one.
- **LEARNING QUESTION.** In pharma commercial, when an AI capability moves to a second brand or market, how much is rebuilt? If most of it is rebuilt, "scale" is repeated custom work.
- **LONG-TERM VALUE.** If the "method travels, context stays" split keeps showing up, it could become a clear way to separate portable steps from local ones. That starts with collecting real second-deployment cases, not with a framework.

- **Recommended state: MONITOR.**
- **Why this state?** Every signal is LOW, and the most striking number is unverified.
- **What moves it next?** A named source for the rollback figure, or one pharma commercial case with before-and-after deployment effort.
