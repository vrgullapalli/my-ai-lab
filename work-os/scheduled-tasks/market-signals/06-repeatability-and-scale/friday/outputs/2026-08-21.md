## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: The portable unit may be the way the project is run, not the stack.**
Pistoia found model type and data source show "no significant association on their own" with success, while project maturity does. Inferred: when a pilot moves, the part people copy (the model, the data feed) is the part that does not explain success. The part that does (owner, maturity, change management) is rarely written down, so it stays behind. Caution: the paper leans toward research, not commercial.

**KEY INSIGHT: Commercial is where the scale route is least proven.**
PharmExec's list of areas with results inside a year leaves commercial out. Inferred: the commercial leader Venkat serves has the least outside proof that a first scale-up can work, so they must show what their own pilot proved.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **What else could explain this?** Budget pressure. "Fewer bets with an owner" is also what you say when money is tight.
- **Counterevidence:** Salesforce's agent index, August 7: businesses start creating agents "within an average of two days. And that number is decreasing month over month, down 53%" ([Salesforce, August 7](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/agentic-enterprise-index-insights-2026/)). Builds are getting faster. But that is building on one platform, not a result repeating in a second brand.
- **What would make us wrong:** a drugmaker's second brand going faster because of shared technology alone, with no change to owner or process.

## Trigger

- Indegene's August 24 and 25 meetings produce a per-client review pass rate, setup time, or Spain ramp time.
- A named drugmaker says it cut its pilot list and the kept ones spread faster.
- A pharma commercial number on scaled AI uses with one named owner.
- Any vendor naming second-brand setup time or cost against the first.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE.** Two questions before expanding a pilot: "Who owns this in the second brand, by name?" and "What did we change in how the work runs, not just the tool?" A blank answer means the pilot has not proved it can travel.
- **DEEPER ASSET.** Not yet. No second-deployment numbers from anyone.
- **LEARNING QUESTION.** In pharma commercial, when a first scale-up fails, does the owner, the process, or the data break first?
- **LONG-TERM VALUE.** If owner and project maturity keep showing up as what travels, a short second-brand readiness check could follow: owner named, process changes written down, what the pilot proved stated plainly. Real cases first.

- **What changed?** A little. The scale wall has two named parts, process and owner. Peer-reviewed data says technology alone does not predict success.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE.** Same as last week.

- **What moves it next?** A second-brand setup time, a ramp time, or a named drugmaker's cut-and-scale story moves it to TALK.
