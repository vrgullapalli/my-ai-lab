## Signal 1: Each new deployment costs as much as the first

**What happened**
- Observed, Sunday: futurist Amy Webb said a client ran "14 or 15 generative AI/agent pilots since the start of the year." None scaled. Executives "restart from zero each time. 'That costs a lot of money'" ([Fortune, August 30](https://fortune.com/2026/08/30/ai-economy-like-dating-app-pilot-purgatory-decks-dotcom-bubble)).
- Claimed, Wednesday: Capgemini's Nisheeth Srivastava: companies "often end up building a similar number of custom integrations for each project, meaning every successive deployment can become more expensive instead of cheaper" ([Techcircle, September 2](https://www.techcircle.in/2026/09/02/enterprise-ai-will-scale-only-when-the-second-use-case-costs-less-than-the-first-capgemini-cto)).
- Claimed, vendor: Red Hat's Albert Law: "Every isolated pilot multiplies maintenance and security costs" ([CDOTrends, September 2](https://www.cdotrends.com/story/5123/bill-nobody-modeled-what-happens-after-ai-pilot-works)).
- Claimed, paid content: PwC: "Every initiative starts from scratch." It says clients cut "roughly 30%" of core workflow cost with a shared base. No clients named ([Fortune Brand Studio, August 31](https://fortune.com/brandstudio/pwc/still-handcrafting-ai-thats-why-your-pilots-arent-paying-off/)).

**Unit of analysis:** how firms across industries build their second and later AI uses. Not pharma.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. EARLY INDICATOR.** Four sources name one mechanism in one week. Only Webb is independent. No one showed a cost per deployment.

**Obvious explanation:** sellers of platforms and services call fragmentation the problem because they sell the fix.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency." Secondary dependency: Foundation.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** Inferred: "it worked here" often fails to travel for a plain reason. Nothing from the first build carries into the second.

**Pattern basis:** new this week. Against it: PwC's 30%, unverified. Status: emerging.

## Signal 2: Scale across units stays rare, and scale adds cost

**What happened**
- Observed, survey of "more than 1,300 leaders": "Fewer than one-quarter of enterprises have successfully scaled AI across multiple business units." Low performers did not know the return on 29% of projects ([TechEdgeAI, September 2](https://techedgeai.com/gartner-survey-finds-only-22-of-organizations-have-successfully-scaled-ai-across-multiple-business-units/)). Gartner's own page would not open.
- Claimed, forecast, Thursday: Bain says "a typical $10 billion consumer packaged goods company will see its annual IT [information technology] costs rise 75% by 2035," from "Greater architectural complexity, higher cybersecurity costs and growing data governance requirements." The article adds: "one-quarter of AI spending goes to waste as pilots are powered down" ([CIO Dive, September 3](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-costs-bain-company-analysis/829488/)).

**Unit of analysis:** large firms across industries, self-reported, plus one consumer goods forecast. Not pharma.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** The third large source in three weeks, after McKinsey, showing breadth ahead of proof. Bain is a model, not a measurement.

**Obvious explanation:** profit lags deployment.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Territory: Transferable Operating Intelligence. Secondary dependency: Operating Model.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** New this week is the cost side. Scale brings security and governance costs a pilot never shows.

**Pattern basis:** repeats last week's McKinsey split. Gartner coverage ran widely, counted once. Status: repeating, strengthening.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: A template is someone else's context, frozen.**
Haleon, spun out of GSK, runs on "basically a clone from GSK. We are in a pharma template now, and we want to be a consumer goods company" ([Computer Weekly, September 1](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366649474/Interview-Haleons-tech-chief-delighted-for-AI-agents-to-feed-SAP-clean-core)). Srivastava calls legacy "an old operating model encoded in technology." Inferred: a standard carries the context of where it was built. A drugmaker rolling one template across brands or markets should ask whose assumptions it holds.

**KEY INSIGHT: Agents travel where a shared format already did the mapping.**
Veeva's new safety agent handles systems that follow E2B, the shared international format for adverse event reports: "any E2B-compliant safety system, including Oracle Argus and ArisGlobal LifeSphere MultiVigilance." Early adopters in November ([Veeva, September 1](https://www.veeva.com/resources/veeva-falcon-safety-to-work-with-all-e2b-compliant-safety-systems-across-any-intake-channels/)). Inferred: the shared format is why one agent can serve rival systems. That narrows last week's "not a data mapping" claim to places where the industry already agreed on a format. I know of no equally shared format for commercial customer data. If so, local mapping stays there.

**KEY INSIGHT: The rented fix moves the restart. It does not end it.**
Inferred: Signal 1 says each project starts from zero. Signal 3 answers with an engineer inside each customer. Unless vendors turn what those engineers learn into product, every client is still a first build, billed differently.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **What else could explain this?** Restarting can be healthy. Many pilots should die, and Gartner's high performers stop weak ones on purpose.
- **Counterevidence:** PwC's claimed 30%. Haleon plans to buy standard agents. Falcon Safety claims reach across systems. None proven.
- **What would make us wrong:** later deployments shown to cost clearly less, or engineers per client falling.

## Trigger

- A firm publishes time or cost for first versus later AI deployments.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE.** Before the next AI use: "What from the first build are we reusing, and what will the second cost compared with the first?" If "nothing" and "about the same," it is a second pilot, not scale. Then: "Whose assumptions does our template carry?"
- **DEEPER ASSET.** Not yet. Needs real first-versus-second cases first.
- **LEARNING QUESTION.** In pharma commercial, does the second brand or market deployment cost less than the first? If not, what was rebuilt: data mapping, medical and legal review, workflow, or people?
- **LONG-TERM VALUE.** The question in conversations, then a few comparisons, then a pattern of what gets rebuilt, then a step inside his bounded diagnostic.
