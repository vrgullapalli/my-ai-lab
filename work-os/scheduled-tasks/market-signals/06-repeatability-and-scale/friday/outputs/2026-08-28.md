## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: Vendors disagree on how much local work scale needs, and each answer matches what they sell.**
Eversana sells people plus a platform and said the Lundbeck deal "goes beyond a simple plug and play AI offering" ([pharmaphorum, August 25](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/lundbeck-taps-eversanas-ai-agency-commercialisation); Eversana owns the outlet). Veeva sells product and says there is no mapping. Inferred: neither view is neutral. Ask where the local work goes. If it goes into the vendor's people, the drugmaker rents the capability and cannot repeat it alone.

**KEY INSIGHT: More firms scaling, same share seeing profit, means breadth is outrunning proof.**
Inferred: the added "scaled" deployments are not, on average, adding measured outcomes. "We scaled to four functions" and "it paid in four functions" are different claims. The market makes only the first.

**KEY INSIGHT: The method travels. The number does not, yet.**
A Tufts study found an AI clinical monitoring agent could return up to "$21 million" in one late-stage cancer program. On other programs, Pamela Tenaerts: "To a degree, I feel like we can, but we don't have data." Ken Getz: companies "can use the same basic approach we've used to derive the net financial valuation" ([PharmaVoice, August 26](https://www.pharmavoice.com/news/ai-finally-proving-worth-drug-rd-tufts/828783/)). Inferred: the way of measuring travels, the result does not. That fits last week's "the portable unit may be how the work is run." Drug research, not commercial.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **What else could explain this?** Drugmakers may hold private numbers. Planning and content work look alike across functions, so extension is easy. McKinsey's flat share may reflect new, earlier-stage respondents.
- **Counterevidence:** companies rarely widen what failed. All three drugmakers went wider.
- **What would make us wrong:** a named drugmaker shows a number that held in a new function or team. Or Falcon's early adopters go live with no mapping and similar results.

## Trigger

- A named drugmaker publishes a first-phase number and the same number in a second function or brand.
- Falcon go-lives reported with setup time per customer and any mapping needed.
- Veeva or Salesforce reports how many customers run standard agents as shipped versus custom.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE.** Two questions before widening a pilot: "What number did the first phase move, and will we measure that same number in the new function?" and "Who holds what we learned: us or the vendor?" If the answers are "none" and "the vendor," the expansion is scope, not repeatability.
- **DEEPER ASSET.** Not yet. No one has shown a second-setting result to build around.
- **LEARNING QUESTION.** When a drugmaker widens a vendor-run AI capability, can it say what made the first phase work without the vendor in the room?
- **LONG-TERM VALUE.** The two questions in real conversations, then a few real cases, then a short "did it travel?" check a leader runs before approving expansion.
