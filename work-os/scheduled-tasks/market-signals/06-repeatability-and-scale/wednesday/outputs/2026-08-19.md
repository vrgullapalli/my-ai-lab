## Pattern 1: Scale advice narrows to fewer bets with a named owner

**What appears to be changing**
- The advice to pharma leaders is moving from "run many pilots" to "back a few that have an owner, clean data, and a clear return." The first scale-up is treated as something to learn from, not a copy-paste of the pilot.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, published opinion: PharmExec, August 19, by Mohd Haque. "Most pharma companies have promising AI pilots. Few have built the conditions to scale them." And: "Quantified value creation requires shifting from broad AI portfolios to a few use cases with explicit ROI [return on investment], reliable enterprise data, and a named operating owner." And: "Treat first production scale-up as a learning phase" ([PharmExec, August 19](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/from-pilot-profit-leaders-scaling-ai-across-drug-lifecycle)).
- Same piece: "Change management determines adoption: train teams on decision workflows, set expectations for trust and overrides, and tie AI outcomes to leaders' budgets and performance metrics."
- Same piece, worth noticing: the areas it says "often deliver measurable outcomes within 12 months" are manufacturing quality, clinical operations, regulatory documentation, and supply-chain planning. Commercial is not on the list.
- Background, before the window: Carta Healthcare, a clinical data company, July 21: "Even where AI has delivered measurable value, 71% of organizations are not expanding it at pace." And: "One in four organizations (26%) reported no clear owner for AI strategy at all." Health systems, not pharma. No sample size given in the release ([PR Newswire, July 21](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/92-of-healthcare-leaders-wont-trust-ai-at-scale-without-deep-clinical-expertise-302829772.html)).
- Background, vendor event page, March 20: "a pharma operations team is staring at an agentic AI pilot that worked and trying to explain to leadership why the next step costs three times as much" ([pharmaphorum event page, March 20](https://pharmaphorum.com/event/agentic-ai-ready-most-pharma-organizations-are-not)). That is marketing copy for a vendor's conference, not a measurement.

**Unit of analysis**
- Published advice to pharma leaders about their AI programs as a whole. Not what any named drugmaker did.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Secondary dependency: Operating Model, because the named condition is an owner.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** It adds a second condition to last week's "process is the wall": the result stops at the edge of the pilot when nobody owns the next step.

**Evidence strength: LOW. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- One opinion piece inside the window. The one number behind it is from July and from health systems. No drugmaker is named narrowing its portfolio.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforces Deloitte's August 12 survey, covered Friday: few scale agents, and processes are rarely ready. Different source, same shape.
- New angle: ownership. Carta's 26% with no owner fits, but it is a different industry and an earlier date.
- Not independent evidence of a shift in behavior. It is a shift in what people are told to do.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: an author restating common advice. "Focus, own it, prove it" is not new.
- What else could explain it: the advice may reflect budget pressure, not scale lessons. Fewer bets are cheaper.
- What would make us wrong: a drugmaker saying it cut its pilot list and the kept ones spread faster. Or one saying it kept many pilots and scaled them anyway.
- Outside this lens: a missing owner is an Operating Model problem. It shows up here because it is where the move to a second setting stops.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A named drugmaker saying it cut the number of AI pilots, and why.
- Any number on how many scaled AI uses have a single named owner, in pharma rather than health systems.
- A commercial example of a first scale-up treated as a test, with what changed before the second brand.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** An opinion piece and older background. Friday can weigh it against the full week.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** Repeated, with one new condition, ownership, next to process readiness.

## Pattern 2: A service vendor reports volume through one review engine, not repeat results

**What appears to be changing**
- A service vendor to pharma describes one AI engine used across many clients for medical and legal review of promotional content. That is the step last Friday asked about: is review the same across brands, or different every time?

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, company statements reported by ScanX, published August 17, covering Indegene's annual shareholder meeting on August 13, one day before the window. The article says Indegene has built its own technology layer called Cortex, and that its AI tools include "Human-less MLR," meaning medical, legal, and regulatory review done without a person. "These tools have already processed over 65,000 medico-legal review assets in 2025." Also: "Clients generating more than USD1 million in revenue increased from 41 to 53." It also names two European acquisitions, Warn & Company and Cake Kommunikations, bought for their "expertise in key European markets" ([ScanX, August 17](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/companies/indegene-shareholders-pass-all-resolutions-28th-agm-appoint-new/48495209)).
- The page would not show me full sentences for management's lines about redesigning how it operates. I am not quoting those.
- The 65,000 figure is for 2025. It is not new this week, but it was newly reported inside the window.

**Unit of analysis**
- One service vendor's review tools, used across its clients. Not reuse inside a drugmaker.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Last Friday's learning question: is the process that blocks scale, such as medical and legal review, the same across brands? Also the territory Transferable Operating Intelligence. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control, because review is the check a drugmaker relies on.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Inferred: if one engine handles review across many clients, part of review may travel. But volume says nothing about whether each client accepted the output, or how much each needed custom rules. And buying local agencies for Europe suggests the market-specific part still comes from people, not the engine.

**Evidence strength: LOW. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Company statements to shareholders, through a secondary news site. No per-client approval rate, no setup time, no error rate.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforces the vendor thread from the last three weeks: OptimizeRx, Definitive Healthcare, Doximity, WPP, and Indegene itself. Same gap: reach and volume, no second-setting result.
- The Spain ramp time from Indegene's July 31 call ("three to four quarters") got no update here.
- Duplicates, not new evidence: Motley Fool reposted Definitive Healthcare's August 10 call on August 17 ([Motley Fool, August 17](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/17/definitive-healthcare-dh-q2-2026-earnings-call/)) and OptimizeRx's August 12 call on August 19 ([Motley Fool, August 19](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/19/optimizerx-oprx-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Both were counted last week.
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet on the review question itself.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: a vendor telling shareholders about volume, because shareholders reward it.
- What else could explain it: 65,000 assets may be many small, similar pieces for a few large clients. That is scale in one setting, not travel across settings.
- What would make us wrong: Indegene showing the same review pass rate across clients, or setup for a new client falling each time.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- Any per-client or per-market number on Indegene's review tools: pass rate, setup time, or rework.
- Any word on Spain's ramp time against "three to four quarters."
- Any drugmaker saying it uses a vendor's automated review across more than one brand, and what it had to add locally.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Nothing is time-bound. The shareholder meeting is past and the numbers are from 2025.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** Repeated. One vendor, no new proof. It does touch last Friday's review question for the first time.
