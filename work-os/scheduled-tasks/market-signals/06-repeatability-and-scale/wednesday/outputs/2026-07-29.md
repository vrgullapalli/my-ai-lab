## Pattern 1: The reusable layer is sitting with vendors, across clients

**What appears to be changing**
- The parts that get reused (agents, data, planning tools) are being built once by a data or service vendor and reused across many pharma clients. The drugmaker rents the reuse instead of building it.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, company claim: IQVIA's chief executive, Ari Bousbib, on the July 28 earnings call: "we now have 294 agents deployed across 90 use cases." And: "an agent is not one model. An agent is built of multiple tasks that are all powered by different models" ([Investing.com call transcript, July 28](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-iqvia-tops-q2-2026-estimates-and-raises-outlook-93CH-4817225)).
- Company claim: "Four of the top 10 pharma companies have already contracted with us to co-develop AI solutions, and 19 of the top 20 pharma companies have already deployed IQVIA AI solutions in their workflow." Also: "clients are moving beyond pilots and data foundation work. They're starting to deploy IQVIA AI agents more broadly" (same transcript).
- Company claim, and the closest thing to a cross-market case all week: "A mid-size pharma client is expanding its use of IQVIA AI across an immunology franchise in 95 countries" (same transcript). The client is not named. No result was given.
- Observed: IQVIA's release credits "increased adoption of our AI solutions" for part of its commercial growth, with no AI numbers ([IQVIA release, July 28](https://ir.iqvia.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/IQVIA-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)).
- Observed: ICON, a contract research organization, signed a multi-year deal with Anthropic. It names four "production capabilities," including "integrating ICON's clinical expertise into client systems." It aims at "automating high-volume, repeatable activity" ([ICON release, July 28](https://www.iconplc.com/news-events/press-releases/icon-announces-multi-year-collaboration-anthropic-bring-ai-clinical)). This is clinical, not commercial.

**Unit of analysis**
- Two vendors that serve many drugmakers: a commercial data and services firm (IQVIA) and a clinical research firm (ICON). This is not a claim about how drugmakers reuse AI inside their own walls.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Need hypothesis: "Agencies may need a credible client-facing capability without hiring a complete executive function or buying another disconnected tool." Also territory T06, Human-Governed Agentic Operating Systems. Secondary dependency: Foundation (the vendor owns the data the agents run on).
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Reuse is real on paper, but it sits in the vendor's shared core. The client's own context (definitions, local rules, brand choices) still has to be added each time. We cannot yet see how much.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- Every number comes from the vendor's own mouth, on an investor call. "Deployed" is not defined. No outcome, cost, or time-to-deploy figure. The 95-country case is one unnamed client.

**Pattern basis**
- Two vendors, two different fields (commercial and clinical), same move: one shared core, many clients.
- Possible hidden repeat: "294 agents across 90 use cases" could be many custom builds under one brand, not one build reused. The transcript does not say.
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: earnings-call selling. Vendors count agents the way they count products.
- Strongest alternative: the reusable part is the vendor's own data. Agents travel because the data under them is the same everywhere. That is reuse of a data asset, not of the client's result.
- What would make us wrong: a named client saying the second market or brand took as long as the first, or that results differed widely across the 95 countries.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- Any detail on the immunology franchise: what stayed the same across 95 countries, and what was set up locally.
- A client, not a vendor, describing a second deployment of a vendor agent and what it had to change.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** These are claims, and nothing gets worse by waiting two days.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** New. It partly answers last week's gap. It is the first commercial-side claim of one capability crossing many markets. But it comes from a vendor, not a drugmaker.

## Pattern 2: Pockets of return, not spread, and the rollback number reads differently now

**What appears to be changing**
- The gap is being named in plain numbers: returns show up in single projects and do not carry across the company. Data readiness is the stated blocker.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, survey: Dun & Bradstreet's survey of 10,000 businesses in 32 countries. "The largest share, 48%, report 'pockets of ROI,' with some pilots showing returns." ROI means return on investment. "Only 6% of organizations report their enterprise data is fully ready to support AI at scale." And: "The challenge now is that AI adoption has outpaced data readiness." Next step, in their words: "improve data readiness to turn early and uneven ROI into more consistent returns at enterprise scale" ([Dun & Bradstreet, July 28](https://www.dnb.com/en-us/newsroom/press-releases/dnb-survey-finds-enterprise-ai-returns-continue-to-advance.html)). They also report that 28% see broad or strong return across several projects.
- Memory update, source dated before this window: last week's unnamed "74% rollback" study is Sinch's "AI Production Paradox" research, published May 13. It says "74% of enterprises have already rolled back or shut down an AI customer communications agent after deployment due to a governance failure." The key line for us: "Rollback rates reach 81% among organizations with the most mature governance frameworks, higher than the overall average." Sinch's own reading: "The most advanced organizations aren't failing less; they're seeing failures sooner." Survey of 2,527 senior decision makers in 10 countries and six industries, healthcare among them ([Sinch, May 13](https://sinch.com/news/sinch-releases-ai-production-paradox/)).

**Unit of analysis**
- Enterprises across industries. Not pharma. Healthcare is one of six industries in the Sinch survey. No pharma breakout in either survey.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy." Secondary dependency: Foundation (data readiness) and Trust and Control (catching failures).
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS** the core belief of this lens, "it worked here" is not "we know how to make it work elsewhere," at the cross-industry level.
- It also **changes last week's reading.** Last week we held the idea that experience does not lower rollback. Sinch's own data points another way: the most mature groups roll back more because they see problems sooner. That is not "experience does not help." It may be "experience helps you notice." Inferred from the vendor's own interpretation.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Large samples, many countries, and now a named source.
- But both are self-reported surveys. Both are published by firms that sell the fix (data for Dun & Bradstreet, communications for Sinch).

**Pattern basis**
- Two surveys, two sellers, two different questions, same shape: local success, no spread.
- Not independent measurement of outcomes. No pharma-specific evidence.
- This is the one thread that clearly **strengthened** this week.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: every data vendor's survey finds that data is the problem.
- What else: "pockets" may reflect normal early rollout order, not a limit on repeat.
- What would make us wrong: a survey that tracks the same companies over time and shows pockets turning into broad returns without data work.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- Any pharma or healthcare breakout in either survey.
- Any drugmaker naming a project whose return held when moved to a second brand or market.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Friday is the right place to rewrite the rollback reading.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** The "live is not lasting" thread repeated and strengthened. The source is now named. Its meaning shifted.
