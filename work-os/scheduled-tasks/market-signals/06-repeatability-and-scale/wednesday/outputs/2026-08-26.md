## Pattern 1: Expansion carries the vendor along, and no result travels with it

**What appears to be changing**
- Inferred: when a pharma AI capability moves to new functions or a full field team, it moves inside the vendor's platform or with the vendor's people. The drugmaker does not show what made the first round work.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, Lundbeck and Eversana, August 25. The first phase "introduced AI-driven workflows to accelerate strategic planning, content development and commercial execution while maintaining rigorous medical, legal and regulatory review processes." Now "Lundbeck will scale AI-enabled commercialization capabilities across multiple areas, including marketing, medical communications, market access and media operations." The release promises "a scalable framework." It gives no metrics ([PR Newswire, August 25](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lundbeck-and-eversana-announce-strategic-partnership-to-advance-ai-powered-commercialization-302858538.html)).
- Observed, same event: "A year after unveiling its AI Agency platform, Eversana has reeled in its first publicly named pharma partner." Quoted in the piece: "we're implementing an AI-powered operating model designed specifically for life sciences" ([Fierce Pharma, August 25](https://www.fiercepharma.com/marketing/lundbeck-strikes-expanded-pact-eversana-broaden-ai-scope-across-us-commercial-org)).
- Observed, vendor statement, August 26: "a top 20 biopharma deployed Vault CRM [customer relationship management] and the Agentic Call Report across its full U.S. field team." Drugmaker not named. No usage or outcome number ([Veeva, August 26](https://www.veeva.com/resources/veeva-announces-fiscal-2027-second-quarter-results/)).
- Claimed by a vendor, August 26: "Agentforce Life Sciences has more than 140 customers, including all five of the largest pharmaceutical companies." The outcome Salesforce cited was its own revenue: customers on an "agentic journey" reach "about twice the company's annual contract value" ([Yahoo Finance, August 26](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/salesforce-q2-earnings-call-highlights-230418017.html)). That is breadth, not a repeated result.

**Unit of analysis**
- Two drugmakers' United States commercial organizations, one named, plus two vendors' customer counts.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Also the territory Transferable Operating Intelligence. Secondary dependency: Operating Model.
- What travels here, inferred: the platform and the outside operator. What the drugmaker learned is not written down in public.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.**

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Weeks of the same gap from sellers. New: two buyer-side expansions. Both are rollout announcements, which count for less.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforcing: Lundbeck and the unnamed top 20 drugmaker are separate events. Salesforce adds breadth only.
- Duplicates: the Lundbeck news ran in several outlets. Counted once.
- Against it: both buyers may hold private results good enough to widen. And Lundbeck kept human review on purpose, a condition carried forward, not dropped.
- Unknown: whether the new functions reuse the first phase's work, or the vendor rebuilds it each time.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: drugmakers rarely publish commercial results.
- What else could explain it: planning and content work may look alike across functions, so extension is easy. Inferred.
- What would make us wrong: Lundbeck or the top 20 drugmaker names a before-and-after number that held in the new function or team.
- Outside this lens: if the vendor runs the work, the scale is rented labor. The drugmaker may not own a capability it can repeat alone.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- Eversana's New York event on August 27. Its August 20 release says it will feature "leaders from both emerging and global pharmaceutical companies already leveraging the platform" ([PR Newswire, August 20](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/eversana-expands-ai-powered-commercialization-platform-with-new-capabilities-across-medical-affairs-market-research-and-commercial-operations-302855424.html)). Watch for a second named drugmaker, or any number from Lundbeck's first phase.
- Veeva's call transcript: how many field reps actually use the call report, and whether markets outside the United States come next.

**State: MONITOR CLOSELY.** A dated event on Thursday could name buyers and results, and that would move this pattern.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** Repeated and strengthened. Last week sellers widened reach. This week buyers did.

## Pattern 2: Vendors split the standard part from the local part

**What appears to be changing**
- Inferred: vendors now sell standard agents plus tools to build custom ones. That is this pillar's split: what stays standard, what stays specific.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, vendor statement, August 26: "August marked a major milestone for Vault AI with new standard agents, broader capabilities for existing agents, and advanced tools for custom agent development" ([Veeva, August 26](https://www.veeva.com/resources/veeva-announces-fiscal-2027-second-quarter-results/)).
- Claimed by Veeva's chief executive on the call, as reported: deployments of Falcon, its agent product, "should not require the extensive data mapping, system cutovers or extract-transform-load work often associated with enterprise software implementations." He also said Veeva "is currently the limiting factor because it needs to further prepare the product" ([Yahoo Finance, August 26](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/veeva-systems-q2-earnings-call-230419426.html)). The release says five early adopters, first go-lives "this year." No live customer yet.
- Trade advice, August 25, page text Venkat supplied: "the fastest path to agent-readiness is exposing that same layer over MCP [Model Context Protocol], rather than standing up a parallel, agent-specific layer that will drift from the first." The same piece warns the shared layer "is deliberately narrow": it knows what a metric adds up to, not how related ideas connect ([CTO Magazine, August 25](https://ctomagazine.com/semantic-layer-ai-agents-iceberg-mcp/)).
- Background, August 20: Eversana's platform was "Originally launched to transform marketing agency operations" and now reaches medical affairs, market research, and field sales ([PR Newswire, August 20](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/eversana-expands-ai-powered-commercialization-platform-with-new-capabilities-across-medical-affairs-market-research-and-commercial-operations-302855424.html)).

**Unit of analysis**
- How two vendors describe their products, plus one trade article. No buyer has reported what the split cost or saved.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Need hypothesis: "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency." Secondary dependency: Meaning and Context.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Custom tools admit that a standard agent does not fit everyone, which supports "local context matters." But the Falcon claim cuts the other way: it says the agent travels without the local data work Venkat's view says is needed.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- Product language and advice. No second deployment measured.

**Pattern basis**
- Two vendors and one trade piece describe the same split.
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet on whether the standard part repeats results.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: "standard plus custom" is the normal software pitch.
- What else could explain it: custom tools may mean the standard agents keep needing rework. That is repeated customization, not reuse.
- What would make us wrong: Falcon early adopters go live with no data mapping and get similar results across different drugmakers.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- Any Veeva number on how many customers run standard agents as shipped versus building custom ones.
- Any customer saying how much it had to configure per market, such as local compliance rules in the call report.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Product claims, nothing time-bound.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** New. It touches last Friday's point that the portable unit may be how the work runs, not the stack.

## Pattern 3: The scale ceiling sits where work crosses team lines

**What appears to be changing**
- Inferred: AI spreads wide inside single teams but rarely links across them.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, vendor-sponsored survey, August 25. Talkdesk paid NewtonX to survey 252 director-level leaders across regions in April 2026. "98% of organizations have deployed AI in their customer journey, only 15% combine agentic AI with cross-departmental orchestration." Also: "nearly 80% of companies limited to 10 or fewer AI automations," "only 35% retain customer context from one system to the next," and "only 5% can quantify AI's impact on business outcomes" ([Talkdesk, August 25](https://www.talkdesk.com/news-and-press/press-releases/state-of-agentic-automation-cx-2026/)).
- Background, August 12: Deloitte found "just 15% have scaled orchestrated, cross-functional multi-agent adoption" and "Only 5% of organizations say their business processes are highly prepared for AI agents" ([PR Newswire, August 12](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-agents-are-only-the-beginning-deloitte-survey-examines-the-ai-readiness-gap-and-reveals-how-enterprises-can-prepare-for-agentic-success-302848848.html)).

**Unit of analysis**
- Customer service organizations across industries. Not pharma.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "Organizations keep adding technology to a coordination and meaning problem." Secondary dependency: Operating Model.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** Two surveys, same 15% figure for work that crosses teams.

**Evidence strength: LOW. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- One survey inside the window, from a seller of the fix it describes.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforces last week's "scale stalls at owner and process." Cross-team links need someone to own the handoff.
- The matching 15% may be coincidence. The surveys asked different questions.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: a vendor survey that finds the problem its product solves.
- What would make us wrong: firms with many automations show no gain from linking teams.
- Outside this lens: losing customer context between systems is a Foundation and Meaning problem. It shows up here because it is where scale stops.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A pharma number on AI uses that span two teams, such as field and marketing, or brand and medical.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Cross-industry, a known shape.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** Repeated. Stable, not accelerating.
