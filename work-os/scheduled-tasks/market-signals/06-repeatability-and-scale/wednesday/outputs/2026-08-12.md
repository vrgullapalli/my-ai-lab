## Pattern 1: Vendors report spread across brands, but not repeat results

**What appears to be changing**
- Vendors selling to drugmakers now describe growth as spread across brands and use cases. They do not say whether the result held in each new brand. One vendor is testing its tool across different kinds of customers first. That is the opposite order from last week.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, company claim: OptimizeRx, a company that places drug messages in doctors' software, August 12. Chief executive Stephen Silvestro: "Our existing customers continue expanding their use of our platform across additional brands, therapeutic areas, and commercial use cases." Finance chief Edward Stelmakh: its "AI-enabled software offerings" grew "25% year-over-year" ([Investing.com call transcript, August 12](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-optimizerx-beats-q2-2026-profit-view-as-shares-slip-93CH-4856270)).
- Observed, same call, against it: Stelmakh said the revenue drop "was largely contained to a limited number of large customers that utilized the lower margin managed services in 2025," and "one of our large customers in 2025 that has not generated revenue this quarter." Silvestro: "budget timing variability and cautious spending behavior." I found no number for how many brands per customer, or any result per brand.
- Observed, company claim: Definitive Healthcare, a health data vendor, call published August 10. Chief executive Kevin Coop on its new AI tool, Turbo: "Our launch begins with an initial stage pilot with a select group of strategic customers this month." The pilot is chosen to represent "a broad customer base that spans both healthcare systems, life sciences organizations, and customers in our diversified segment." And: "Feedback from our pilot customers will help shape the final experience" ([Investing.com call transcript, August 10](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-definitive-healthcare-tops-q2-2026-eps-forecast-93CH-4850490)).
- Same call: "The response in our life sciences segment has been slower." And: "One longstanding population intelligence customer moved from an initial test into a total activation commitment of more than $300,000."

**Unit of analysis**
- Two vendors that sell data and software to many drugmakers. Not reuse inside one drugmaker.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Need hypothesis: "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency." Also the territory Transferable Operating Intelligence. Secondary dependency: Use, meaning which brand decision got better.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Spread across brands keeps showing up. Proof that the result repeats does not. But Definitive shows at least one vendor testing across different settings before launch, not after.

**Evidence strength: LOW. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Vendor claims to investors. OptimizeRx gives no per-brand numbers. Definitive's pilot has not started producing results.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforces last week's vendor thread: Indegene, Doximity, and WPP expanded with no second-setting numbers. These are two new, independent vendors.
- Contradicts it in one way: Definitive tests first, across health systems, drugmakers, and other buyers. That is portability tested on purpose, not assumed.
- "Across additional brands" could be repeated custom setup for each brand. Nothing here rules that out.
- Not a new pattern. Same thread, more cases, same gap.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: vendors describe wider use because investors reward it.
- What else could explain it: OptimizeRx lost revenue from big customers. Talking about spread across brands may be offsetting that story, not reporting reuse.
- What would make us wrong: OptimizeRx showing the same result across brands at one customer. Or Definitive's pilot working the same for health systems and drugmakers.
- Outside this lens: Definitive says drugmakers respond "slower." That may be a buying problem, not a reuse problem.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A vendor naming time to set up a second brand compared with the first.
- Whether Definitive's pilot group shows different results for drugmakers versus health systems.
- Any follow-up from Indegene on Spain's ramp time against the "three to four quarters" its finance chief gave on July 31.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Nothing here is time-bound, and Friday can weigh it against the full week.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** Repeated. Two new vendors, no new proof. One counter-case: test first, then launch.

## Pattern 2: Drugmakers trust their own AI more than their vendors'

**What appears to be changing**
- Drugmakers judge AI built inside their walls far better than AI their vendors bring. If that holds, a vendor's shared core may not carry the trust a buyer needs.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, survey: Life Science Strategy Group, a research firm, August 12: "71% of sponsors rate their own AI/LLM output highly. Only 27% say the same of their CROs and vendors." Sponsors here means drugmakers. LLM means large language model. CRO means contract research organization ([PR.com release, August 12](https://www.pr.com/press-release/976221)).
- Same release: "Advanced applications such as agentic AI, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and large-scale systems integration are the ceiling on sponsor use today."
- Sample: "110 biopharma decision-makers across North America and Europe, company size, and from discovery through commercial."

**Unit of analysis**
- How drugmakers rate AI output, their own versus their vendors'. Mostly research and clinical vendors are named. Commercial is in the sample, but I found no commercial breakout.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Need hypothesis: "Agencies may need a credible client-facing capability without hiring a complete executive function or buying another disconnected tool." Also last week's belief that reuse forms at the vendor. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Vendors build one core and sell it widely. Buyers may not trust it the way they trust what they built. What travels from vendor to client may be the tool, not the trust.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- One paid research report, seen only through its press release. Small sample. No commercial split.

**Pattern basis**
- New. Nothing earlier in this series measured buyer trust in vendor AI.
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: people rate their own work higher than anyone else's.
- What else could explain it: a research firm that sells to vendors has a reason to show vendors falling behind.
- What would make us wrong: a commercial breakout where drugmakers rate agency or data vendor AI as highly as their own.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- Any commercial-side number from the same report.
- A drugmaker choosing to build in-house instead of using a vendor's shared AI, and saying why.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** One survey, released today. It needs more before it means anything.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** New. It pushes against last week's "reuse forms at the vendor."
