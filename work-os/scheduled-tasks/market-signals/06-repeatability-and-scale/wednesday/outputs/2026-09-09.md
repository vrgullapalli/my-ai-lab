## Pattern 1: Hidden hand work behind the pilot shows when scope widens

**What appears to be changing**
- Inferred: sources now name why "it worked here" fails to travel. The pilot rested on hand work: a cleaned data pull, a narrow set of cases, a person watching. When users, product lines, or functions are added, that work does not come along.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, pharma buyer, Friday: Lundbeck's chief commercial officer, Shae Maughmer, on its widening rollout with Eversana: "If you're not careful in correctly choosing where you're going to deploy the tool and how you deploy it, it can actually create a lot of inefficiency and more workload." And: "we've actually had a situation which, as the tool learned, it learned the wrong things" ([pharmaphorum, September 4](https://pharmaphorum.com/sales-marketing/one-year-later-eversana-plans-go-beyond-ai-agency)). Eversana owns pharmaphorum, which says it is editorially independent.
- Claimed, trade press, Sunday: "Somebody hand-assembled the extract that made the demo work, and nobody can assemble it again next Tuesday at 9 a.m., for 40,000 customers, with lineage a regulator would accept" ([CDOTrends, September 6](https://www.cdotrends.com/story/5125/your-ai-pilot-didnt-fail-it-starved)). The experts quoted work for Denodo, a data software vendor.
- Claimed, vendor case, Wednesday: at Zepto, an Indian delivery firm, "Expansion from groceries into apparel, electronics, and beauty introduced new refund, exchange, and return journeys," and "new failure modes surfaced every few weeks." The fix was to make testing the core of how agents get built ([Databricks, September 9](https://www.databricks.com/blog/evaluation-first-ai-agents-how-zepto-scales-customer-support-databricks-and-mlflow)).
- Claimed, trade analysis, Wednesday: "EY's strongest finance, tax and assurance outcomes required custom automation and connected systems rather than standard Copilot licenses" ([UC Today, September 9](https://uctoday.com/ey-microsoft-copilot-deployment-the-path-to-ai-productivity)).
- Claimed, pharma service firm, Wednesday: "The wide value gap between isolated success and widespread adoption is the defining feature of the current landscape, and it is rarely explained by model quality" ([IQVIA, September 9](https://www.iqvia.com/blogs/2026/09/ai-readiness-and-operating-models)).

**Unit of analysis**
- Enterprise AI programs as they widen, across industries, plus one pharma buyer. Not a pharma trend.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Need hypothesis: "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency." Territory: System Reconstruction. Secondary dependency: Foundation.
- What must stay true, inferred: the conditions the pilot quietly had. Most were never written down, so no one carries them.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.**

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Four kinds of source point one way in a half-week. But three are vendors or vendor-linked, and none gives a before-and-after number.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforces last Friday's "each new deployment costs as much as the first," and gives the reason. New: a pharma buyer's voice, though with no number and no detail.
- Against, background, missed in earlier checks: as Uber's agent use grew 9.4 times, "cost per session is down 52% from its June peak," model held constant. Uber adds that its savings "are unique to our environment" ([Uber, August 27](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/efficient-software-factory/)). That is cost per use, not per new deployment. Still, use got cheaper once one team owned the shared layer.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: pilots are small on purpose. Scale always finds new problems.
- What else could explain it: vendors selling data, testing, or platform tools describe the gap their product fills.
- What would make us wrong: a drugmaker widening a capability with no new failure modes and no added review work.
- Outside this lens: much of this is Foundation, showing up only on the move.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- Lundbeck or Eversana saying what "learned the wrong things" meant, and what they changed.
- Any firm reporting cost or time per new deployment, not per session.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** The cause is clearer, but nothing dated lands before Friday.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** repeated and sharpened.

## Pattern 2: The rented delivery layer grows, and the proof comes from inside the family

**What appears to be changing**
- Inferred: large vendors are building standing units of engineers inside customers. The promise is repeatable solutions. The evidence is one in-house case.

**Evidence so far**
- Observed, Tuesday: Accenture and Google Cloud formed a group that will "establish a 1,000-person FDE [forward deployed engineer] workforce." One goal: "Building repeatable, industry-specific solutions that reduce time-to-value." The only named result is YouTube, "boosting customer sentiment by 11% and slashing average handle time by 37%" ([Accenture, September 8](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2026/accenture-and-google-cloud-deepen-partnership-with-formation-of-new-accenture-gemini-enterprise-business-group)).
- Observed, same day: the engineers will "build custom AI applications on the Gemini Enterprise platform." OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Amazon "have all recently launched separate business units" for this work ([TechCrunch, September 8](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/google-cloud-races-to-catch-up-in-the-ai-deployment-wars-with-accenture-deal)).
- Independent read, same day: "YouTube is also owned by Alphabet, which owns Google Cloud," so "the first external case study is still outstanding." And: "The models are abundant and increasingly interchangeable. The people who can install them inside a bank are not" ([The Next Web, September 8](https://thenextweb.com/news/accenture-google-cloud-gemini-enterprise-forward-deployed-engineers)).

**Unit of analysis**
- How vendors and consulting firms deliver enterprise AI. Cross-industry. No pharma client named.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Need hypothesis: "Agencies may need a credible client-facing capability without hiring a complete executive function or buying another disconnected tool." Territory: Transferable Operating Intelligence. Secondary dependency: Operating Model.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS** the reading that deployment know-how sits in vendor people.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM for the move, LOW for repeat results. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Hiring shows demand. The one result comes from a sister company.

**Pattern basis**
- Third week running: the search firm's count, Lightcast's jobs jump, Deloitte, PwC with Palantir, now Accenture with Google.
- A tension inside one launch: "custom" applications per customer, yet "repeatable" solutions per industry. Which wins is the repeatability question.
- In pharma: still one hiring plan, Axtria's. Interesting signal. No pattern yet.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: new platforms need hands-on setup until they mature.
- What else could explain it: if models learn to deploy themselves, these firms are "selling scaffolding for a building that no longer needs it" (The Next Web).
- What would make us wrong: engineer time per client clearly falling from the first client to later ones.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A first outside customer result for the Accenture group, or any such unit naming a life sciences client and what it reused.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** A big launch, but no result to judge.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** repeated and strengthened. Bigger players, same gap.

## Pattern 3: Pharma content vendors try to turn local rules into shared data

**What appears to be changing**
- Inferred: in promotional content, vendors are splitting a shared core from a local layer. The core: claims, evidence, one operating model. The local layer: rules by market and channel, and how strict the checks are.

**Evidence so far**
- Claimed, Tuesday: Salesforce launched Regulated Content Management. It treats "market- and channel-specific obligations (such as Fair Balance or Important Safety Information)" as "living, interconnected data." It admits: "Until today, agents were simply layered on top of fragmented processes and data, resulting in compliance gaps and re-work" ([Salesforce, September 8](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/proactive-intelligence-life-sciences/)). The product page says obligations are managed "by content type, market, and channel, with manual extraction and annotation on reference documents." Early adopters get it in October 2026 ([Salesforce product page](https://www.salesforce.com/life-sciences/regulated-content-management/)).
- Claimed, Wednesday: IQVIA calls for "the same operating model, applied with a calibrated bar so that a commercial use case gets promotional-compliance and drift monitoring rather than a clinical validation package" ([IQVIA, September 9](https://www.iqvia.com/blogs/2026/09/ai-readiness-and-operating-models)).
- Background, September 2: Veeva's review agent runs "compliance checks against approved labels and local regulations to minimize manual effort" ([Veeva MedTech, September 2](https://www.veeva.com/medtech/resources/modernize-mlr-with-ai/)).

**Unit of analysis**
- Pharma content and medical, legal, and regulatory review software. Three vendors. Not customer data. No customer results.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "Local definitions, tagging, mappings, scripts, and ownership choices make the same data support different answers." Territory: Continuity of Meaning. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **TOO EARLY TO TELL.** If it works, the local part travels as data instead of being rebuilt for each market. But Salesforce's own page says the local rules still need "manual extraction."

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** Three vendors, one direction, no go-lives.

**Pattern basis**
- Builds on last Friday's inference: Veeva's safety agent can serve rival systems because a shared industry format for adverse event reports already did the mapping. Here vendors try to build such a format for content.
- Against: each may build its own format, shared only inside one vendor.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: launch timing. Salesforce ties these products to Dreamforce, its yearly conference.
- What would make us wrong: early adopters report that each new market still needs its rules rebuilt by hand.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A named early adopter for Salesforce's product or Veeva's Falcon review agent, with markets in scope.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Early adopters start in October. Nothing to test yet.

**Pattern status vs. last week:** new. It carries last Friday's shared-format point into promotional content.
