## Signal 1: Pharma commercial AI runs through vendors

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: IQVIA and Indegene each described pharma clients using their AI. Observed: four drug makers this week, like two last week, gave no commercial AI number. Interpretation: commercial AI arrives through vendors, and pharma does not report it.
- **Unit of analysis.** How pharma commercial AI gets disclosed. Not outcomes.
- **Evidence.**
  - IQVIA: "19 of the top 20 pharma companies have already deployed IQVIA AI solutions in their workflow," and commercial clients "are moving beyond pilots and data foundation work" ([Investing.com transcript, July 28](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-iqvia-tops-q2-2026-estimates-and-raises-outlook-93CH-4817225)). Vendor claim.
  - Indegene, which runs commercial and medical work for drug makers: after "the proof of concept we completed with a multinational pharmaceutical company," it is "in advanced discussions to close the next phase." It signed a "$10 million-plus outcome-based omnichannel marketing deal" ([Investing.com transcript, July 31](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-indegene-posts-strong-q1-2026-growth-stock-rises-93CH-4826762)). Vendor claim, independent of IQVIA.
  - Against it, from Indegene itself: "enterprise adoptions remain measured and the pace of change on the ground is slower."
  - Bristol Myers Squibb is "expanding the use of AI to help our teams move faster." Its named AI partnerships are for scientists ([Investing.com transcript, July 30](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-bristol-myers-beats-q2-2026-estimates-as-growth-portfolio-shines-93CH-4824833)). No commercial detail.
  - No AI talk on Sanofi's call ([Investing.com transcript, July 30](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-sanofi-beats-q2-2026-estimates-but-stock-falls-premarket-93CH-4824332)). None outside risk factors for AbbVie ([AbbVie, July 31](https://news.abbvie.com/2026-07-31-AbbVie-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results)) or GSK ([GSK, July 28](https://www.gsk.com/en-gb/media/press-releases/gsk-delivers-strong-q2-core-results-performance-and-continued-momentum/)).
- **Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Two independent vendors plus steady pharma silence. Claims, not outcomes.
- **Obvious explanation.** Vendors sell AI to investors. Drug makers sell pipelines.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Hypothesis affected.** The accountability condition: the leader owns the outcome, not the technology, and depends on vendors. Primary pillar: Operating Model. Also Use.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** Last week this was an inference from silence. This week two vendors describe it from the other side.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Repeating, and strengthening. Vendor claims and pharma silence come from different sources and point the same way. Doceree, last week, is a third vendor building for this spot.
- Tension: IQVIA sounds broad. Indegene says slow. Both can be true if many companies run small uses.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**
- **KEY INSIGHT: "Deployed at 19 of 20" and "adoption is measured" count different things.** One tool in one workflow counts as deployed. Change on the ground is slower. The accountable leader hears the first number from vendors and lives with the second. That gap is where "what does this prove?" sits.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else could explain this?** Drug makers may talk about commercial AI at investor days, or on calls we did not read.
- **Counterevidence.** Indegene's "measured" line. Bristol Myers Squibb says AI spans its "enterprise operations," which may include commercial work.
- **What would make us wrong?** A drug maker reports its own commercial AI result, with a number, built in-house.

**TRIGGER**
- A drug maker names a commercial AI outcome with a figure.
- A commercial leader, in conversation, cannot say which decisions vendor AI now makes.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value.** Ask: "Your vendors say you have deployed AI. Which decisions does it make, and who checks them?"
- **Deeper asset.** Not yet. If talks confirm the blind spot, a map of where vendor AI decides is the likely shape.
- **Learning question.** Do commercial leaders know which of their decisions vendor AI now shapes?
- **Long-term value.** If often not, that map becomes the way into governance work and the diagnostic. Talks first, then the map, then a method.

**DECISION**
- **What changed?** The pharma commercial gap is now described by vendors, not just implied by silence.
- **Recommended state: TALK.**
- **Why this state?** Last week's trigger was a pharma number or an agency reporting AI work with pharma clients. Indegene met half of it. The question can now be put to a real buyer.
- **What moves it next?** A buyer confirming the blind spot moves it to TEST, with a simple vendor-AI map.

## Signal 2: Sellers are starting to carry the risk of results

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: Amazon committed money to put its engineers inside customer companies. Observed: Indegene earns most of its revenue on output and outcome contracts. Claimed: Microsoft says its job is to help customers "turn tokens into business results." Interpretation: selling access is no longer enough. Sellers now staff and price for results.
- **Unit of analysis.** Large AI sellers and one pharma commercial services firm. Not buyers.
- **Evidence.**
  - Amazon, July 30: a $1 billion investment in forward deployed engineering, which embeds its AI engineers directly with customers. Its chief executive: "our AI and Chips businesses each eclipsed run rates of more than $25 billion" ([Amazon, July 30](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-earnings-q2-2026-report)). Observed commitment.
  - Indegene: "about 60% of revenue now comes from output- and outcome-based contracts." On the omnichannel deal, "revenue recognition is deferred by about three quarters," and results are "measured on a quarterly basis and that trued up on a full year basis" ([Investing.com transcript, July 31](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-indegene-posts-strong-q1-2026-growth-stock-rises-93CH-4826762)). Company-reported.
  - Microsoft passed "30 million paid seats" ([Microsoft, July 29](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast)). A call summary quotes it: net paid seat adds "more than doubled sequentially," and Copilot revenue "accelerated over 60% quarter-over-quarter" after GitHub moved to usage-based billing ([Digital Applied, July 30](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/microsoft-fy26-q4-earnings-copilot-arr)). Secondary source.
  - Against it: investors say "now it is the less the better" on AI spending ([Fortune, July 26](https://fortune.com/2026/07/26/big-tech-earnings-meta-microsoft-apple-amazon-market-revolt-ai-spending/)). Meta still guided "$130-145 billion" ([Meta, July 29](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)).
- **Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Money and contract terms, not just words. Still seller-side only.
- **Obvious explanation.** Sellers need usage to justify huge spending, so they pay to make customers use what they bought.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Hypothesis affected.** "A capability only counts when the organization can use it," and the problem hypothesis that deployment outruns an organization's ability to redesign the workflow and prove the result. Primary pillar: Use. Also Operating Model.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** Sellers now spend to close the gap between access and use. That admits the gap is real.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Emerging. Three kinds of evidence point the same way: staffing (Amazon), pricing (Indegene), and billing and language (Microsoft).
- Against: Microsoft's seat growth shows buyers still pay for access. Indegene says pharma adoption is slow.
- Speeding up on the seller side. Too early to tell for buyers.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**
- **KEY INSIGHT: Outcome contracts turn definitions into money.** If a vendor is paid on results, someone must define the result, the baseline, and which data counts. "Our data does not match" stops being an analytics complaint and becomes a billing dispute. Indegene's quarterly measure and yearly true-up is that negotiation, written into a contract.
- **KEY INSIGHT: An engineer placed by the seller fixes use but blurs judgment.** The party paid to make it work also says whether it worked. The more vendors move inside, the more an independent check matters. His advisory work either competes with these engineers or checks them. That choice is worth making on purpose.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else could explain this?** Forward deployed engineering may be ordinary consulting with a new name.
- **Counterevidence.** Microsoft and Amazon price on use, not results. Indegene's deal pays out later, so no result is proven yet.
- **What would make us wrong?** Outcome contracts stay rare, and embedded engineering shrinks once the sales push ends.

**TRIGGER**
- A second pharma services firm or agency reports outcome pricing, with a share of revenue.
- A drug maker disputes or renegotiates how an outcome is measured.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value.** Ask: "If you paid this vendor only for results, how would you both measure the result, and on whose data?"
- **Deeper asset.** Not yet. Later, maybe a short check of an outcome contract's measure, baseline, and data source.
- **Learning question.** In pharma outcome contracts, who defines the outcome, and who holds the data it is measured on?
- **Long-term value.** If outcome pricing spreads, defining and checking the measure becomes repeatable work. One contract read, then a pattern, then a method.

**DECISION**
- **What changed?** Sellers now spend to make AI get used, not just to sell it.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE.**
- **Why this state?** The behavior is real and has a pharma case. The buyer side is unknown.
- **What moves it next?** A second pharma services firm pricing on results moves it to TALK.

## Signal 3: Control over agents and health data slides toward vendors

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: Cyera agreed to buy Oasis Security, which controls AI agent identities, for about $1 billion. Observed: records patients connect to ChatGPT Health fall outside the federal health privacy law, HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act). Background: the European Union agreed in May to delay its high-risk AI rules. Interpretation, mine: control is being bought privately while public rules hold back.
- **Unit of analysis.** The rules around AI agents and consumer health data.
- **Evidence.**
  - Oasis "focuses on non-human identities, primarily AI agents" ([TechCrunch, July 28](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/cyera-agrees-to-acquire-oasis-security-for-1b-to-safeguard-proliferating-ai-agents/)).
  - "Once that data leaves a patient's provider, HIPAA no longer protects it" ([Paubox, July 29](https://www.paubox.com/blog/chatgpt-health-launches-nationwide-but-medical-records-not-hipaa-protected)).
  - The European Union text was "expected to be published in the EU's Official Journal shortly" ([Cloud Security Alliance, July 8](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/)). I could not confirm the publication date from a source dated this week.
- **Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** Three events in three areas.
- **Obvious explanation.** Unrelated news.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Hypothesis affected.** Territory: Context Continuity and High-Context AI. Also last week's insight that when vendors sell trust as a feature, buyers stop checking it. Primary pillar: Trust and Control.
- **Direction: NOTHING CHANGED.** Too thin to move the belief. It extends last week's ChatGPT Health signal with a privacy gap on record.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Emerging at best. The link is inferred. No buyer or regulator behavior yet.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**
- Nothing beyond Signal 2's point about who judges.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else could explain this?** Security firms merge, privacy firms warn, Europe delays. Normal.
- **Counterevidence.** The August 2 transparency duties in Europe still stand.
- **What would make us wrong?** Public rules on agents or consumer health data tighten soon.

**TRIGGER**
- A second large agent-control deal.
- A United States regulator or state acting on health records held by consumer AI.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value.** Ask: "Which of your AI agents can reach customer data, and who granted that access?"
- **Deeper asset.** Not yet.
- **Learning question.** Do pharma commercial teams know how many agents touch customer data today?
- **Long-term value.** Too early.

**DECISION**
- **What changed?** Nothing changed.
- **Recommended state: MONITOR.**
- **Why this state?** Real events, no pattern yet.
- **What moves it next?** Either trigger above.
