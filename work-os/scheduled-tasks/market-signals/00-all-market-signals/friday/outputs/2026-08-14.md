## Signal 1: The proof gap moves from drug makers to their suppliers

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: a buyer survey, an investigation, and a research paper question whether AI delivered. Interpretation: "show me what it improved" now points at vendors too.
- **Unit of analysis.** How pharma and health buyers judge AI from suppliers.
- **Evidence.**
  - Life Science Strategy Group, August 12, survey of 110 biopharma decision-makers: "71% of sponsors rate their own AI/LLM output highly. Only 27% say the same" of their contract research organizations and vendors ([PR.com, August 12](https://www.pr.com/press-release/976221)). LLM means large language model. Small sample. Missed midweek.
  - STAT, August 12, on Commure, a $7 billion health AI company: "some of its customers report suffering steep financial losses." The company says "the vast majority of its hundreds of customers are happy" ([STAT, August 12](https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/12/inside-commure-athelas-mad-dash-automate-health-care/)). Independent. Providers, not pharma. Paywalled; I read the opening only.
  - Nature Reviews Drug Discovery, August 7: "Evidence of their clinically relevant impact is, so far, disappointingly limited" ([Nature Reviews Drug Discovery, August 7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41573-026-01496-2)). Independent. Research, not commercial.
  - Novo Nordisk, August 10: it "has already seen measurable results," then names none ([Novo Nordisk, August 10](https://www.novonordisk.com/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916594)). "Novo did not disclose the financial terms" ([Drug Discovery Trends, August 10](https://www.drugdiscoverytrends.com/novo-nordisk-announces-strategic-partnership-with-aws-will-it-be-enough-to-turn-around-its-financial-performance/)).
- **Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Three independent sources. None is pharma commercial.
- **Obvious explanation.** AI hype peaked. Critics caught up.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Hypothesis affected.** Problem hypothesis: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Territory: Decision Integrity Under Complexity. Primary pillar: Trust and Control. Also Operating Model.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** Last week the gap sat inside drug makers. Now it also sits between buyers and suppliers.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Pfizer, then Novo, claimed gains with no number. Now buyers discount vendor AI. Different evidence types agree.
- Against it: the survey may show pride in in-house work, not vendor failure.
- Accelerating, mostly outside commercial teams.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**
- **KEY INSIGHT: The 71 versus 27 gap is a grading problem, not only a quality problem.** Inferred: few buyers use one shared test for their own AI and their vendors'. If so, the fix is a common standard, not a better vendor.
- **KEY INSIGHT: "Measurable" is replacing the measure.** Novo said "measurable results" and gave no figure.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else could explain this?** Buyers always rate vendors lower.
- **Counterevidence.** Commure says most customers are happy. The survey is small.
- **What would make us wrong?** A pharma buyer publishes a vendor AI result it checked itself, with a figure.

**TRIGGER**
- A drug maker or agency writes a shared AI acceptance test into vendor contracts.
- A second investigation of a pharma-facing AI vendor's results.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value.** Ask: "Would you grade your own team's AI and your vendor's AI with the same test? What is it?"
- **Deeper asset.** Possibly one test that grades in-house and vendor AI alike. Talks first.
- **Learning question.** Do commercial buyers have a written standard for accepting vendor AI?
- **Long-term value.** If none exists, a neutral grading method could anchor the diagnostic.

**DECISION**
- **What changed?** The proof gap now includes distrust of suppliers, not just missing numbers.
- **Recommended state: TALK.**
- **Why this state?** Cheap to ask, and sharp.
- **What moves it next?** Two buyers who cannot name a shared test move it to TEST.

## Signal 2: Pharma buys AI in small, short pieces while budgets stay tight

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: three pharma suppliers report tight budgets and short deals, but growth in AI products. Interpretation: pharma is shrinking each AI bet, not stopping.
- **Unit of analysis.** How pharma buys from marketing and data suppliers. Not advisory buying.
- **Evidence.**
  - OptimizeRx, a doctor-messaging supplier, August 12: "Revenue decreased 30% to $20.5 million" ([GlobeNewswire, August 12](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/12/3344034/0/en/optimizerx-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-updates-fiscal-year-2026-guidance.html)). On the call: "AI-enabled software offerings, which grew 25% year-over-year." Pharma has "been a little bit conservative with budgets" ([Investing.com transcript, August 12](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-optimizerx-beats-q2-2026-profit-view-as-shares-slip-93CH-4856270)). Company-reported.
  - Same company, August 13 summary: "A major client... did not generate any revenue in Q2" ([Yahoo Finance, August 13](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/optimizerx-corp-oprx-q2-2026-050346456.html)).
  - Definitive Healthcare, August 11: "no significant shift back to multi-year deals," yet "its strongest new business quarter in biopharma in three years" ([Yahoo Finance, August 11](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/definitive-healthcare-corp-q2-2026-123000143.html)). Company-reported.
  - Doximity, August 8: AI search deals use "conservative inventory caps and shorter commitments of three to four months" ([Yahoo Finance, August 8](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/doximity-q1-earnings-call-highlights-050347561.html)). Company-reported.
- **Evidence strength: MEDIUM. EARLY INDICATOR.** Three companies, one buyer behavior. All sellers describing their own sales.
- **Obvious explanation.** Drug pricing pressure makes buyers cautious.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Hypothesis affected.** Need hypothesis: "Leaders need a fast, bounded way to see what their current foundation can actually support." Business opportunity: a bounded diagnostic. Primary pillar: Use. Also Operating Model.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** Inferred. Buyers avoiding long commitments suit a bounded offer. But this is product buying, not advice buying.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- New this week. Three independent sellers describe one buyer.
- At OptimizeRx, AI lines grow while totals shrink: a shift inside the budget.
- Against it: Definitive's strong biopharma quarter.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**
- **KEY INSIGHT: Short deals pull the proof question forward.** A three-to-four-month deal must show value before renewal. Inferred: buyers need a result sooner, and Signal 1 shows they rarely have a way to measure one.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else could explain this?** Sellers blame the economy for lost accounts.
- **Counterevidence.** Definitive's best biopharma quarter in three years.
- **What would make us wrong?** Multi-year pharma deals return next quarter at these suppliers.

**TRIGGER**
- Doximity's short deals renew larger in its fall selling season.
- A drug maker names a fixed-term budget line for AI pilots.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value.** Ask: "Your AI contract ends in four months. What result earns the renewal?"
- **Deeper asset.** Not yet.
- **Learning question.** At renewal of a short AI deal, who decides, on what evidence?
- **Long-term value.** If renewals turn on proof, a renewal check could become a repeat need.

**DECISION**
- **What changed?** Short, capped AI buying is now a pattern.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE.**
- **Why this state?** Visible behavior. Its link to advisory buying is untested.
- **What moves it next?** A buyer who says a renewal hinges on proof they cannot produce.
