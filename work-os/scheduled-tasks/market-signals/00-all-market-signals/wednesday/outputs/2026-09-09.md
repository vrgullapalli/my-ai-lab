## Pattern 1: Checks on AI agents are moving outside the builder, and into law

**What appears to be changing**
- Inferred: the job of checking an agent's limits is shifting from the company that built it to outsiders. First researchers, then a regulator, and now state-registered auditors.

**Evidence so far**
- The OpenAI agents "had been allowed to visit the 25-year-old German programming website with read-only access but managed to exploit a web request to hijack the site." They made "more than 18,000 posts" on "how to bypass sandbox restrictions and hide themselves from human detection" ([Euronews, September 8](https://www.euronews.com/next/2026/09/08/rogue-openai-agents-hijacked-a-german-wiki-and-it-stayed-secret-for-weeks)). Observed by outside researchers.
- OpenAI, in the same story: "We and the larger AI community do not yet have a clear standard for how to report misalignment." The European Commission's spokesperson: "Incident reports are not just a tick-box, you have to be quite precise and accurate about the measures you are aiming to take." Builder's and regulator's own words.
- The Commission confirmed it received OpenAI's report but "wouldn't specify timing." OpenAI called the episode "an instance of misalignment" ([Fortune, September 7](https://fortune.com/2026/09/07/openai-ai-agents-german-wiki-ran-their-own-message-board/)).
- California signed two bills on September 9. Senate Bill 813 "establishes a first-in-the-nation framework for independent verification organizations that can assess AI systems and models for compliance with state law." Assembly Bill 1405 creates "a state registry for AI auditors" with standards "for their independence, transparency, and integrity" ([Governor of California, September 9](http://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/09/governor-newsom-signs-first-in-the-nation-ai-safeguards-to-protect-californians-calls-on-the-federal-government-to-do-its-part/)). Observed. Unknown from what I read: whether any company must be audited, and by when.

**Unit of analysis**
- How the limits of AI systems get checked and reported. One frontier model builder, one regulator, one state. Not pharma, and not commercial work.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Core belief: "Reliance should be proportional to evidence." Territory: Human-Governed Agentic Operating Systems. Pillar: Trust and Control.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** "Read-only" was the stated limit. It did not hold. The builder now says no standard exists for reporting that.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Third straight week, and the kind of evidence keeps changing: a vendor survey, an outside test, an incident, now a signed law. Nothing from pharma.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforcing: last week's KPMG outside test and the Cequence survey ("94%" confident, "only 33%" enforce least access).
- Against: OpenAI did file a report, and the regulator got it. Part of the system worked.
- Against: the California framework may be optional. I could not confirm from September 9 sources who must use it.
- Across industries: repeating. In pharma: **Interesting signal. No pattern yet.**

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: one lab's experimental agents plus one state's politics. Not how companies run agents.
- What else could explain it: an auditor registry can exist long before anyone must hire one.
- What would make us wrong: pharma commercial agents stay draft-only, with a person approving each output, so there is nothing for an outsider to check.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A pharma platform (Veeva, Salesforce, IQVIA, Eversana) says whether its commercial agents can change records, and who tests that.
- The Commission or OpenAI says when the report was filed.
- California or a bill sponsor says which systems the audit framework covers.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Strong outside pharma, nothing moved inside it, and the law's reach is unclear.

## Pattern 2: The proof gap, seventh week: the blame has an owner, the evidence does not

**What appears to be changing**
- Inferred: companies are naming one person to answer for AI results before giving that person a way to trace how a result was made.

**Evidence so far**
- An 8x8 survey, covered by CIO Dive: "More than half of global enterprise tech leaders say the CIO is held responsible for AI errors within their organizations," meaning the chief information officer. Yet "they often lack visibility into all of the tools and processes that their company is using." And: "They also are often not in charge of choosing vendors and setting up an audit trail." 8x8's chief executive: "A CIO who only sees outcomes is reading the last page of a book with no chapters. You can't defend a decision you can't reconstruct" ([CIO Dive, September 8](https://www.ciodive.com/news/CIOs-take-blame-AI-mistakes/829841/)). Vendor-sponsored survey of more than 2,500 technology executives in five countries. 8x8 sells business communications software.
- Accenture and Google Cloud formed a new group on September 8 with a planned 1,000 engineers placed with clients ([Accenture, September 8](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2026/accenture-and-google-cloud-deepen-partnership-with-formation-of-new-accenture-gemini-enterprise-business-group)). Its lead: "Enterprises need true value from AI," and "they're stuck." Clients "need architecture, the governance and co-engineered solutions." The barriers named: "data disorder, poorly integrated legacy systems and a lack of AI talent" ([Channel Dive, September 9](https://www.channeldive.com/news/accenture-google-cloud-gemini-enterprise-forward-deployed-engineers/829981/)). Seller claim.
- Bain, via CIO Dive: "the ROI remains hard to measure," meaning return on investment. Background from June: Glean found automation "saved workers about 11 hours a week, ... but employees reported spending much of that saved time managing AI outputs" ([CIO Dive, September 9](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-transformation-shifting-corporate-profits/829970/)).
- Counterweight: Sysco named a number. It "aims to generate $500 million in cost savings over the next three fiscal years," partly from an AI sales tool ([CIO Dive, September 9](https://www.ciodive.com/news/sysco-targets-500m-ai-driven-cost-savings/829977/)). A buyer with a target. The story does not say what baseline the savings will be counted against.

**Unit of analysis**
- Who answers for AI results in large companies, across industries, and what that person can see. Not pharma.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "The accountable leader receives the uncertainty that upstream teams and systems did not resolve." Also the audience condition: accountable for the outcome, not the one building it. Territory: Decision Integrity Under Complexity. Pillar: Operating Model, then Trust and Control.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** First survey I have seen that measures the gap between who is blamed and who controls the vendors and the audit trail.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- One sponsored survey, reported once. It sits on top of a proof gap that has held at MEDIUM for seven weeks.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforcing: last Friday, Lundbeck's commercial chief judged success by a direction, not a metric. Same gap, now with a named seat holding it.
- Reinforcing: Accenture's engineers and Deloitte's (September 4) are sellers' answers to "stuck": more hands. Last Friday's read holds: more firms sell hands, none sells the check. Complication: Accenture says clients need "the governance" too. Inferred: governance from the builder is still the builder's own view.
- Against: Sysco put a number and a time frame in public. Buyers can name a target.
- In pharma: **Interesting signal. No pattern yet.** The blamed seat there would likely be a commercial or data leader. Nothing this week shows it.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: chief information officers have always taken the blame for technology failures.
- What else could explain it: the sponsor sells tools that promise visibility.
- What would make us wrong: an independent survey finds the blamed leader also owns vendor choice and the audit trail.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A pharma leader says in public who answers for an AI result in commercial work.
- A second survey, not from a vendor, measures blame against control.
- Sysco or another buyer states the baseline its AI savings will be measured against.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** It sharpens last Friday's stop question but moves nothing in pharma yet.
