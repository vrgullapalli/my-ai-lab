# All Midweek 2026-07-22
_Backfill written on 2026-09-10 for 2026-07-22. No daily briefs existed that week, so this brief gathered its own evidence from sources dated 2026-07-17 to 2026-07-22._

## The short answer

Two things may be forming this week. Neither needs action before Friday.

1. Big software makers are paying to own the trusted-data layer that sits under AI. Four moves in six days point the same way.
2. Large companies are paying for AI in production, but pharma's own public words this week put AI in research, not in commercial work.

---

## Pattern 1: Software makers are buying the trusted-data layer under AI

**What appears to be changing**
- Vendors are selling AI on the strength of the data underneath it, and paying to own that layer. The pitch is shifting from "a smarter model" to "a model that sits on data you can trust."

**Evidence so far**
- SAP closed its purchase of Prior Labs, a maker of AI built for tables of business data, on July 17. SAP committed "more than €1 billion over the next four years to scale it into a globally leading frontier AI lab for the structured data that underpins the world's businesses" ([SAP, July 17](https://news.sap.com/2026/07/sap-completes-prior-labs-acquisition/)). SAP's chief technology officer said "the greatest untapped opportunity in enterprise AI wasn't large language models; it was AI built for the structured data that runs the world's businesses" ([Tech.eu, July 17](https://tech.eu/2026/07/17/sap-acquires-prior-labs-just-18-months-after-launch-in-eur1b-deal/)). Observed.
- Progress Software agreed to buy Domo's analytics business for "$400 million in cash." Progress's chief executive said Domo "brings together data integration, governed analytics, automation, and AI-powered data products in a way that helps customers move faster with trusted data" ([Domo release via Yahoo Finance, July 22](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/domo-announces-agreement-sell-substantially-201500810.html)). Observed deal. The reason is claimed.
- ServiceNow said its AI Control Tower, a tool for overseeing AI across a company, "expanded with new discovery, observation, governance, security, and measurement capabilities" ([ServiceNow, July 22](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)). Claimed by the vendor.
- The one pharma instance: Doceree's Semmelweis model, launched July 14 and covered in depth on July 21, runs on "Doceree's ESPYIAN identity graph of more than 6 million verified health care providers." It is "designed to optimize toward filled and refilled prescriptions rather than impressions or claims" ([Pharmaceutical Commerce, July 21](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/inside-semmelweis-docerees-ai-bet-on-pharma-marketing)). The article gives no accuracy or outcome data. Claimed.

**Unit of analysis**
- Enterprise data and AI software vendors. This is not yet a claim about pharma buyers.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy." Pillar: Foundation first, then Trust and Control.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS**. Vendors are now spending money on the idea that AI is only as good as the governed data under it.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- Three independent companies, with real money behind two of them. But this is vendor behavior and vendor language. No buyer spend or outcome is visible yet.

**Pattern basis**
- SAP and Progress reinforce each other. Both are spending on the data layer, not the model.
- ServiceNow adds governance, not data. It is related but weaker.
- Doceree is the only pharma-specific case. It is one vendor, and its claims have no outcome proof yet.
- Emerging, not yet repeating. This is the first week in the record.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: vendors say "trusted data" in every cycle. It sells.
- What else: Domo may simply be a weak company sold cheap. It stays public after the sale to keep more than $900 million in past tax losses. That looks like a clean-up, not a bet on demand.
- What would make us wrong: buyers keep buying models and agents directly and skip the data layer.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- SAP's next quarterly results. Does SAP tie the Prior Labs deal to customer demand, or only to its own strategy? Does it spend or give up profit to do it?
- Any pharma commercial buyer who names data quality or identity as the reason for a purchase.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** One more earnings report this week will show whether this is a direction or just deal noise.

## Pattern 2: Large companies pay for AI in production. Pharma commercial is silent.

**What appears to be changing**
- Across big companies, AI spending has moved from trials into paid, running systems. Pharma's own public statements this week do not show the same move in commercial work.

**Evidence so far**
- ServiceNow: its AI "crossed $1 billion in annual contract value," and "agentic deployments of ServiceNow AI increased ninefold in just nine months" ([ServiceNow, July 22](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)). Observed revenue. Vendor-reported deployments.
- IBM: "As AI adoption moves from experimentation to enterprise-scale deployment, the company is also investing in more specialized technical and client-facing talent" ([IBM, July 22](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-07-22-IBM-RELEASES-SECOND-QUARTER-RESULTS)). Claimed.
- Alphabet: "Cloud backlog grew to $514 billion," "Nearly 90% of the Fortune 100 are using Gemini Enterprise," and "We continue to be supply constrained" ([Google, July 22](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q2-2026/)). Observed backlog. Usage is claimed.
- Counterevidence from pharma: Novartis's July 21 results release mentions AI once, in its list of risks: "the development or adoption of new technologies, including artificial intelligence" ([Novartis, July 21](https://www.novartis.com/news/media-releases/novartis-delivered-sales-growth-q2-and-further-advanced-pipeline-full-year-guidance-reaffirmed)). Observed.
- Bristol Myers Squibb's big AI computing expansion on July 20 is research only. The release describes AI "handling the execution of complex, data-intensive tasks while scientists focus on direction, interpretation, and the decisions that require deep human expertise" ([Bristol Myers Squibb, July 20](https://news.bms.com/news/corporate-financial/2026/Bristol-Myers-Squibb-to-Build-the-Most-Powerful-AI-Factory-in-Life-Sciences-with-NVIDIA/default.aspx)). Observed. There is no mention of commercial use.
- Cost is still unsettled. Anthropic changed its plans for its top model again: it "will be included in all Max and Team Premium plans starting July 20, but with sharply reduced limits," and lower-tier users get "a one-time $100 usage credit, then have to pay API prices," meaning pay-per-use through the application programming interface ([The Decoder, July 18](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-slashes-claude-fable-5-limits-in-max-and-team-premium-and-pushes-pro-users-toward-api-pricing/)). Observed.

**Unit of analysis**
- Large enterprises in general, compared with how pharma companies describe their own AI in public. Not pharma commercial teams directly.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Pillar: Use, then Operating Model.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES**. Spending is real in general. But in pharma, the public money and language this week sit in research. Commercial AI may be quieter, or it may be happening without being talked about.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM for large enterprises. LOW for pharma commercial. REINFORCING INDICATOR for the general shift.**
- Three separate earnings reports agree. For pharma, two company statements are too few to judge.

**Pattern basis**
- ServiceNow, IBM, and Alphabet reinforce each other on spend. They are all sellers, not buyers.
- Novartis and Bristol Myers Squibb point the other way for commercial work.
- Anthropic's plan change is a separate thread about cost, not adoption.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: pharma talks about AI where investors reward it, which is research.
- What else: commercial AI may be sitting inside vendor contracts, like customer relationship management tools and agencies, where pharma does not report it.
- What would make us wrong: a pharma earnings call names a commercial AI result with a number.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- Roche's half-year results. Does it mention AI outside research and diagnostics?
- Any pharma company that names a commercial AI outcome, not just an AI activity.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** More pharma results are due this week and next. One day will not change the read.
