## Pattern 1: Wrong agent answers traced to missing business meaning

**What appears to be changing**
- The story of why agents fail is moving from "the model is weak" to "the agent does not know what our words mean." The offered fix is a governed layer holding approved definitions, trusted sources, and rules.

**Evidence so far**
- Background, July 10: a VentureBeat survey of 101 companies found "57% of enterprises traced a confident but wrong AI agent answer to missing or inconsistent business context." Only "Twenty-five percent of respondents run one in production," meaning a governed context layer. "Retrieval over documents is the default way agents get business context for 38% of enterprises" ([VentureBeat, July 10](https://venturebeat.com/data/57-of-enterprises-have-watched-ai-agents-be-confidently-wrong-the-fix-is-an-agentic-context-layer-but-who-has-one)). Observed survey result. Small sample.
- Neo4j, July 20: "Enterprise AI doesn't fail because of the model... It fails because the agent doesn't understand the business it's acting in." Its example: "the app knows status = 2 means 'suspended pending review'; so does the analyst; the agent doesn't" ([Neo4j, July 20](https://neo4j.com/blog/agentic-ai/enterprise-knowledge-layer/)). Claimed by a vendor. No customers or results named.
- OvalEdge, July 20: agents "need to understand what the data means, which definitions are approved, which sources are trusted, and which policies apply before they generate an answer or support a decision" ([OvalEdge, July 20](https://www.ovaledge.com/blog/enterprise-ontology-platform)). Claimed by a vendor.
- Atlan, July 22: "A knowledge graph without an ontology can connect 'Customer' to a dozen tables, but cannot tell an agent whether 'Customer' means a lead or a purchaser." Atlan reports its own test: "Adding this context improved AI's text-to-SQL accuracy by 38%," meaning accuracy at turning plain questions into database queries ([Atlan, July 22](https://atlan.com/know/ai-agent/ontology/ontology-design-for-ai/)). Self-reported. Not independent.

**Unit of analysis**
- Enterprise data and AI software vendors, plus one small cross-industry survey. Not pharma.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy." Also his Context Continuity and High-Context AI territory. Primary lens: Meaning and Context. Secondary: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS**. The survey puts a number on "access is not understanding." The vendor writing adds volume, not proof.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM for the problem, LOW for the fix. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- One survey from before this week. Three vendor pieces this week selling the same answer. No buyer outcome.

**Pattern basis**
- The survey and vendor writing point the same way, but vendors are not independent. Three posts in three days is repeated talk, not change.
- The caution sits inside the survey: only a quarter run a governed layer.
- Emerging. Not yet repeating.

**Challenge**
- Simplest read: vendors who sell graphs, catalogs, and ontologies are marketing into agent budgets.
- What else: many "wrong answers" may come from bad data, weak testing, or unreliable models. A survey that asks about context invites a context answer.
- Wrong if: companies with a governed layer report the same error rate as companies without one.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A named company, ideally in life sciences, that traces an agent error to one specific definition and fixes it with a definition, not more documents.
- Any measured result from someone other than the vendor.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** The problem is clear, but this week's proof is vendor talk.

## Pattern 2: Shared definitions kept up as ongoing work, in pharma

**What appears to be changing**
- A shared definition set is not built once. It gets change requests, versions, and validation rules.

**Evidence so far**
- Pistoia Alliance, a nonprofit group of life sciences companies, released version 1.7 of its Identification of Medicinal Products Ontology on July 22. It calls the ontology "the open, machine-readable framework that turns the ISO IDMP standards into a shared digital language for describing medicinal products consistently across systems, companies and jurisdictions" (ISO is the International Organization for Standardization, IDMP is Identification of Medicinal Products). The release adds pack size rules "following EMA and ISO guidance" (EMA is the European Medicines Agency), maps units like vial and syringe to agency reference data, and adds validation rules. It answers "change requests raised by the group of experts" ([Pistoia Alliance, July 22](https://pistoiaalliance.org/news/pistoia-alliance-releases-version-1-7-of-the-idmp-ontology/)). Observed.
- Background, July 10 and 13: the Open Semantic Interchange, a shared format for business metric definitions led by Snowflake, was accepted into the Apache Incubator, a neutral open-source home, and renamed Apache Ossie ([Apache Ossie, July 10](https://ossie.apache.org/updates/)). Solid, a context-layer startup, joined so "semantic context can automatically move seamlessly across AI agents, data warehouses..." ([Solid release, July 13](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/solid-joins-snowflake-and-industry-leaders-to-advance-open-standards-for-ai-ready-semantic-context-302834217.html)). Observed move. Purpose claimed.

**Unit of analysis**
- Product master data in pharma regulatory work, and cross-industry metric definitions. Not commercial customer data.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Need hypothesis: "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency." Also his Continuity of Meaning territory. Secondary: Operating Model.
- Direction: **TOO EARLY TO TELL** for commercial work. It shows the shape of the work, not demand.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- The Pistoia release is real and in pharma. It is one release, and it is regulatory.

**Pattern basis**
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet. Both efforts treat definitions as a shared, maintained asset, but they serve different people.

**Challenge**
- Simplest read: regulators require this product data, so the industry keeps it current. Compliance work, not an AI shift.
- What else: standards groups ship versions on a schedule. A version number is not news by itself.
- Wrong if: commercial teams never name shared definitions as the thing blocking AI.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A commercial-side definition effort, such as one shared meaning of "active prescriber" or "reached customer" across brands, from a pharma company or its agency.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** One release does not make a trend.

## Pattern 3: A pharma marketing vendor sells "clinical context" as the product

**What appears to be changing**
- In pharma marketing AI, the pitch is moving from "a smarter model" to "a model that knows where the doctor stands." The context is the product.

**Evidence so far**
- Background, July 14: Doceree launched Semmelweis. The release says it "reasons over deterministic clinical context," including an identity graph "of more than six million verified HCPs" (health care providers), and "distinguishes between a physician researching a therapy and one about to prescribe it" ([Doceree release, July 14](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/doceree-unveils-semmelweis-the-first-ai-model-purpose-built-for-pharma-commercialization-302825341.html)). Claimed by the vendor.
- Pharmaceutical Commerce, July 21: the model is "grounded in clinical context rather than the open web." The article gives no outcome data and quotes no outside voices ([Pharmaceutical Commerce, July 21](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/inside-semmelweis-docerees-ai-bet-on-pharma-marketing)). Same event. Not independent.
- Background, June 16: Tellius, an analytics vendor, shows why words matter in pharma data. "TRx share or NRx share?" it asks, meaning total or new prescriptions. "Each is a defensible judgment call, and changing any one of them between runs moves the answer while the data sits still" ([Tellius, June 16](https://www.tellius.com/resources/blog/ai-agents-for-pharma-commercial-teams-the-2026-field-guide-to-use-cases-vendors-and-whats-actually-real)). Claimed.

**Unit of analysis**
- Pharma commercial AI vendors. One company this week.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Local definitions, tagging, mappings, scripts, and ownership choices make the same data support different answers." Secondary: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES**. If the context lives in a vendor's graph and signals, the pharma company may not own the meaning its AI acts on.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- One launch and one article about that launch.

**Pattern basis**
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet.

**Challenge**
- Simplest read: a vendor launch with a big claim.
- What else: "clinical context" may be a new label for targeting data the vendor already sold.
- Wrong if: buyers treat it as another media channel and never ask whose definitions it runs on.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A pharma company or agency naming the product and a result.
- A second pharma commercial vendor making the same "context is the product" claim.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** One vendor, no buyer.
