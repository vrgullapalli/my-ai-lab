## Pattern 1: Business definitions move into code, with version control

**What appears to be changing**
- Last week, a vendor said keeping meaning current is harder than building it. This week, a large platform shipped a way to handle definitions like software: written in code, versioned, and pushed out together with the apps that use them. Observed product change. What it means for meaning is my inference.

**Evidence so far**
- Palantir, August 4, "Introducing SuperRepo." It says: "Your code definitions are the source of truth, and they materialize as real entities on your enrollment when your product is deployed" ([Palantir announcements, August 4](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/announcements/2026-08)). Observed release.
- Same note: "SDK bindings are generated from your Ontology-as-code definitions and regenerated automatically whenever those definitions change." SDK means software development kit. In plain words, when a definition changes, the code that depends on it updates too. Observed release.
- Same note: the bundle is "self-contained, reproducible, and cryptographically signed." That means you can prove which version of the definitions was in use. Observed release.
- Last week's context, reopened: Modus's chief technology officer, Tomer Mesika, said "Building a context layer is not the hardest part. Keeping it current is." He added, "Every change your business makes changes the context AI depends on" ([The Next Web, July 29](https://thenextweb.com/news/modus-10m-seed-insight-partners-context-warehouse-enterprise-ai)). Vendor claim, dated before this window.

**Unit of analysis**
- One software platform's developer tooling for its business model of objects, links, and actions, which Palantir calls an ontology. Cross-industry. Not pharma. No customer named.

**Hypothesis affected**
- His Continuity of Meaning territory: how a system can change without losing the shared meaning that makes its outputs trustworthy. Also the need hypothesis "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency."
- Primary lens: Meaning and Context. Secondary: Repeatability and Scale, since the bundle installs the same definitions in more than one place.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS, narrowly.** A platform now builds definition upkeep into its release process. That is a small, real move from "upkeep is hard" to "upkeep is a workflow." Inferred.
- What this adds: version control answers "which definition was live, and when?" It doesn't answer "who gets to change it?" A code review only works if the reviewer knows what "active prescriber" is supposed to mean. So the ownership gap from the last two weeks is still open. Inferred.

**Evidence strength: LOW. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- It's an observed release, not a claim. But it's one vendor, aimed at developers, with no customer and no result. It adds to last week's point, and doesn't prove anyone needed it.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforcing: a vendor naming upkeep as the real cost (July 29), expert change requests driving a pharma regulatory ontology (July 22), a federal health data standard on a yearly update cycle (July 23), and now a platform building versioned definitions into its release process (August 4). Four weeks, four kinds of source.
- Contradicting: nothing found. But nothing shows a commercial team paying for upkeep either.
- Probably noise: none this week.
- Status: Repeating, thin. Not accelerating.

**Challenge**
- Simplest read: this is developer convenience. One repository, local testing, simpler deployment. Palantir never uses the word "meaning."
- What else: "definitions as code" is an old software habit, now reaching business models. It may say more about how developers like to work than about meaning.
- Outside this lens: this could be a Foundation or Operating Model story, about release tooling and who approves changes. The meaning part is my reading of it.
- Wrong if: no customer ever describes definition changes going through review, or commercial teams keep changing definitions in spreadsheets and dashboards that never touch code.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A second platform or data vendor shipping version history or review steps for business definitions, not just for code.
- Anyone, especially a pharma commercial or agency team, naming who approves a change to a shared definition and how the change reaches their AI tools.
