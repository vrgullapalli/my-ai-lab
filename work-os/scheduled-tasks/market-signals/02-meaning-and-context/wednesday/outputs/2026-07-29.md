## Pattern 1: Definition layers get funded, and they learn meaning from use

**What appears to be changing**
- The fix is moving from "write down your definitions" to "let software work out your definitions from how your analysts already query the data." Investors are now paying for that.

**Evidence so far**
- Credible Data, July 27 and 28: raised a $10 million seed round. Its release says it "analyzes how an enterprise actually uses its data," using "real queries and edge cases surfaced during use," "to generate a universal, governable semantic model" ([PR Newswire, July 28](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/credible-data-raises-10-million-to-bring-trusted-business-context-to-enterprise-ai-data-302835776.html)). Claimed by the vendor.
- One named customer, quoted in the same release. Peter Nummerdor of VideoAmp says it gives "AI systems the same governed understanding of our metrics that our best analysts carry in their heads." Claimed through the vendor. No numbers.
- Chief executive Kyle Nesbit: "It's the same pain point people have had for 30 years, the lack of governed data analysis. Now with AI, it's the same problem, but orders of magnitude more chaos and pain" ([VentureBeat, July 27](https://venturebeat.com/data/57-of-enterprises-traced-a-wrong-ai-answer-to-missing-business-context-credible-bets-portable-open-source-semantic-code-beats-proprietary-metadata)).
- Modus, July 29: raised a $10 million seed round led by Insight Partners. Its product "learns from metadata and usage patterns across data warehouses, BI tools, pipelines, code repositories, documentation, and collaboration systems," where BI means business intelligence. It says it "composes only the context relevant to a particular AI interaction" ([The Next Web, July 29](https://thenextweb.com/news/modus-10m-seed-insight-partners-context-warehouse-enterprise-ai)). No named customers. No measured results.
- Background, July 23: Neo4j's new preview has "AI tooling" that "proposes a graph model from your tables," including relationships "even when foreign keys are not declared" ([Neo4j, July 23](https://neo4j.com/blog/auradb/neo4j-virtual-graph-is-now-in-public-preview/)). Claimed. Same idea: the machine proposes the structure, a person approves it.

**Unit of analysis**
- Two enterprise data startups and one graph database vendor. Cross-industry. Not pharma.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Local definitions, tagging, mappings, scripts, and ownership choices make the same data support different answers." Also his Transferable Operating Intelligence territory: which parts of expert judgment can be made explicit and reusable. Primary lens: Meaning and Context. Secondary: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Learning meaning from use is a real way to capture what analysts carry in their heads. But if two teams calculate "share" two different ways, the tool learns both. Someone still has to decide which one wins. Inferred.

**Evidence strength: LOW to MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR for the problem, EARLY INDICATOR for the method.**
- Funding is an observed commitment, stronger than a blog post. But every result is a vendor claim. One customer quote, no numbers.

**Pattern basis**
- Two separate companies with different investors, the same week, the same method. That is more than one event.
- The survey figure in the VentureBeat piece comes from the same June survey as last week. That's a duplicate, so it adds no new support.
- Nothing contradicts it. Nothing proves the fix works.
- Status: Repeating.

**Challenge**
- Simplest read: seed money follows a hot label. "Context layer" is this summer's pitch.
- What else: this may be old business intelligence modeling with a new name. The chief executive almost says so: "the same pain point people have had for 30 years."
- Outside this lens: many wrong answers still come from bad data or weak testing. A definitions layer fixes neither.
- Wrong if: the learned models need the same hand review as before, so nothing new gets captured.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A customer, speaking for itself rather than through the vendor, explaining how the tool handled two teams with conflicting definitions of the same metric.
- Any pharma or life sciences buyer using one of these tools.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Money is a real signal, but no buyer has shown a result.

## Pattern 2: Keeping meaning current is named as the hard part

**What appears to be changing**
- Last week's pharma example showed definitions get versions and change requests. This week, a vendor calls upkeep the real job, and a federal health standard shows its yearly upkeep cycle.

**Evidence so far**
- Modus's chief technology officer, Tomer Mesika: "Every change your business makes changes the context AI depends on." The article adds that "keeping those systems accurate as organizations evolve can become an ongoing engineering responsibility" ([The Next Web, July 29](https://thenextweb.com/news/modus-10m-seed-insight-partners-context-warehouse-enterprise-ai)). Claimed.
- Background, July 23: the Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology released version 7 of the United States Core Data for Interoperability, a shared set of health data elements. It adds 31 new elements. The bulletin describes "a draft version released for comment in January and a final version released in July," a schedule it says lets the standard "keep pace with clinical, technological, and policy changes" ([Health IT standards bulletin, July 23](https://healthit.gov/standards-and-technology/onc-standards-bulletin/onc-standards-bulletin-2026-2/)). Observed.
- Last week, July 22: Pistoia Alliance's ontology version 1.7 answered "change requests raised by the group of experts" ([Pistoia Alliance, July 22](https://pistoiaalliance.org/news/pistoia-alliance-releases-version-1-7-of-the-idmp-ontology/)).

**Unit of analysis**
- Shared definition sets in health records and pharma regulatory product data, plus one vendor's claim. Not pharma commercial.

**Hypothesis affected**
- His Continuity of Meaning territory. Also the need hypothesis "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency." Secondary: Operating Model.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS, narrowly.** Two real upkeep cycles in two weeks, plus a vendor naming upkeep as the job. Still no commercial example.

**Evidence strength: LOW. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- The standards cycles are observed. The vendor line is a sales pitch.

**Pattern basis**
- The same idea shows up in pharma regulatory work, health records, and an enterprise vendor. Different domains, which helps.
- But standards bodies always ship on a schedule. That part is not new.
- Status: Repeating, thin.

**Challenge**
- Simplest read: upkeep was always part of standards work. The vendor is selling automated upkeep.
- What else: compliance deadlines drive the standards, not AI.
- Wrong if: commercial teams build a definitions layer once and never report upkeep as a cost.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A pharma company or agency naming who maintains a commercial definition, like "active prescriber," and how a change reaches its AI tools.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Real, but not commercial and not new in kind.
