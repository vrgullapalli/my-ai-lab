## Pattern 1: Context failures are common, and mostly invisible without a definition layer

**What appears to be changing**
- The question is moving from "do agents fail on context?" to "can you even tell when they do?" Observed survey finding. The framing is the survey's. What it means for Venkat's buyers is my inference.

**Evidence so far**
- VentureBeat Pulse Research, August 12, 101 firms with over 100 employees, one July wave: "Sixty-eight percent have traced a confident but wrong agent answer to missing or inconsistent business context in the past six months" ([VentureBeat, August 12](https://venturebeat.com/resources/agent-context-layers-enterprises-governing-their-ai-data-are-catching-twice-as-many-bad-answers-as-the-ones-who-arent)). Claimed by respondents.
- Same report: firms that have or are building a layer "report recurring context failures at 50%," against 21% for those without one.
- Its reading: "Without one, the same failure occurs and gets logged as a model problem, a user error, or nothing at all." And: "A low reported context-failure rate is not evidence of a healthy context layer. It is at least as likely to be evidence that nobody is looking."
- Earlier wave, for comparison: 57% in June ([VentureBeat, July 10](https://venturebeat.com/data/57-of-enterprises-have-watched-ai-agents-be-confidently-wrong-the-fix-is-an-agentic-context-layer-but-who-has-one)). The comparison is mine, not the survey's.
- Pharma echo, low weight: an August 7 consulting blog on pharma commercial data says "the same HCP, product, or territory is represented inconsistently" across sources (HCP means healthcare professional) ([Perceptive Analytics, August 7](https://www.perceptive-analytics.com/how-to-build-pharma-commercial-data-for-ai-in-2026/)). A vendor's view, no named client.

**Unit of analysis**
- Cross-industry firms with deployed agents. Healthcare and life sciences are 14% of respondents, so roughly 14 firms. Not pharma commercial specifically.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy." Also the need hypothesis "Leaders need a fast, bounded way to see what their current foundation can actually support." Secondary: Trust and Control.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS, with a twist.** Context failures look common. The twist: a firm reporting few failures may just lack the definitions to trace them. Inferred.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Named survey, stated method, no sponsor disclosed. But it's small, self-reported, cross-industry, and people who built a layer may simply look harder.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforcing: last week's Databricks, Palantir, and Veeva meaning layers; Veeva's own note that agents lacked business context.
- Contradicting: none found. But no firm named, no failure described in detail.
- Status: Repeating. Now measured, not just asserted.

**Challenge**
- Simplest read: a media outlet selling a "context layer" story to a context-layer audience.
- What else: the 50% versus 21% gap may reflect who answers, not what they can see.
- Outside this lens: some "context" failures may be stale data or weak retrieval, a Foundation problem.
- Wrong if: a later wave shows firms with layers catching fewer failures over time, or failures traced to model error, not meaning.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A named firm describing one traced failure: which definition, what the agent said, who caught it.
- The same survey split out for healthcare and life sciences.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Useful framing, but no buyer and nothing time-bound.

## Pattern 2: Can you see what changed, and who changed it?

**What appears to be changing**
- Last week, platforms started ranking competing definitions for agents. This week, the missing piece got named: no record of what the ranking learned. Meanwhile, another platform shipped a record of who edited what. Observed. The link between them is my inference.

**Evidence so far**
- Databricks community thread on its ontology, a practitioner on July 30: "Popular isn't the same as correct. The ranking can surface a widely-used definition that's wrong for the specific question." Also: "there's no visible diff or approval flow" ([Databricks Community](https://community.databricks.com/t5/data-engineering/genie-ontology/td-p/164536)). Dated before the window. Found late, not new this week.
- Same thread, a Databricks staff member on August 4: it "provides per-answer citations, but not yet a full reviewable changelog or diff of newly learned context." A vendor concedes the gap.
- The practitioner called that "the main adoption blocker" in regulated settings.

**Hypothesis affected**

- Outside this lens: this is partly an Operating Model question. Who approves a definition change?
- Wrong if: Databricks ships a changelog soon and nobody complains, or ranked definitions prove right without review.

**Next evidence before Friday**
