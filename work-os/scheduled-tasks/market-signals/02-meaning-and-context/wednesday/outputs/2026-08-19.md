## Pattern 1: Reviewing what a machine learned about meaning. Some platforms do, some don't

**What appears to be changing**
- Platforms that learn definitions from usage are splitting into two kinds. One kind lets a person approve learned meaning before it goes live. The other kind doesn't, yet. Observed for AWS and Databricks. The split is my inference.

**Evidence so far**
- AWS, as reported June 17: its new knowledge graph service "gets smarter through agent usage over time." A vice president: "The knowledge graph improves itself over time as it learns which sources produce correct results and which parts get used." Then: "Data stewards manage the graph through the AWS Management Console, reviewing inferred relationships, promoting them to production and attaching business definitions and usage rules" ([VentureBeat, June 17](https://venturebeat.com/data/aws-enters-the-context-layer-race-with-a-graph-that-learns-from-agents-not-manual-curation)). Before the window. It's a vendor description reported by the outlet. I haven't seen it work.
- Databricks, still: a staff member said on August 4 its ontology offers "per-answer citations, but not yet a full reviewable changelog or diff of newly learned context" ([Databricks Community](https://community.databricks.com/t5/data-engineering/genie-ontology/td-p/164536)). No release note changed that from August 14 to 19. Observed.
- Palantir, August 18, a neighbor: its new agent tool for improving AI systems offers "Integration with Global Branching for reviewing proposed changes before merging" ([Palantir announcements](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/announcements/2026-08)). Observed. It reviews changes to AI systems, not changes to definitions.

**Unit of analysis**
- Three cross-industry data platforms' agent features. Not pharma. No customer named.

**Hypothesis affected**
- His Context Continuity territory: carrying context forward "without gaining invisible authority." Also "Organizations keep adding technology to a coordination and meaning problem." Secondary: Trust and Control.
- **Direction: COMPLICATES.** The worry holds for Databricks. But human sign-off on learned meaning already exists as a design choice elsewhere. So the question for a leader isn't "does anyone review this?" It's "does *your* platform let someone review it, and does anyone actually do it?" Inferred.

**Evidence strength: LOW. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Vendor descriptions and one staff reply. No real case of a wrong learned definition, caught or missed.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforcing: the August 6 default-on and August 13 open-to-all Databricks releases, the practitioner warning from July 30, and now a platform that designs review in.
- Contradicting: AWS, which weakens "the whole market skips review."
- Noise: none.
- Status: Repeating. Not strengthening. No new Databricks move this week.

**Challenge**
- Simplest read: new preview features ship first and add admin screens later. AWS may just be further along.
- What else: a steward review screen is not proof anyone uses it. A review step nobody staffs is the same as none.
- Outside this lens: who staffs that review is an Operating Model question.
- Wrong if: Databricks ships a changelog and nobody cares, or AWS customers report stewards rubber-stamping inferred definitions.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A practitioner describing a wrong learned definition, and who caught it.
- Anyone saying whether AWS stewards actually review inferred definitions in practice, or approve them in bulk.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** It corrects our framing but meets no trigger.

## Interesting signal. No pattern yet: Palantir turns past analyses into reusable knowledge

- Palantir, August 18: its analyst agent "now supports new ways to capture reusable knowledge, analyze time series data, work with Ontology interfaces, and export results to Foundry resources." And: "The new Analysis lookup tool turns past analyses into a source of reusable knowledge" ([Palantir announcements](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/announcements/2026-08)). Observed release. No customer or result named.
- Why it may matter, inferred: this is one way to capture judgment that usually lives in analysts' heads. But a past analysis carries the definitions it used back then. A pharma consultant put the risk plainly on August 7: "Historical data reflects old system logic, not current definitions" ([Perceptive Analytics](https://www.perceptive-analytics.com/how-to-build-pharma-commercial-data-for-ai-in-2026/)). Background, before the window. Reusing old analyses could carry an old meaning into a new answer.
- Hypothesis: his Transferable Operating Intelligence territory, which parts of expert judgment can be made reusable. **TOO EARLY TO TELL.**
- Watch next: whether reused analyses show which definitions they used and when.
- **State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.**

## Interesting signal. No pattern yet: Private equity buys into a knowledge graph company
