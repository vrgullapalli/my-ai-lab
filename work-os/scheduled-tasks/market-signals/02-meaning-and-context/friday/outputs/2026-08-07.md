## The short answer

Three big platforms shipped ways to hold business meaning for agents. They gave three different answers to last week's open question: who picks the winning definition?

1. **A machine now picks the winner, by default.** On August 6, Databricks turned on a feature that ranks competing definitions by source, use, and freshness.
2. **Pharma's own platform made meaning an expert's writing job.** Veeva's new release asks subject matter experts to describe fields in business terms, because its agents were reading raw field names.
3. **Demand is still silent.** No buyer, no funding, no measured fix.

**Since last week**
- **Strengthened:** upkeep. From a vendor saying it's hard to three platforms shipping a place for it.
- **Changed:** vendors now answer "who picks the winner?" in their products, differently.
- **Faded:** definition-layer funding, and the medical-inquiry thread.

## Signal 1: Platforms start ruling on definitions themselves

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: Databricks turned on its business-meaning feature, which it calls an ontology, by default. It weighs competing definitions. Palantir shipped definitions kept as versioned code. Inferred: picking the winning definition is moving into software.
- **Unit of analysis.** Two cross-industry data platforms and their agent features. Not pharma. No customer named.
- **Evidence.**
  - Databricks, August 6: "Genie Ontology enabled by default (Public Preview): The ontology is a unified context layer that gives Genie a business-aware map of your organization" ([Databricks release notes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/databricks/ai-bi/release-notes/2026)). Observed.
  - How it judges, from the June launch post: it "weighs where a definition came from, the relative authority of that source's author, how often people rely on it, how closely it ties to certified and widely-used assets, and how fresh it is" ([Databricks blog, June 16](https://www.databricks.com/blog/introducing-genie-one-genie-ontology-and-genie-agents)). Vendor description, before the window. It doesn't say how a conflict gets overridden. Its accuracy claim, 84.5% against 52.4%, comes from its own 28-question test.
  - Palantir, August 4: "Your code definitions are the source of truth," in a bundle that is "cryptographically signed" ([Palantir announcements](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/announcements/2026-08)). Observed. Here a person approving a code change decides.
  - Independent: an August 7 analyst report says agents need "information that is authoritative, contextualized, permitted for use and traceable through the workflow" ([Verdantix, August 7](https://www.verdantix.com/vantage/report/market-trends--data-readiness-for-ai-agents)). Summary page only.
  - Counterevidence: SAP frames the agent problem as control, not meaning. Agents spread "faster than the enterprise can inventory them, assign ownership, control permissions" ([SAP News, August 3](https://news.sap.com/2026/08/agent-sprawl-why-ai-governance-is-now-board-level-issue/)).
- **Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Two independent, observed product changes, one a default setting. No customer result.
- **Obvious explanation.** Platforms turning on this year's "context" label to drive use.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Affected:** "Organizations keep adding technology to a coordination and meaning problem." Also his Context Continuity territory: carrying context forward "without gaining invisible authority." Secondary: Operating Model.
- **Direction: COMPLICATES.** Last week, settling a definition conflict looked like a people job no tool touched. Now one platform settles it by ranking, by default.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Databricks, Palantir, and Veeva (Signal 2) each shipped a meaning layer for agents within eight days, with three judges: a formula, a code reviewer, an expert. That split is the finding.
- Adds nothing new: the AIwire repeat.
- Status: Strengthening on supply. Flat on proof.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**

**KEY INSIGHT: Popularity is not authority.**
- Databricks weighs "how often people rely on it." In pharma commercial, the most-used "active prescriber" may be the field team's version, the one finance would reject. A ranking can make the loudest team's definition the agent's truth without anyone deciding. Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: The purchase now sets who owns meaning.**
- The judge depends on which platform a company bought. No business leader chose it. That's "the pieces have owners, the connections often do not," built into the tools. Inferred.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else:** cost and speed. Meaning may be a side effect.
- **Outside this lens:** weak testing or poor data explain wrong answers too.
- **Counterevidence:** no customer, no traced failure, no pharma use.
- **Wrong if:** customers report the ranking picks right without human override, or teams certify one definition and the ranking never matters.

**TRIGGER**
- A practitioner describes a ranked definition that was wrong, how they found it, and who fixed it. Or a vendor adds a sign-off step before a definition wins.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value:** "If your tool picks between two definitions by how often each is used, whose version wins? Would finance agree?"
- **Deeper asset:** not yet.
- **Learning question:** do commercial analytics leaders know their platform ranks definitions, and would they want to override it?
- **Long-term value:** if machine ranking becomes normal, auditing what won and why becomes the scarce skill, first as a review, then as ongoing advisory.

**DECISION**
- **What changed:** software now answers "who picks the winner," differently per vendor.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE.** Unchanged.
- **Why:** closest to his promise and now in shipped products, but no buyer has spoken.
- **What moves it next:** one practitioner describing a wrong ranked definition moves it to TALK.

## Signal 2: Pharma's biggest platform made meaning an expert's writing job

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: Veeva's 26R2 release adds a layer where experts describe what fields and metrics mean, so agents stop reading raw field names. General release came July 31 and August 7. Inferred: the dominant life sciences platform now treats meaning as something people must write and keep up.
- **Unit of analysis.** Veeva Vault, used for regulatory, quality, and clinical content. Not its customer relationship management product. Not commercial.
- **Evidence.**
  - Veeva's own before-and-after: "Previously, agents reasoned primarily over physical component names, such as `product__v` and `start_date__v`, which lack the business context, synonyms, and descriptive detail needed for accurate query generation" ([Veeva, What's New in 26R2](https://rn.veevavault.help/en/gr/whats-new-in-26r2/)). Observed.
  - Same page: "Subject matter experts can now describe the meaning of objects, document types, fields, relationships, metrics, and picklists in business terms." It needs setup. It isn't on automatically.
  - Dates: "July 31, 2026," then "August 7, 2026" for general release ([Veeva, About the 26R2 Release](https://rn.veevavault.help/en/gr/about-the-26r2-release/)).
  - One doubt: Veeva's developer notes don't list these parts; the general notes do.
- **Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** Observed release, plain admission of the gap. One vendor, outside commercial, no adoption data.
- **Obvious explanation.** A fix for chat accuracy.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Affected:** "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency." Also Continuity of Meaning. Secondary: Operating Model.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** The main pharma platform says its agents lacked business context and ships a place for experts to supply it. First pharma platform evidence in this pillar in weeks.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Fifth week the upkeep point appears, now from a pharma vendor.
- Contradicting: none. Nothing shows a pharma team has filled it in.
- Status: Repeating, getting clearer. Commercial still absent.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**

**KEY INSIGHT: A setup field is a new, unnamed job.**
- Someone must now write and keep up a meaning for every field agents touch. Veeva built the place, not the owner or the budget. Last week's "budgets fund the build, nobody funds upkeep" now has a concrete home inside pharma. Inferred.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else:** old data dictionaries, repackaged for agents.
- **Outside this lens:** failures may come from weak query tools, not missing meaning.
- **Wrong if:** customers leave the layer empty and agents work fine, or it never reaches commercial products.

**TRIGGER**
- The same layer appears in Veeva's commercial products, or a pharma team names who fills it in.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value:** "Your agent reads `start_date`. Start of what: the contract, the program, or the patient? Who wrote that down?"
- **Deeper asset:** not yet.
- **Learning question:** inside a pharma company, who would be asked to describe these fields, and do they have time?
- **Long-term value:** if this becomes routine setup, checking what's missing or stale in it becomes a repeatable diagnostic.

**DECISION**
- **What changed:** meaning-for-agents moved inside the platform pharma already runs.
- **Recommended state: MONITOR.**
- **Why:** pharma-specific, but not commercial and not yet used.
- **What moves it next:** it reaches commercial products, or an owner is named.
