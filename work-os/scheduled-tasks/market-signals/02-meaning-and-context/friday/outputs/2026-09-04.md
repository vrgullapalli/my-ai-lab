## Signal 1: Fixing a wrong answer now has an address

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: two data platforms tied agents to specific definitions, and one named who fixes them. Inferred: ownership is moving from articles into product designs.
- **Unit of analysis.** Agent designs at Databricks and Snowflake, one integration vendor, one biotech analytics team.
- **Evidence.**
  - Databricks, September 3, healthcare blog: "When an agent returns an incorrect result because an underlying metric was misdefined, the issue doesn't belong to the AI engineering team. Instead, it goes straight to the Data Product Owner, who corrects the catalog definition" ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/governance-beyond-security-knowledge-context-ontology-lakehouse)). Advice, no customer.
  - Snowflake, September 2, generally available: "you can trace the whole path from a table, through the semantic view, to the agent that consumes it" ([Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-09-02-cortex-agent-lineage)). A semantic view is its set of business definitions. Observed.
  - Boomi, September 2: "expert-endorsed business definitions and end-to-end data lineage" ([Boomi](https://via.ritzau.dk/pressemeddelelse/15131008/boomi-delivers-the-critical-infrastructure-that-brings-control-to-enterprise-ai?publisherId=90456&lang=en)). Who endorses: not said. Claimed.
  - Agios Pharmaceuticals, Tellius case study, September 1: the team validated "metric by metric against the reports the business already trusted." Deepanshu Arora, who leads commercial analytics: "AI has skills. What it lacks is context, and that's where you come in" ([Tellius](https://www.tellius.com/resources/blog/how-agios-pharmaceuticals-runs-ai-native-commercial-analytics-at-launch-scale)). Buyer words, vendor page.
  - Counterevidence, Microsoft, updated August 28, preview: an edited description "applies only to the data agent," which then "no longer receives description updates from the source" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/fabric/data-science/data-agent-schema-object-descriptions)).
- **Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** A shipped feature, a design, a launch claim, a buyer practice. Not HIGH: no owner shown catching a stale definition.
- **Obvious explanation.** Platforms selling governance as agent accuracy.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Affected:** "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency." Continuity of Meaning. Secondary: Operating Model.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** One platform names the owner and fix route; another ships the trace an owner needs. Still missing: who reviews a definition nobody has flagged.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Three vendors, three kinds of evidence, plus a buyer. Contradicting: Microsoft. Status: Strengthening, in designs, not yet in operations.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**

**KEY INSIGHT: The fix route decides whether meaning stays shared.**
- Databricks sends a fix up to one owner. Microsoft keeps it inside the agent and cuts that agent off from source updates. Same act, opposite results. Inferred: a brand team tuning its own agent could quietly fork what "new patient" means.

**KEY INSIGHT: Narrow agents are more accurate, and each grows its own meaning.**
- Databricks calls one agent per data product "the single largest accuracy lever available." A DataHub-sponsored survey by BARC, the Business Application Research Center, September 3: "Forty-four percent of respondents manage context within a single agent, team, or platform" ([Business Wire](https://business.times-online.com/times-online/article/bizwire-2026-9-3-barc-study-of-285-organizations-finds-context-leaders-are-four-times-more-likely-to-lead-in-ai)). Inferred: reuse across brands becomes the next problem.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else:** weak evaluation. Owners hear of wrong definitions only if someone checks.
- **Counterevidence:** Microsoft's default. No customer result anywhere.
- **Wrong if:** pharma teams say fixes still go to engineers, or owners exist only on paper.

**TRIGGER**
- A pharma or agency team naming who corrects a definition after an agent gets it wrong. Or a vendor adding a review date per definition.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value:** "When your AI gives a wrong number, who fixes the definition, and does the fix reach every tool using it?"
- **Deeper asset:** per key measure, a map of owner, fix route, and users. Only after a pharma team confirms the gap.
- **Learning question:** when a pharma analyst corrects an AI answer, does the fix go to the source, the agent, or nowhere?
- **Long-term value:** question, then fix-route evidence, then a diagnostic of where meaning forks, then advisory as agents multiply.

**DECISION**
- **What changed:** ownership of meaning is in platform designs, not only articles.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE.** Held.
- **Why:** MEDIUM evidence, no pharma operating evidence.
- **What moves it next:** one pharma leader describing where fixes go moves it to TALK.

## Signal 2: Context that goes stale or learns the wrong thing

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: a pharma commercial chief said a tool learned wrong; a knowledge product shipped expiry dates; a survey named staleness. Inferred: last week's "correct but outdated meaning" is showing up in practice.
- **Unit of analysis.** One pharma company's commercial tool, one knowledge product, one survey.
- **Evidence.**
  - Shae Maughmer, Chief Commercial Officer, Lundbeck, at an EVERSANA and Google event, reported September 4: "these agents, they're not always set it and forget it. And we've actually had a situation which, as the tool learned, it learned the wrong things" ([pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/sales-marketing/one-year-later-eversana-plans-go-beyond-ai-agency)). EVERSANA owns pharmaphorum, the page says. What was learned: unknown.
  - Stack Overflow, September 3: "Stale content leads to agent mistakes and human confusion." Announcements can now carry "a scheduled expiration date" that keeps them out of "agent retrieval contexts" ([Stack Overflow blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/09/03/security-control-and-accessibility-si-2026-6)). Observed. Announcements only.
  - The BARC survey names "the ongoing difficulty of keeping context current as source data and business conditions change." Sponsored.
  - Contrast, Coworker.ai, September 3: a memory layer called "current and correct" by its customer, with nothing on how old facts are handled ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/coworker-ai-om2-organizational-memory-token-costs)). Claimed.
- **Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** One pharma sentence without detail, one narrow feature, one sponsored survey.
- **Obvious explanation.** AI tools drift. Nothing special about meaning.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Affected:** Continuity of Meaning; Context Continuity and High-Context AI. Secondary: Trust and Control.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** Last week this was our inference; now a pharma commercial leader says it in public. Weakly: the wrong thing could be data, a rule, or tone.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Last week's memory releases now meet their failure case. Three kinds of evidence, none contradicting. Status: Emerging.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**

**KEY INSIGHT: Expiry dates fit notices, not meaning.**
- A notice has a known end. A definition like "active prescriber" goes stale when strategy shifts, with no date attached. Inferred: the first staleness tools solve the easy case. The hard case needs an owner, which is Signal 1's question applied to memory.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else:** workflow design, bad user feedback, or model performance.
- **Counterevidence:** on August 25, Lundbeck announced it would broaden its use of EVERSANA's platform across its United States commercial organization ([Lundbeck](https://www.lundbeck.com/us/newsroom/2026/lundbeck-and-eversana-announce-strategic-partnership)). Background. Whether that is the tool that learned wrong: unknown.
- **Wrong if:** the wrong learning was format or style, not business meaning.

**TRIGGER**
- A pharma team saying what a tool learned wrong and how it was caught. Or a memory product showing a learned date and owner per item.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value:** "What has your AI tool learned since launch, and who checked it?"
- **Learning question:** when a pharma AI tool learns wrong, is it caught from the numbers or from a complaint?
- **Deeper asset, long-term value:** not yet.

**DECISION**
- **What changed:** learned-wrong context is a reported pharma experience.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE.** Up from MONITOR.
- **Why:** one public pharma statement, cheap to follow up.
- **What moves it next:** detail on one case moves it to TALK. Two quiet weeks move it back.

**CHALLENGE, TRIGGER, VALUE**
