## Signal 1: Definitions get version history, tests, and a way to travel

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: two platforms posted five releases in three days that treat definitions like software. Inferred: this is becoming standard plumbing.
- **Unit of analysis.** Definition tools at Snowflake and Google. Not pharma.
- **Evidence.**
  - Snowflake, August 26, preview: "Git-backed version control for collaboration and change tracking" and "Request-ID debugging to trace an incorrect answer and apply the fix as an inline diff" ([Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-08-26-semantic-studio-preview)). ID means identifier. Observed.
  - Snowflake, August 28, urging a move to its agent product: "Your semantic views carry over unchanged" ([Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-08-28-cortex-analyst-transition-cortex-agents)). Observed.
  - Google Looker, August 28: "verified queries, also known as golden queries, are now generally available." Plus a test that "verifies whether changes in your dbt models will cause SQL errors in your Looker Explores," where dbt is a data modeling tool and SQL is Structured Query Language ([Looker release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/looker/docs/release-notes)). Observed.
  - Google Knowledge Catalog, August 26, preview: imports "semantic (MetricFlow)" and "lineage metadata from dbt Core" ([Knowledge Catalog release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/dataplex/docs/release-notes)). Missed midweek. Observed.
  - Counterevidence: Databricks, still no changelog.
- **Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Two independent companies, shipped releases. Up from LOW. No customer or result.
- **Obvious explanation.** Vendors catching up to what data teams wanted.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Affected:** "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability before more automation raises the cost of inconsistency." Continuity of Meaning. Secondary: Trust and Control.
- **Direction: COMPLICATES.** Repeatability and provenance are becoming features you buy. Ownership, and knowing when a definition stops fitting, appear in none of these releases.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Reinforcing: Snowflake and Google, different routes. Contradicting: last week's Databricks local definitions. Adds nothing: Snowflake's multi-value tags. Status: Strengthening.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**

**KEY INSIGHT: These controls test that meaning is consistent, not that it is still right.**
- Looker's test catches a change that breaks a query. A golden query locks in yesterday's right answer. Neither asks if the definition fits today's question. Inferred.
- Paul Wachtler, Senior Vice President of AI, Data and Strategy at Exiger, InformationWeek, August 28: "The data can be correct. The definition can be correct. The answer can still be wrong." And: "A definition approved at the start of a quarter may no longer fit after the forecast changes or leadership shifts the decision it is trying to make" ([InformationWeek](https://www.informationweek.com/machine-learning-ai/why-ai-analysts-give-confident-answers-to-the-wrong-questions)). A vendor executive's view, not a result.
- Background, Tellius, a pharma analytics vendor, June 16: once a share measure is locked, "every future run inherits it" ([Tellius](https://www.tellius.com/resources/blog/ai-agents-for-pharma-commercial-teams-the-2026-field-guide-to-use-cases-vendors-and-whats-actually-real)). That is also how a stale definition travels. Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: A definition that moves keeps its text and loses its reasons.**
- "Carry over unchanged" is true of the text. Who approved it, and what it left out on purpose, was never in the file. Inferred.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else:** weak evaluation. Tracing needs someone checking answers.
- **Counterevidence:** golden queries capture some judgment. **Wrong if:** users report fewer disputed numbers without adding an owner.

**TRIGGER**
- A pharma commercial team describing a versioned, correct definition applied to the wrong period or decision. Or a vendor adding an owner and review date per definition.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value:** "When did someone last confirm this definition still fits the decision it feeds? Who?"
- **Deeper asset:** a list of key measures with owner, review date, and the decision served. Only if a pharma team confirms the gap.
- **Learning question:** when brand strategy shifts mid-year, does anyone revisit how key measures are defined, or only the targets?
- **Long-term value:** as tools absorb version control, his lasting ground is judgment about when meaning must change.

**DECISION**
- **What changed:** consistent definitions are becoming a platform feature.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE.** Up from MONITOR.
- **Why:** MEDIUM evidence, and it changes what counts as rare.
- **What moves it next:** one pharma leader confirming a stale definition moves it to TALK.

## Signal 2: Business rules become tools the agent must call

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: two vendors on August 27 made business logic something an agent calls, not reads. Inferred: rules are leaving prompts.
- **Unit of analysis.** A customer relationship management platform and a data platform.
- **Evidence.**
  - Salesforce and Anthropic, "Claudeforce," August 27. It "routes actions through Salesforce to help ensure business rules are always enforced when an action is taken." Chief executive Marc Benioff: "Probabilistic intelligence alone doesn't run a company, and deterministic systems don't reason" ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/ap/news/press-releases/2026/08/27/sg-salesforce-and-anthropic-announce-claudeforce-the-1-ai-meets-the-1-ai-crm/)). Pilots only, no life sciences mention. Claimed.
  - Databricks, August 27: "Agent authors can now attach both scalar and table-valued Unity Catalog functions as tools for a Genie Agent" ([Databricks release notes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/databricks/ai-bi/release-notes/2026)). Observed.
  - CTO Magazine (CTO means chief technology officer), August 25, advises "Wrapping common business logic into predefined metric tools" ([CTO Magazine](https://ctomagazine.com/semantic-layer-ai-agents-iceberg-mcp/)). Advice.
- **Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** One claim, one feature, one article.
- **Obvious explanation.** Vendors protecting their place by owning the rules.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Affected:** Human-Governed Agentic Operating Systems, and his probabilistic-versus-deterministic principle. Secondary: Trust and Control.
- **Direction: COMPLICATES.** His split is now a sales line. But enforcing a rule says nothing about whether the model picked the right one.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Two vendors, one day. Too early to tell.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**

**KEY INSIGHT: Enforcing a rule and interpreting it are two jobs.**
- Deciding which rule applies still sits with the model. Wachtler: "The contextual layer tells the system whether those definitions apply to the question being asked." Inferred link.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else:** positioning. **Wrong if:** pilot errors come mostly from bad data, not wrong rule choice.

**TRIGGER**
- A named life sciences pilot, or a case where an enforced rule blocked a wrong action.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value:** "Which business rules does the system enforce, and which does it only describe to the AI?" Deeper asset, learning question, long-term value: not yet.

**DECISION**
- **What changed:** a large vendor now says his principle out loud.
- **Recommended state: MONITOR.** Moves with a life sciences customer named in a pilot.
