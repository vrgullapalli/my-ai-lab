## Signal 1: Wrong agent answers traced to missing business meaning

**SIGNAL**
- What happened: vendors and one survey name the same cause for wrong agent answers: the agent does not know what the company's words mean. The fix offered is a governed layer of approved definitions, sources, and rules. Observed: the survey and posts. Inferred: a shift in how the market explains failure.
- Unit of analysis: enterprise data and AI vendors, and a small cross-industry survey. Not pharma.
- Evidence:
  - Background, July 10: "57% of enterprises traced a confident but wrong AI agent answer to missing or inconsistent business context," from a survey of 101 companies. Only "Twenty-five percent of respondents run one in production" ([VentureBeat, July 10](https://venturebeat.com/data/57-of-enterprises-have-watched-ai-agents-be-confidently-wrong-the-fix-is-an-agentic-context-layer-but-who-has-one)).
  - "It fails because the agent doesn't understand the business it's acting in" ([Neo4j, July 20](https://neo4j.com/blog/agentic-ai/enterprise-knowledge-layer/)). Vendor claim.
  - Agents must know "which definitions are approved, which sources are trusted" ([OvalEdge, July 20](https://www.ovaledge.com/blog/enterprise-ontology-platform)). Vendor claim.
  - An ontology tells an agent "whether 'Customer' means a lead or a purchaser," with a self-reported 38% accuracy gain ([Atlan, July 22](https://atlan.com/know/ai-agent/ontology/ontology-design-for-ai/)). Vendor claim.
  - The three vendor posts all sell the fix. Not independent.
- Evidence strength: **MEDIUM. EARLY INDICATOR.** One survey gives the problem weight. No buyer outcome, no named failure, no independent result.
- Obvious explanation: vendors are marketing into agent budgets.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- Affected: "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy." Also his Context Continuity and High-Context AI territory. Secondary: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** More than half of surveyed companies saw a confident wrong answer traced to meaning. That supports "access is not understanding." It does not show the fix works.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Survey and vendors agree, but that is one survey plus repeated talk. Stable, not accelerating, as far as I could check.
- Nothing contradicts it this week. The survey's own number, a quarter in production, says the fix is still rare.

**KEY INSIGHT**
- The survey says retrieval over documents is the most common way agents get context, at 38%. Documents rarely say which definition should win. So the most common setup hands the agent many answers and no rule for choosing. More documents can make it worse. The missing piece is authority, not access.

**CHALLENGE**
- What else: bad data, weak testing, or unreliable models can produce the same wrong answers. A survey framed around "context" invites a context answer.
- Counterevidence: none direct. Only the thin adoption figure.
- Wrong if: companies with a governed layer show the same error rate as those without.

**TRIGGER**
- A named company, ideally in life sciences, traces an agent error to one definition and reports a measured fix.

**VALUE**
- Fast value: "Pick one word your AI uses, like 'customer' or 'share.' Where is the approved definition written down, and does the AI read it?"
- Deeper asset: not yet. The need is plausible, but no buyer evidence this week.
- Learning question: when pharma commercial agents give wrong answers, how often is the cause a definition versus bad data?
- Long-term value: if named failures keep tracing to definitions, his "same data, different answers" work fits agent programs, not just reporting. Evidence first, then a test, then a stronger diagnostic.

**DECISION**
- What changed: we now have a number for how often wrong answers trace to meaning, not just a belief.
- State: **INVESTIGATE.**
- Why: it sits in the middle of his promise, and real cases now matter more than more reading.
- What moves it: one independent, named case with a measured fix moves it to TALK.

## Signal 2: Shared definitions kept up as ongoing work

**SIGNAL**
- What happened: a pharma industry group shipped a new version of a shared product-definition set, driven by expert change requests. Separately, an open standard for metric definitions moved to a neutral home. Observed.
- Unit of analysis: pharma regulatory product data, and cross-industry metric definitions. Not commercial customer data.
- Evidence:
  - Pistoia Alliance released version 1.7 of its Identification of Medicinal Products Ontology on July 22, "a shared digital language for describing medicinal products consistently across systems, companies and jurisdictions." It answers "change requests raised by the group of experts" ([Pistoia Alliance, July 22](https://pistoiaalliance.org/news/pistoia-alliance-releases-version-1-7-of-the-idmp-ontology/)).
  - Background: the Open Semantic Interchange was accepted into the Apache Incubator as Apache Ossie ([Apache Ossie, July 10](https://ossie.apache.org/updates/)), and Solid joined it ([Solid release, July 13](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/solid-joins-snowflake-and-industry-leaders-to-advance-open-standards-for-ai-ready-semantic-context-302834217.html)).
- Evidence strength: **LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- Obvious explanation: regulators require the product data, and standards groups ship on a schedule.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- Affected: his Continuity of Meaning territory, and the need hypothesis "Teams need shared definitions, ownership, provenance, and repeatability." Secondary: Operating Model.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS**, narrowly. Continuity of Meaning gains a real example of meaning kept current through change. The commercial need hypothesis is unchanged.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Too early to tell. Two efforts, two domains, no commercial case.

**KEY INSIGHT**
- The cost of a shared definition is not building it. It is keeping it current. Version 1.7 exists because experts kept filing changes. Any company that builds a definitions layer for AI takes on that same ongoing job, and most budgets only fund the build.

**CHALLENGE**
- What else: this is compliance work that would happen with or without AI.
- Counterevidence: nothing ties the release to AI use.
- Wrong if: commercial teams never cite shared definitions as a blocker.

**TRIGGER**
- A pharma company or agency announces shared commercial definitions, such as "active prescriber," across brands or vendors.

**VALUE**
- Fast value: "Who files a change request when a definition changes, and how does the AI hear about it?"
- Deeper asset: not yet.
- Learning question: does anyone in pharma commercial own definition upkeep as a named job?
- Long-term value: if upkeep becomes a named job, it sits right where the pieces have owners and the connections do not.

**DECISION**
- What changed: nothing about commercial demand. We saw what upkeep looks like.
- State: **MONITOR.**
- Why: real, but regulatory and single.
- What moves it: a commercial-side example.

## Signal 3: A vendor sells "clinical context" as the product

**SIGNAL**
- What happened: a pharma marketing vendor launched a model sold on its context (an identity graph and intent signals), not the model itself. Claimed.
- Unit of analysis: pharma commercial AI vendors. One company.
- Evidence:
  - The model "reasons over deterministic clinical context," an identity graph "of more than six million verified HCPs" (health care providers) ([Doceree release, July 14](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/doceree-unveils-semmelweis-the-first-ai-model-purpose-built-for-pharma-commercialization-302825341.html)).
  - Coverage on July 21 gave no outcome data and no outside voices ([Pharmaceutical Commerce, July 21](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/inside-semmelweis-docerees-ai-bet-on-pharma-marketing)). Same event.
  - Background: "TRx share or NRx share?" (total or new prescriptions) "changing any one of them between runs moves the answer while the data sits still" ([Tellius, June 16](https://www.tellius.com/resources/blog/ai-agents-for-pharma-commercial-teams-the-2026-field-guide-to-use-cases-vendors-and-whats-actually-real)).
- Evidence strength: **LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- Obvious explanation: a launch with a big claim.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- Affected: "Local definitions, tagging, mappings, scripts, and ownership choices make the same data support different answers." Secondary: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** If the meaning lives in the vendor's graph, the pharma company may act on definitions it cannot see.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet. One vendor.

**KEY INSIGHT**
- "About to prescribe" is a definition. Someone chose the signals and cutoffs behind it. When that choice lives inside a vendor, the brand team inherits a meaning it never agreed to, and a second vendor may define it differently. That is the "our data does not match" problem, moved into AI.

**CHALLENGE**
- What else: a new label on targeting data already sold.
- Counterevidence: no buyer has said anything.
- Wrong if: buyers never ask whose definitions the model uses.

**TRIGGER**
- A second pharma commercial vendor sells its own context layer, or a buyer reports a result.

**VALUE**
- Fast value: "Whose definition of 'about to prescribe' is this, and can we see it?"
- Deeper asset: not yet.
- Learning question: do brand teams ask vendors for their definitions, or accept the output?
- Long-term value: too early.

**DECISION**
- What changed: a clearer view of how context ownership could leave the company.
- State: **MONITOR.**
- Why: one vendor, no buyer.
- What moves it: a second vendor with the same claim.
