## Signal 2: Context failures are common, and mostly invisible without definitions

**SIGNAL**
- **What happened.** Observed: a second survey wave puts traced context failures at 68%, up from 57%. Firms with a definition layer report more of them.
- **Unit of analysis.** Cross-industry firms with deployed agents. Healthcare and life sciences are 14% of 101 respondents.
- **Evidence.**
  - VentureBeat, August 12: "Sixty-eight percent have traced a confident but wrong agent answer to missing or inconsistent business context in the past six months." Firms with a layer report "recurring context failures at 50%, while those without one report them at 21%." Its reading: "A low reported context-failure rate is not evidence of a healthy context layer. It is at least as likely to be evidence that nobody is looking" ([VentureBeat, August 12](https://venturebeat.com/resources/agent-context-layers-enterprises-governing-their-ai-data-are-catching-twice-as-many-bad-answers-as-the-ones-who-arent)). Self-reported. No sponsor disclosed.
  - Earlier wave, July 23: 57%, and only 25% had a definition layer in production ([VentureBeat, July 23](https://venturebeat.com/resources/the-ai-context-gap-enterprise-ai-organizations-have-a-trust-problem-not-a-retrieval-problem-and-most-are-still-building-the-fix)). Before the window. The comparison is mine.
- **Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Named survey, stated method. Small, one outlet.
- **Obvious explanation.** An outlet building a "context layer" story across waves.

**HYPOTHESIS UPDATE**
- **Affected:** "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy." Also the need for "a fast, bounded way to see what their current foundation can actually support." Secondary: Trust and Control.
- **Direction: STRENGTHENS.** Context failures look common. A firm reporting few may lack the definitions to trace them.

**PATTERN BASIS**
- Two waves, same outlet, same size. Not a trend yet. No named firm, no failure described.
- Status: Repeating.

**NON-OBVIOUS ANALYSIS**

**KEY INSIGHT: A clean report can be a warning sign.**
- A leader hearing "few agent errors" should ask how errors get labeled. Without definitions, a meaning failure gets logged as a model problem or user error. Inferred, following the survey.

**CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION**
- **What else:** firms that built a layer may just answer more carefully.
- **Outside this lens:** stale data or weak retrieval, both Foundation problems.
- **Wrong if:** a later wave shows firms with layers reporting fewer failures over time.

**TRIGGER**
- A named firm describes one traced failure: which definition, what the agent said, who caught it. Or the survey splits out healthcare and life sciences.

**VALUE**
- **Fast value:** "When your agent is wrong, what box does it get logged under? Is 'wrong definition' an option?"
- **Deeper asset:** not yet.
- **Learning question:** in pharma commercial, how do teams label agent errors today?
- **Long-term value:** an error-labeling check could become the first step of his diagnostic.

**DECISION**
- **What changed:** a second measurement, and a sharper point: low failure counts can mean nobody's looking.
- **Recommended state: MONITOR.**
- **Why:** useful framing, but cross-industry and self-reported.
- **What moves it next:** a pharma breakout, or one named failure.
