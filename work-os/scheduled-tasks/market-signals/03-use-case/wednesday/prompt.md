YOUR JOB TODAY: the Use Case Market Signals Wednesday Signal Check

PURPOSE

Review the Use Case Market Signals daily briefings from the current week so far and identify anything that may be developing into a meaningful pattern before the Friday synthesis.

Do not repeat the daily briefs. Do not produce a full weekly synthesis.

The goal is to answer: what may be emerging fast enough that Venkat should notice, investigate, or respond to before Friday about how AI and customer-data capabilities are actually being used?

Use Case asks: what specific work, decision, behavior, or outcome is the AI or customer-data capability supposed to improve, for whom, and under what conditions?

Having AI, data, models, agents, or a platform does not establish a use case. A real use case connects the capability to a specific problem or need, a real user or actor, a job to be done, a decision or piece of work that changes, a clear workflow and trigger, an output someone can act on, a defined role for AI and for people, clear constraints, exceptions, and boundaries, a measurable change in work, behavior, customer experience, or business outcome, and evidence that the capability is useful enough to become part of real work.

Focus on signals involving: new AI or customer-data use cases entering real workflows; movement from generic capabilities toward specific jobs; pilots moving into operational use; pilots being stopped, narrowed, or redesigned; changes in how people perform specific work; new decisions being augmented, automated, or delegated; workflow redesign around AI or customer intelligence; new triggers for when AI or data is used; changes in the role of humans within a workflow; measurable productivity, quality, speed, revenue, customer, or decision outcomes; user adoption or rejection; workarounds indicating unmet needs; use cases receiving budget or executive attention; use cases losing investment because value is unclear; narrow use cases outperforming broad platform ambitions; unexpected or adjacent use cases emerging from real-world adoption; evidence that a use case works only under certain conditions; changes in what buyers expect AI or customer data to actually accomplish.

Do not treat a signal as Use Case merely because a company launches AI, deploys an agent, buys a platform, or announces an AI strategy. The signal must tell us something meaningful about the actual work, decision, behavior, or outcome being changed.

1. SCAN THE WEEK SO FAR

Review the individual Use Case signals captured since the previous Friday. Look for:
- multiple signals pointing toward the same underlying use-case change
- a strong new signal that materially changes an existing belief
- acceleration in an existing pattern
- meaningful counterevidence
- movement from experimentation into real workflow use
- movement from broad AI claims toward narrower, specific jobs
- specific use cases receiving more or less investment
- evidence of actual user adoption or rejection
- changes in workflow, decision-making, or human roles
- measurable outcomes emerging
- repeated failure of certain use cases
- use cases being narrowed because broad ambitions did not work
- new customer, healthcare professional, patient, field, marketing, analytics, medical, or commercial workflows using AI or customer data
- workarounds revealing jobs that current products do not solve
- changes in buyer expectations about useful AI
- something unusually time-sensitive
Do not treat repeated coverage of the same event as independent evidence.

2. SURFACE ONLY THE STRONGEST EMERGING PATTERNS

Return no more than 3 emerging patterns. Fewer is acceptable. For each:

EMERGING SIGNAL
- What appears to be changing? Describe the underlying Use Case change, not the individual headlines.
- Evidence so far: the specific observations supporting it.
- Unit of analysis: what exactly are we making a claim about? For example a specific company, pharma companies, a buyer or user, a commercial workflow, a marketing workflow, a field workflow, an analytics workflow, a medical workflow, a customer interaction, a decision, a job to be done, an AI agent use case, a customer-data use case, or a market or vendor category. Do not generalize beyond the evidence.

3. CONNECT IT TO A HYPOTHESIS

Hypothesis affected: what existing Use Case belief, problem hypothesis, need hypothesis, audience hypothesis, business opportunity, or intellectual territory does this evidence affect?

The primary capability lens must be Use Case. When useful, identify a secondary dependency involving Foundation, Meaning and Context, Operating Model, Trust and Control, or Repeatability and Scale. Do not move the signal into another lens simply because another condition is affected.

Ask: what does this evidence change about which work, decisions, behaviors, or outcomes AI and customer data can actually improve?

Direction: STRENGTHENS, WEAKENS, COMPLICATES, or TOO EARLY TO TELL.

4. JUDGE THE EVIDENCE

Evidence strength: LOW, MEDIUM, or HIGH. Then whether this is primarily an EARLY INDICATOR, a REINFORCING INDICATOR, or CONFIRMING EVIDENCE. Briefly explain why.

Give greater weight to observable changes such as:
- real workflow deployment
- repeated user adoption
- budget allocation
- use-case expansion
- measurable outcomes
- workflow redesign
- changed decision-making
- use cases moving into production
- pilots being narrowed or stopped
- users creating workarounds
- documented failure or abandonment
Give less weight to:
- demonstrations
- generic case studies
- vendor use-case lists
- broad claims about productivity
- announced pilots with no evidence of actual use

5. CHECK WHETHER IT IS ACTUALLY A PATTERN

Pattern basis. Identify: which Use Case signals reinforce one another; whether different types of evidence point toward the same underlying change; which signals contradict the interpretation; which observations are probably duplicates or noise; whether the same use case or underlying need appears across multiple companies, users, or workflows.

Do not turn one pilot, demonstration, case study, or product announcement into a market trend.

If the evidence does not yet support a pattern, say: 'Interesting signal. No pattern yet.'

6. CHALLENGE THE INTERPRETATION

Most obvious explanation. What is the simplest interpretation?
What else could explain this? The strongest plausible alternative.
What would make us wrong? What evidence would materially weaken the current interpretation?

Also check whether an apparent Use Case problem is actually caused by something outside this lens, such as:
- poor data or infrastructure
- missing context
- unclear ownership
- weak trust or controls
- poor implementation
- organizational resistance
- inability to scale
Do not conclude that a use case is bad simply because its implementation failed. Likewise, do not conclude that a use case is valuable simply because the technology worked.
Keep this short.

7. DEFINE WHAT TO WATCH NEXT

Next evidence: the specific observable evidence that would strengthen or weaken this signal before Friday. Be concrete. For example:
- another pharma company deploys the same use case
- a pilot moves into normal workflow
- users repeatedly adopt the capability without being forced
- budgets shift toward the use case
- measurable outcomes appear
- human roles or decision rights change
- the use case expands to additional teams
- companies narrow or abandon the use case
- users create workarounds around existing tools
- buyer language becomes more specific about the job being solved
- vendors redesign products around the emerging workflow
- the same use case produces different results under different conditions
Avoid vague statements such as 'watch for more news'.

8. DECIDE WHETHER ANYTHING REQUIRES ATTENTION BEFORE FRIDAY

Exactly one state per emerging signal: WAIT FOR FRIDAY, MONITOR CLOSELY, INVESTIGATE NOW, TALK NOW, TEST NOW, or ACT NOW. Most should be WAIT FOR FRIDAY unless there is a real reason not to wait. One sentence of reason.

MIDWEEK SIGNAL MAP

End with a compact table: Emerging Pattern, Hypothesis, Direction, Evidence, State, Watch Next.

Then answer: what deserves attention before Friday? List only Use Case signals where waiting until Friday could create a meaningful missed opportunity. If none qualify, say: 'Nothing requires action before Friday. Let the evidence accumulate.'

RULES

Maximum 3 emerging patterns. Every selected signal must materially relate to Use Case. Prefer no signal over a weak signal. Do not create content ideas. Do not recommend assets or products. Do not perform the Friday synthesis early. Do not manufacture trends from isolated events. Separate observable evidence from interpretation. Include counterevidence. Be willing to say 'too early to tell'.
Focus on actual work, decisions, behaviors, workflows, users, and outcomes. Do not treat possessing AI, data, agents, models, or platforms as proof of a use case. Do not treat a pilot as proof of adoption. Do not treat technical success as proof of user or business value. Do not automatically interpret implementation failure as evidence that the use case itself is weak.
Keep it concise, plainspoken, practical, and non-hyped.
