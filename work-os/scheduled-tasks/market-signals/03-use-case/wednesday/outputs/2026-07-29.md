## The short answer

Nothing needs action before Friday. One thing is new, and one thing repeated.

1. **New:** a large health data vendor says top drugmakers are moving AI (artificial intelligence) agents out of pilots and into wider commercial use, on named jobs. It gave no result numbers. Only the seller is saying it.
2. **Repeated:** two more large drugmakers reported half-year results. Neither named a commercial AI job. One named data and technology in research only.
3. **Faded:** the Doceree prescription-lift claim. Nothing new this week. No customer confirmed it and no one outside checked it.

A note on coverage: this was earnings week, so most of the evidence dated July 24 to 29 is company results. Three pages would not open (listed at the end). Three older sources appear as background, each labeled.

## Pattern 1: A vendor says commercial AI agents are leaving the pilot stage, with jobs named and no results shown

**What appears to be changing**
- The claim is moving from "we have AI" to "clients are using it on these jobs, more widely." For the first time in this series, the jobs named are commercial ones: launch planning and company-wide analytics.
- The proof has not moved. No before-and-after number, no measured outcome, and no client named.

**Evidence so far**
- IQVIA's chief executive, Ari Bousbib, on the July 28 call: "we now have 294 agents deployed across 90 use cases," and "19 of the top 20 pharma companies have already deployed IQVIA AI solutions" ([Investing.com transcript, July 28](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-iqvia-tops-q2-2026-estimates-and-raises-outlook-93CH-4817225)). Claimed.
- On commercial clients, he said they are "starting to deploy IQVIA AI agents more broadly." His two examples: "A mid-size pharma client is expanding its use of IQVIA AI across an immunology franchise in 95 countries" with a "launch planning conversational AI agent," and "We are working with a top five large pharma to deliver a complete AI-enabled enterprise analytics solution that seamlessly brings together data technology and advisory support" (same transcript). Claimed. Neither client is named.
- The written release is more careful. It credits "increased adoption of our AI solutions" as one of several drivers of commercial revenue growth. It gives no adoption figure ([IQVIA release, July 28](https://ir.iqvia.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/IQVIA-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)). Observed as a filing. What it claims is the company's view.
- A news summary of the slides adds a caution in its own words: the company's "heavy investment in AI capabilities must continue translating into measurable revenue gains and productivity improvements" ([Investing.com, July 28](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/iqvia-q2-2026-slides-revenue-tops-44b-guidance-raised-on-ai-momentum-93CH-4817321)). That is the reporter's framing, not an IQVIA quote.
- On the call, no result numbers were given for any agent. The transcript names benefits as "speed, precision, and accuracy" with nothing measured behind them.

**Unit of analysis**
- One vendor's account of its own clients' use. The one commercial workflow named in any detail: launch planning at one unnamed mid-size drugmaker. It says nothing about pharma commercial teams in general.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result."
- Primary lens: Use Case. Secondary: Trust and Control. "Deployed" is the vendor's word. Nobody has said what the agent's output changes or who acts on it.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Named commercial jobs spreading to 95 countries counts against "stuck in pilots." No value measure anywhere supports the rest of the hypothesis.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- One seller describing its own customers on an earnings call, where it has every reason to sound upbeat. "Deployed in their workflow" can mean a license switched on. It does not show people using it or decisions changing.

**Pattern basis**
- This extends last week's pattern, "a named job, a claimed payoff, no proof yet." The jobs are now commercial and the scale claim is bigger. The proof gap is the same.
- It reinforces the midweek check's Bristol Myers Squibb and Doceree items only in shape: named task, no number. There is no shared client, workflow, or measure.
- Counterevidence, background, outside the window: a survey of more than 30 drug manufacturers found companies most often tried AI for "predictive sales analytics (45%)," and "Only 8% of pilot projects reached full-scale deployment" ([GxP News, July 15](https://gxpnews.net/en/2026/07/most-pharma-companies-unprepared-for-ai-adoption-survey-finds/)). The sample was 63% Russian companies, so it is weak for United States pharma. It points the other way all the same.
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet on whether commercial use is actually spreading.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: an earnings-call sales pitch. Big numbers, no clients named.
- Strongest alternative: "19 of the top 20" may count any IQVIA AI feature in any function, including clinical trials, where most of the call's detail sat. The commercial share is unknown.
- What would make us wrong: a named drugmaker describing the launch planning or analytics agent in its own words, with a changed decision or a measured result.
- Outside this lens: one read of the transcript has Bousbib describing clients "moving beyond pilots and data foundation work." That suggests the hold-up has been data readiness, not whether the job is worth doing. That is a Foundation question. Inferred.

**Watch next**
- Any drugmaker, not IQVIA, naming a launch planning or analytics AI job in results due before Friday.
- A second commercial data or software vendor giving deployment counts for commercial agents. That would make it a category claim and not one company's.
- Any figure from IQVIA or a client on what the launch planning agent changed: time to plan, markets covered, decisions altered.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** It is a single seller's claim with no time pressure. Friday can weigh it against whatever drugmakers say this week.

## Pattern 2: Drugmakers' own reports still name no commercial AI job

**What appears to be changing**
- Nothing new, which is the point. This week, as last, where a drugmaker's own results mention AI or data at all, it is in research. The commercial side stays silent.
- Set next to Pattern 1, a split is showing: vendors say commercial AI is spreading. The drugmakers they sell to do not say so in public.

**Evidence so far**
- AstraZeneca, half-year results, July 27: the release contains no sentence mentioning AI, data science, machine learning, digital, or technology ([BioSpace copy of the release, July 27](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/astrazeneca-results-h1-and-q2-2026)). Observed. I did not check the call.
- GSK, July 28: the written release does not mention AI, data, or technology, including where its £1.9 billion yearly savings program will come from ([GSK release, July 28](https://www.gsk.com/en-gb/media/press-releases/gsk-delivers-strong-q2-core-results-performance-and-continued-momentum/)). Observed.
- On GSK's call, the head of research and development, Tony Wood, gave the only data line: "Much of this has been achieved through our use of data and technology. It enables faster decision-making with greater confidence" ([Investing.com transcript, July 28](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-gsk-posts-q2-2026-beat-as-shares-rise-335-93CH-4817727)). That is research, not commercial, and it has no measure behind it. Claimed.

**Unit of analysis**
- What two large drugmakers chose to say in their results this week. It is about disclosure, not about what their commercial teams actually run.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Same problem hypothesis as Pattern 1. Primary lens: Use Case. Secondary: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES**, as last Friday. Named AI jobs at drugmakers stay in the lab. Commercial proof comes only from sellers.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Two independent first-hand sources, plus last week's three (Bristol Myers Squibb, Novartis, Roche), all with the same shape. What they prove is narrow: silence in investor material.

**Pattern basis**
- Repeating. Five large drugmakers across two weeks, and none named a commercial AI job with a number.
- Contradicting signal: IQVIA's claim that nearly all top drugmakers have AI in their workflow. The two only conflict if you assume companies disclose what they use. They may not.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: earnings season is about the pipeline. Investors do not ask about sales tools.
- Strongest alternative: commercial AI is ordinary back-office tooling now, and nobody announces the tools they rely on. Background, outside the window: a Pharmaceutical Commerce mid-year piece quoted an agency leader saying "the real challenge for the rest of 2026 isn't whether we use AI. That will increasingly be taken for granted" ([Pharmaceutical Commerce, July 21](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/mid-year-check-in-has-pharmas-commercial-caution-lifted-)).
- What would make us wrong: a drugmaker's chief executive naming a field, marketing, or analytics AI job on a results call this week.

**Watch next**
- The half-year results due before Friday from other large drugmakers: does any name a commercial AI job, and does it come with a number?
- Whether analysts start asking about commercial AI on calls. Buyer and investor interest usually shows up there first.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** More results arrive this week, and Friday is the natural point to count them.
