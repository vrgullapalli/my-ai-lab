## Pattern 1: A drugmaker widens AI across its commercial group before showing a measure

**What appears to be changing**
- Until now, only sellers said AI was moving "beyond pilots." This week a buyer acted on it. Lundbeck spent more after a first phase.
- It widened by function, not by proving one job first.

**Evidence so far**
- Joint release, August 25: "Through the initial work, Lundbeck and EVERSANA introduced AI-driven workflows to accelerate strategic planning, content development and commercial execution while maintaining rigorous medical, legal and regulatory review processes" ([PR Newswire, August 25](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lundbeck-and-eversana-announce-strategic-partnership-to-advance-ai-powered-commercialization-302858538.html)). Observed as an announcement. The release gives no number of any kind.
- The next step covers "marketing, medical communications, market access and media operations, with the companies also exploring additional use cases" ([Pharmaceutical Executive, August 25](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/roundup-mckesson-acquire-precision-medicine-lundbeck-eversana-partnership)). Observed. The jobs beyond the first three are still being worked out.
- Tom Gibbs, president of Lundbeck U.S.: "Our ambition is to bring human expertise and artificial intelligence together to strengthen how we think, decide and execute across our business." And the aim is to "improve the speed and quality of execution" (same release). Claimed goal.
- Fierce Pharma, August 25: Lundbeck is the platform's first publicly named drugmaker partner, a year after launch ([Fierce Pharma, August 25](https://www.fiercepharma.com/marketing/lundbeck-strikes-expanded-pact-eversana-broaden-ai-scope-across-us-commercial-org)). Observed.
- An outside read, August 26, from LucidQuest, a diligence firm: "No deal value, contract duration or quantified Lundbeck-specific productivity target was disclosed." It says EVERSANA's own "50% faster" and "30% lower cost" claims "should currently be treated as platform evidence, not verified Lundbeck-specific performance" ([LucidQuest, August 26](https://www.lqventures.com/lundbeck-eversana-ai-commercialization/)). Independent.
- Repeat coverage, counted once: [Life Science Nordic, August 26](https://www.lifesciencenordic.com/article/view/1242337/lundbeck_enters_into_ai_partnership_in_the_us).

**Unit of analysis**
- One drugmaker's U.S. commercial group and one vendor. Named jobs: planning, content development, commercial execution. Not a claim about drugmakers in general.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result."
- Primary lens: Use Case. Secondary: Operating Model. EVERSANA's innovation chief says "we're not simply introducing AI tools into existing workflows," which is a claim of workflow redesign.
- Territory: Decision Integrity Under Complexity.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** A buyer scaled across four functions with no public measure. It also complicates last week's belief that only sellers speak. A buyer now speaks. It still does not say what it measures.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- A named buyer and a real spending choice sit on top of a five-week pattern. But it is one deal, from one release, and value is not shown.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforces: EVERSANA's own "beyond pilots" line on August 13 and 20, Syneos on August 14, and Axtria's hiring on August 13, all from earlier weeks.
- Same seller three times in two weeks: EVERSANA. Lundbeck is the only new voice.
- Contradicting: the expansion itself suggests Lundbeck saw enough to pay for more. Inferred.
- Noise: five outlets, one release.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: the first phase worked well enough inside Lundbeck, and it does not publish numbers.
- Strongest alternative: naming a client helps the seller, two days before its August 27 event. The deal may be partly marketing.
- Outside this lens: if content gets drafted faster but medical, legal, and regulatory review stays the bottleneck, that is an implementation limit, not a weak use case.
- What would make us wrong: Lundbeck or EVERSANA publishing a before and after for one job, such as days from brief to approved content.

**Watch next**
- EVERSANA's August 27 event: any Lundbeck number tied to one job.
- Which of the four areas goes first, and who at Lundbeck owns it.
- A second drugmaker named for the same platform.

**State: MONITOR CLOSELY.** EVERSANA's event is tomorrow and is the most likely place for the first buyer-backed number in this series.

## Pattern 2: A narrow field job reaches a whole team: AI-written visit notes

**What appears to be changing**
- Veeva's call-note job was marked faded last week. It returns with a scale claim.
- The job: a sales rep speaks notes after a doctor visit. The AI turns them into structured records and runs compliance checks. The notes can flag treatment barriers for someone to fix.

**Evidence so far**
- Veeva's quarterly results, August 26, released at 4:05 p.m. Eastern, late on the brief date: "a top 20 biopharma deployed Vault CRM and the Agentic Call Report across its full U.S. field team in the quarter" ([PR Newswire, August 26](http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/veeva-announces-fiscal-2027-second-quarter-results-302860933.html)). Claimed by the seller. Customer not named. No usage or outcome.
- Same release: "The company also acquired Copli in the quarter and launched Veeva Falcon MLR to automate content reviews." Background, June 23: Veeva says the tool could "eliminate 70% or more of manual MLR labor within five years" ([PR Newswire, June 23](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/veeva-acquires-copli-launches-veeva-falcon-mlr-to-accelerate-content-review-302807241.html)). MLR means medical, legal, and regulatory review. A forecast, no customer named.
- A MarketBeat summary of the results call, August 26, on Veeva's newer AI agents: "Gassner said Veeva is currently the limiting factor because it needs to further prepare the product and work with early adopters" ([Yahoo Finance, August 26](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/veeva-systems-q2-earnings-call-230419426.html)). Observed. Buyer interest is ahead of the product.
- Background, August 10, Veeva's president of commercial strategy: "When field teams use an agentic call report, we see that 65% of compliant, free-text field notes identify actionable treatment barriers not captured by traditional reports" ([Pharmaceutical Executive, August 10](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/generating-commercial-evidence-agentic-field-brand-engagement-accelerate-launch-performance)). Claimed. No method.

**Unit of analysis**
- One unnamed large drugmaker's U.S. field team, and one vendor's product. Reps do the note taking. The value shows up later, with whoever acts on a flagged barrier.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Same problem hypothesis as Pattern 1. Also the series belief that "measures stop where the AI's part ends."
- Primary lens: Use Case. Secondary: Operating Model, because a flagged barrier only matters if someone owns the fix.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** The spread is wider, a whole team. The proof still stops at the note.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- One line in an earnings release. Unnamed customer. No usage rate, no outcome.

**Pattern basis**
- Lundbeck's first AI jobs were planning and content. Veeva's are notes and content review. EVERSANA listed "MLR Automation" among new tools on August 20 ([PR Newswire, August 20](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/eversana-expands-ai-powered-commercialization-platform-with-new-capabilities-across-medical-affairs-market-research-and-commercial-operations-302855424.html)). Three sources point at document jobs. Inferred.
- Only one of the three is a buyer. Interesting signal. No pattern yet.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: the call-note tool came bundled with a switch to Veeva's customer relationship management system. Rolling it out to the whole team may be part of that switch, not a choice about the AI job.
- What would make us wrong: reps not using voice notes, or the customer turning the feature off.

**Watch next**
- The call transcript or follow-up coverage: how many reps actually use voice notes, or the customer's name.
- A drugmaker saying what happened to barriers the notes flagged.
- The first named customer live on Veeva's content review tool.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** One earnings line. More coverage of the call will be out by Friday.
