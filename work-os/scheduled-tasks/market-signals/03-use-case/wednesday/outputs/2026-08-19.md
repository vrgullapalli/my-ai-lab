## Pattern 1: Patient-support AI narrows to one job: catching patients before they drop off

**What appears to be changing**
- Past weeks' narrow jobs sat with reps and marketers. This week the job sits with the people who keep patients on a drug: pharmacies and patient support hubs (the services that help patients get insurance approval and stay on therapy).
- The job: predict who is likely to stop, then reach them at the right moment.

**Evidence so far**
- Adheris (until now called MedAdvisor Solutions), covered by Pharmaceutical Executive on August 14. Its platform "pairs precision segmentation with real-time adaptation of each patient's experience." And: "Adheris points to concrete outcomes from that platform, including returning approximately 65% of patients to therapy and driving more than 15.8 million incremental refills for clients in recent years" ([Pharmaceutical Executive, August 14](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/medadvisor-solutions-rebrands-adheris)). Claimed. No method, baseline, time frame, or client.
- Background, the company's own release two days before the window: it "combines precision segmentation and predictive analytics to identify and act on the moments that matter most in each patient's treatment journey" ([release via PRWeb, August 12](https://www.prweb.com/releases/medadvisor-solutions-is-now-adheris-company-returns-to-its-original-name-and-sharpens-focus-on-innovating-patient-engagement-302849325.html)). Claimed. This reads as a customer-data use case more than an AI one. Inferred.
- Cardinal Health's patient support unit, Sonexus, in a sponsored post on Drug Channels, August 14: "Many clients come looking for ways to embrace AI, which we have increasingly done through functions such as improving call quality and predicting non-adherence" ([Drug Channels, sponsored, August 14](https://www.drugchannels.net/2026/08/hub-transitions-need-proven-plans-smart.html)). Claimed. Its one number, "increased positive patient sentiment by 5% during the handoff," is about switching hub vendors, not about AI.

**Unit of analysis**
- Two sellers of patient-support services to drugmakers. The job belongs to hub and pharmacy staff working for the drugmaker. Not doctors, not reps.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result."
- Primary lens: Use Case. Secondary: Foundation. The job only works if pharmacy fill data and hub records reach the model in time to act.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Last Friday's read was that measures stop where the AI's part ends. Adheris counts refills and patients back on therapy, which is the outcome line. But the seller counts it, over unstated years, with no baseline shown.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- Two sellers. One rebrand story and one sponsored post. Neither names a drugmaker.

**Pattern basis**
- Two separate companies named the same job in the same week. That is the reinforcing part.
- Both are selling. The Adheris coverage repeats its own release, so it is one source, not two.
- A caution on numbers: this is the second "65%" in two weeks. Veeva's 65% counted rep notes that flag a barrier. Adheris's 65% counts patients returned to therapy. Same number, different steps in the chain. They should never be compared.
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: adherence programs are old. Sellers are putting a new label on long-running refill reminder work.
- Strongest alternative: the refill gains may come from reminders and pharmacist outreach, with prediction adding little. Inferred.
- What would make us wrong: a drugmaker saying which patients the model flagged, what the hub did, and how many stayed on therapy compared with patients who were not flagged.
- Outside this lens: if the prediction is right but nobody calls the patient in time, that is an ownership failure, not a bad use case.

**Watch next**
- A drugmaker naming an adherence prediction program, with a before and after.
- Adheris saying how "incremental refills" is measured, and against what baseline.
- A third hub or pharmacy seller naming the same job, or a drugmaker taking it in-house.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Two seller claims will not change by Friday.

## Pattern 2: Sellers now say "beyond pilots." Still no buyer measure.

**What appears to be changing**
- The words shifted. Services firms now describe AI as leaving the pilot stage for "measurable" value. The numbers did not follow.

**Evidence so far**
- Syneos Health podcast, August 14: the company "is moving AI beyond experimentation to support measurable business impact." Its examples: AI "can improve productivity, optimize channel mix and uncover growth opportunities in areas such as rare disease" ([Syneos Health, August 14](https://www.syneoshealth.com/insights-hub/moving-beyond-ai-hype-measurable-commercial-impact)). Claimed. No client, number, or method on the page.
- Pharmaceutical Executive contributed article, August 19: "Most pharma companies have promising AI pilots. Few have built the conditions to scale them." It cites Boston Consulting Group: "AI-first biopharma companies are reporting 5 percent to 15 percent revenue uplift" ([Pharmaceutical Executive, August 19](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/from-pilot-profit-leaders-scaling-ai-across-drug-lifecycle)). Claimed, secondhand. No company named, no commercial example. The author's employer is not shown on the page I read.
- Pharmaceutical Commerce, August 19: an article arguing that AI for drug safety monitoring (pharmacovigilance) is now a commercial and market access need ([Pharmaceutical Commerce, August 19](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/ai-pharmacovigilance-commercial-market-access-imperative)). It names no company using it. Observed.
- Background, August 13, already counted last week: EVERSANA's chief executive said its platform "moves beyond experimentation and delivers measurable business value," with no client or number ([Pharmaceutical Executive, August 13](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/two-decades-of-technology-transformation-in-pharma)).

**Unit of analysis:** public statements by two services firms and two contributed articles. Disclosure, not practice.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Same hypothesis. Primary: Use Case. Secondary: Trust and Control. Territory: Decision Integrity Under Complexity.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS**, narrowly. "Measurable" is now a selling word. In public, the measure is still missing.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Fifth week, same shape. The new items alone are LOW: talk, not results.

**Pattern basis**
- Syneos repeats EVERSANA's line from last week almost word for word. Two services firms, same claim, same missing number.
- Contradicting: Adheris did give a number (Pattern 1). It sits at the outcome level, but it is still the seller's.
- Noise: the consulting figure is an industry estimate passed along, not a commercial result.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: sellers echo what buyers ask for. Buyers want proof, so sellers say "measurable."
- What else could explain it: buyers may hold real measures privately and not let sellers publish them.
- What would make us wrong: one drug company or agency naming its measure for a commercial AI tool, against a baseline.

**Watch next**
- Syneos or EVERSANA naming one client result, with how it was measured.
- A drugmaker talk or job posting that names a commercial AI job and the number it is judged by.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Friday's investigation stands. Nothing here is time-sensitive.
