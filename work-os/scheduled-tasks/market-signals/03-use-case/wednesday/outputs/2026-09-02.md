## Pattern 1: The buyer is asked for a measure and gives a direction

**What appears to be changing**
- Last week a buyer acted. This week a buyer was asked, on the record, how it will know the work paid off.
- The answer was a direction ("accelerate the commercial model"), not a number. It also showed the rollout is gated by internal pilots.

**Evidence so far**
- Shae Maughmer, Lundbeck's senior vice president and chief commercial officer, to Medical Marketing and Media, August 31: the rollout is "stepwise, guided by results from a series of internal pilots run on the platform." Then: "Lundbeck has not disclosed performance data from those pilots, but Maughmer said the results they saw were encouraging enough to warrant the continued expansion" ([MM+M, August 31](https://www.mmm-online.com/news/eversana-expanded-ai-agency-platform-lundbeck-going-all-in/)). Observed.
- Asked how she would judge success a year from now, she "pointed back to Lundbeck's commercial objective rather than a specific metric": "I go back to the North Star in terms of what the objective is: using AI to accelerate the commercial model" (same article). Observed.
- A second buyer voice at EVERSANA's event, Graham Goodrich, chief commercial officer at Apnimed, a biotech: "With AI Agency, Eversana and Google have burned the boats" (same). Praise. No job, no number.
- A contrast, August 31: Johnson & Johnson posted a job in its United States pharmaceutical group to "Lead end-to-end AI product development across Marketing and Field Engagement functions, including ideation, design, development, deployment, and performance measurement" ([J&J careers, posted August 31](https://www.careers.jnj.com/en/jobs/r-089505/manager-ai-next-frontiers)). Observed. A commercial-side role that owns measurement. One posting.

**Unit of analysis**
- One drugmaker's United States commercial group, one vendor, and one job posting at a second drugmaker. Not drugmakers in general.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Problem hypothesis: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result."
- Primary lens: Use Case. Secondary: Operating Model, because whoever owns the measure decides whether the use case can be proven.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** The buyer did not give a public measure, which strengthens the belief. But "guided by results from a series of internal pilots" says measures exist inside. The gap may be about sharing, not a baseline never set. That partly answers Friday's learning question.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- A named executive answering the exact question, in trade press. One company, and the pilot results are still unseen.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforces six weeks of "beyond pilots" with no buyer number, and last week's Lundbeck expansion.
- Veeva's chief executive, on buyers of its research-side agents: "The customers know how to deal with it. They just want a partner that can scale it across multiple areas" ([Veeva call transcript, Motley Fool, published August 31](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/31/veeva-veev-q2-2027-earnings-call-transcript)). A seller's read. It matches Lundbeck buying breadth from a partner.
- Contradicts: the J&J posting suggests one buyer is building an inside owner for measurement.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: drugmakers keep pilot numbers private for competitive and legal reasons.
- Strongest alternative: the pilots measured speed of content work, and speed alone does not justify the spend in public, so the goal is stated instead.
- Outside this lens: if review by medical, legal, and regulatory teams is still the bottleneck, faster drafting may not show up in any commercial number. That is an implementation limit, not a weak use case.
- What would make us wrong: Lundbeck, or any drugmaker, naming one pilot result, such as days from brief to approved content.

**Watch next**
- Any trade interview where Lundbeck names which of the four areas went first and what the pilot measured.
- Who hires into the J&J role, and whether other drugmakers post roles that own AI performance measurement in commercial.
- Whether Apnimed, or another EVERSANA client, says which job it uses the platform for.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Nothing here is time-sensitive, and Friday's planned conversation with a commercial leader tests the same question.

## Pattern 2: A drugmaker publishes numbers, and they count time and use, not outcomes

**What appears to be changing**
- For the first time in this series, a drugmaker put its own AI numbers in public.
- The numbers measure inputs: days saved, cost saved, users, and questions answered. Business outcomes are promised for later.

**Evidence so far**
- Lupin, an India-based drugmaker, August 29, by its chief financial officer and head of information technology: "₹42.8 crore in cost savings and 34,336 man-days reclaimed in FY26 from AI-led initiatives," with "over 25 high-impact AI use cases (from an initial pool of 250+ ideas), now scaled to over 15,000 users" ([Lupin, August 29](https://www.lupin.com/media/perspectives/lupin-s-ai-led-transformation-delivers-428-million-in-savings-reclaims-34-336-man-days-in-fy-26)). Fiscal year 2026 ran April 2025 to March 2026. Company-reported.
- For sales reps, the counts are use, not results: its digital sales platform was "adopted by 11,000+ active users (93%+ adoption)," and its AI assistant "Resolved 12,500+ internal queries at 99.5% resolution" (same). Observed as reported.
- Savings are credited to "the cumulative impact of embedding AI and automation across the organization," not a single initiative (same).
- The named future outcomes are all outside commercial: "further reductions in batch-release turnaround times, shorter supply chain lead times, lower working-capital buffers and stronger global regulatory inspection readiness," over "the next 12 to 24 months" (same).
- Background, from a year earlier: Lupin's annual filing reported "₹400 Mn (₹ 40 Crore) in cost savings and 12,000 man-days reclaimed in FY25," its fiscal year 2025 ([Lupin, August 25, 2025](https://www.lupin.com/media/perspectives/digital-intelligence-accelerates-drug-discovery)). The same page also cites a ₹435 crore figure, so Lupin's own totals are not fully consistent.

**My arithmetic, inferred:** days reclaimed went from 12,000 to 34,336, nearly three times. Savings went from 400 to 428 million rupees, about 7% more. The two years may not count the same things. Still, most of the freed time did not show up as savings, and Lupin does not say what it became beyond "higher-value work."

**Unit of analysis**
- One drugmaker, headquartered outside the United States, reporting on itself across all functions. The commercial part is its sales-rep tools in India. Not a claim about American commercial teams.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Series belief: "value is defined by sellers, when at all." And Friday's insight that numbers show up where the job is a bounded check with one owner.
- Primary lens: Use Case. Secondary: Repeatability and Scale, since 25 of 250 ideas made it.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** A buyer does publish numbers. But the commercial numbers are adoption counts, and the outcome numbers sit in manufacturing and supply chain, where jobs are bounded. That fits Friday's insight.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- Direct company numbers, with a prior year to compare. One company, self-reported, outside Venkat's market.

**Pattern basis**
- Reinforces: Friday's Tufts trial-monitoring and Cigna routing numbers, both bounded jobs. Lupin's first AI program to scale, a year ago, was also a bounded job, yield optimization in a plant.
- Also reinforces narrowing: roughly 1 in 10 ideas became a scaled use case.
- Contradicts: Lupin says reps got time back "to focus on high-value customer engagement." No measure given.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: commercial value is harder to isolate than plant yield, so companies report what they can count.
- Strongest alternative: Lupin reports adoption because that is what a finance chief can defend, not because commercial results are absent.
- Outside this lens: an adoption rate of 93% may reflect a required tool, not a chosen one. That is an operating choice, not proof the use case works.
- What would make us wrong: a drugmaker reporting a sales-rep AI tool tied to a change in prescribing, access, or reach, with a baseline.

**Watch next**
- A United States drugmaker publishing time saved and what the time went to.
- Lupin's commercial numbers moving past adoption.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Concrete, but one company outside Venkat's market.
