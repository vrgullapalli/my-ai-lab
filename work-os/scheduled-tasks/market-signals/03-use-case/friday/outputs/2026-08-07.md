## Signal 1: Pharma money moves to one narrow AI job: reaching doctors inside AI search

**What happened**
- Observed: on August 6, Doximity, the doctors' network, said it signed its first drug company customers for sponsored programs inside its AI search tool.
- Not known: which drugs, what the programs show doctors, or what result the drug companies expect.

**Unit of analysis:** one platform's drug company customers, buying one job: being present when a doctor asks an AI tool a clinical question.

**Evidence**
- From the call ([StockAnalysis transcript, August 6](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/docs/transcripts/668286-q1-2027/)): "We have onboarded our first cohort of AI Search customers across more than two dozen programs." Claimed.
- Same call: "The overall pharma spending environment remains tight," and Doximity is "starting to win innovation budgets with the launch of AI Search." Claimed.
- Doctors' side: "AI Search query growth of over 25% quarter-over-quarter" ([Doximity release, August 6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1516513/000151651326000038/doximity-2026630xex991.htm)). Claimed.
- The only money number is the seller's margin: "more than 10 times per search in revenue than it costs us to run." Nothing on what drug companies get back.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** One seller. But it is signed contracts from buyers, not a launch. Budget outweighs announcements.

**Obvious explanation:** marketing money follows doctors' attention, and doctors' attention is moving to AI search.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: the problem hypothesis "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Primary: Use Case. Secondary: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Buyers are paying for a specific AI job. But the job is placement, and the proof on offer is usage.

**Pattern basis:** new for this series. It rhymes with Indegene last week: budget tied to a commercial job. But Indegene is paid on outcomes; this, as far as the call says, is paid for placement. Inferred. Too early to tell.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: The buyer pays, the doctor uses, and they need different proof**
- In Signal 1 the user is the doctor; the buyer is the drug company. Doximity reports usage. The buyer needs doctors to act differently. Inferred.
- Why it matters: "usage is up" can be true while the buyer's job goes unmet.

**KEY INSIGHT: Three buyers, three measures, none shows AI's own effect**
- Pfizer counts bundled savings. Doximity's buyers get placement and usage. Indegene's client counts a private outcome. Observed that they differ; inferred that none isolates AI.
- Why it matters: the accountable leader will be asked "what did AI do?" and none answers it. Decision Integrity Under Complexity, in plain form.

**KEY INSIGHT: Sellers say AI savings go back to clients as lower prices**
- WPP's chief executive, August 6: "AI tooling will drive productivity gains and reduce our cost to serve, and clients will expect us to pass those savings on" ([Yahoo Finance call summary, August 6](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/wpp-plc-wpp-h1-2026-150135384.html)). Claimed. It repeats Indegene's "GenAI is driving price deflation" from last week.
- Why it matters: if AI's value shows up as a cheaper invoice, buyers may count savings and never ask whether the work got better. Inferred.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **What else could explain this?** Doximity's deals may be ordinary ad buys in a new slot, labeled "innovation." Pfizer's "more effective" field force may mean fewer reps. Neither is shown.
- **Counterevidence:** WPP says its AI data layer is "driving meaningful uplift in media performance for clients" ([WPP interim results, August 6](https://www.wpp.com/en/news/2026-interim-results)). Claimed, no number.
- **What would make us wrong:** a drug company names what it measures from AI search, such as new prescribers against a baseline. Or Pfizer puts a number on its field claim.

## Trigger

- A drug company names the outcome it bought in AI search.
- A second platform reports drug company money for placement inside an AI tool.
- A second outcome-priced deal, or Indegene names its measure.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE:** four questions for any AI placement or tool buy. "Who uses it?" "Who pays?" "What does the payer need that user to do differently?" "Who counts that, against what?"
- **DEEPER ASSET:** not yet. Thin evidence.
- **LEARNING QUESTION:** When drug companies buy presence inside doctors' AI tools, do they count reach, engagement, or prescribing? Who owns the number?
- **LONG-TERM VALUE:** if buyers keep paying for AI jobs they cannot measure, someone independent has to define the measure first. The four questions, then a review of one buyer's AI spend, then a standing check before signing.

## Decision

- **What changed?** Buyer money is going to a narrow, named AI job. The one buyer naming commercial AI jobs cannot show what AI saved.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE**, scoped to what buyers count as commercial AI value.
- **Why this state?** Real budget appeared two weeks running, each time with a private or missing measure. Cheap to learn more.
- **What moves it next?** A buyer naming its measure moves it to TALK. Both measures proving to be reach or activity moves it to MONITOR.
