## Signal 1: The one narrow field job for AI rests on a seller's two-month-old number

**What happened:** Veeva described a field job on August 10: a rep speaks visit notes, AI flags treatment barriers, a person fixes them. On August 11, Lilly committed to Veeva's CRM (customer relationship management) system. Observed. Not known: who uses this job, or how many barriers got fixed.

**Unit of analysis:** one seller's description of one field workflow, plus one drugmaker's platform choice.

**Evidence**
- Matt Farrell, Veeva, August 10: "When field teams use an agentic call report, we see that 65% of compliant, free-text field notes identify actionable treatment barriers not captured by traditional reports" ([Pharmaceutical Executive](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/generating-commercial-evidence-agentic-field-brand-engagement-accelerate-launch-performance)). Claimed. No method.
- The one example: a hospital approval step took "up to four weeks," so patients moved to another drug. "The issue was escalated to a reimbursement account manager to gain hospital-level approval." Claimed. No company named.
- Background, June 17, Veeva's president of Europe: "We're seeing that 65% of all interactions creating actionable insights about blockers to prescription" ([pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/digital/veeva-agentic-commercial-evidence-led-ai-pharma)). Same seller, same number.
- Lilly's Giuseppe Firenze, August 11: "Vault CRM will help Lilly bring our breakthrough medicines to more patients" ([Veeva release](https://www.veeva.com/resources/veeva-vault-crm-selected-by-eli-lilly-and-company/)). The release mentions the "Agentic Call Report" but not that Lilly will use it. Observed.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** One seller repeating its own number. Lilly's money went to a platform, not this job.

**Obvious explanation:** a seller telling its best story next to a big customer win.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Primary: Use Case. Secondary: Operating Model, since the value sits with whoever owns the fix.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Still the clearest field job in the series, with a trigger, output, and human owner. But its proof has not moved since June.

**Pattern basis:** isolated. Wednesday treated it as new; it is two months old.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: The measure stops where the AI's part ends**
- Veeva counts notes that flag a barrier. The value comes a step later, when a reimbursement account manager wins approval. Nobody reports that step. Inferred.
- Why it matters: "65% of notes flag a barrier" can be true while no patient gets the preferred drug faster. The use case is the handoff, not the note.

**KEY INSIGHT: "Without analyst support" moves the job to a new user**
- OptimizeRx sells list building to marketers "without code or analyst support." The user shifts from analyst to marketer. Inferred.
- Why it matters: the person who checked list quality leaves the loop. Who catches a bad list is not stated.

**KEY INSIGHT: A few buyers decide the category**
- OptimizeRx's drop sits with a few customers, one at zero. Observed. "Tight budgets" may mean a handful of leaders said no. Inferred.
- Why it matters: those "no" decisions best show what buyers think these tools are worth, and none are public.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **What else could explain this?** Buyers may measure privately; silence may be legal caution. OptimizeRx's lost buyer may reflect pricing policy, not AI value.
- **Counterevidence:** Definitive's strong new-customer quarter. Veeva's claimed example of one company fixing a flagged barrier.
- **What would make us wrong:** a drug company names the barrier-fix job with a count, or an agency shows a before and after for list building.

## Trigger

- Lilly, or any drugmaker, says it uses AI call notes to find access barriers, with fixes or days to approval counted.
- Veeva publishes how it measured the 65% or the 10%.
- OptimizeRx names an agency or ad-buying platform with the audience tool live.
- Definitive names a drugmaker in its pilot, or what the pilot measures.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE:** "Where does your number stop?" Sort any AI result into three lines: what the AI produced, what a person did with it, what changed for the patient or the sale. Ask who counts line three. Veeva's 65% is line one.
- **DEEPER ASSET:** not yet. The sort is a question for conversations, not a product.
- **LEARNING QUESTION:** When rep notes flag an access barrier, who owns the fix, and does anyone count fixes against a baseline, such as days to approval?
- **LONG-TERM VALUE:** if measures keep stopping at the AI's output, someone must follow the handoff to the outcome: the sort in conversations, then one buyer's results read through it, then a check before renewing an AI tool.

## Decision

- **What changed?** Little. The clearest field job for AI is older and less proven than it looked Wednesday.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE.**
- **Why this state?** The question is open and cheap to work. Neither last week's bar for TALK nor for MONITOR was met.
- **What moves it next?** A buyer naming its measure moves it to TALK. Two more weeks of seller-only numbers moves it to MONITOR.
