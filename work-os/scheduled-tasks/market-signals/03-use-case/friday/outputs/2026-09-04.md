## Signal 1: A buyer says where AI adds work, but not what it improved

**What happened?** Observed: two chief commercial officers spoke at EVERSANA's August 27 client event. Lundbeck's gave a goal, not a measure, and warned that misplaced AI adds work. Interpretation: buyers learn where AI fits inside pilots, and keep it private.

**Unit of analysis:** two drugmakers' United States commercial groups, one vendor, one event.

**Evidence**
- Shae Maughmer, Lundbeck: "If you're not careful in correctly choosing where you're going to deploy the tool and how you deploy it, it can actually create a lot of inefficiency and more workload." And: "we've actually had a situation which, as the tool learned, it learned the wrong things" ([pharmaphorum, September 4](https://pharmaphorum.com/sales-marketing/one-year-later-eversana-plans-go-beyond-ai-agency)). pharmaphorum is owned by EVERSANA.
- Independent outlet, same executive: rollout is "stepwise, guided by results from a series of internal pilots," and "Lundbeck has not disclosed performance data." On success: "I go back to the North Star" ([Medical Marketing and Media, August 31](https://www.mmm-online.com/news/eversana-expanded-ai-agency-platform-lundbeck-going-all-in/)).
- Graham Goodrich, Apnimed: "we're going have a lot of amazing kitchens with people who don't necessarily know how to cook," and "cooking in this future is redesigning your processes, redesigning your decision-making" (pharmaphorum, September 4).

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Named buyers describing real use. But two reports of one vendor-hosted event, counted once.

**Obvious explanation:** early rollouts have bumps, and buyers keep details private.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Primary: Use Case. Secondary: Trust and Control (who catches a tool that learned wrong).
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** The buyer itself now says placement decides value and agents drift after launch.

**Pattern basis:** repeating and clearer: last week Lundbeck acted, Wednesday it gave a goal, Friday it named failure modes. Still no job named.

## Signal 2: Buyer-signed numbers appear in narrow jobs outside commercial

**What happened?** Observed: Takeda scaled an AI tool for drafting regulatory filings after a study its staff co-wrote. Interpretation: the before and after exists where one team owns a bounded output.

**Unit of analysis:** regulatory writing at Takeda; company-wide AI at Lupin, an India-based drugmaker. Not United States commercial work.

**Evidence**
- Takeda and Weave Bio: the study "showed a 97% reduction in time to first draft within the evaluated use case, from approximately 100 hours to three," now across "14 active programs." Takeda's regulatory chief: "regulatory professionals retain responsibility for content, verification and final decision-making" ([BioSpace, September 1](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/weave-bio-announces-collaboration-with-takeda-to-deploy-ai-native-regulatory-automation-across-key-drug-development-activities)).
- The study's caveat (background, 2025): manual times "were estimated based on the experience of regulatory writers"; AI times were measured ([arXiv study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.09738)).
- Lupin: "Digital sales platform adopted by 11,000+ active users (93%+ adoption)," with no sales result given ([Lupin, August 29](https://www.lupin.com/media/perspectives/lupin-s-ai-led-transformation-delivers-428-million-in-savings-reclaims-34-336-man-days-in-fy-26)).

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** A buyer co-signed a measured result and scaled on it. But the baseline was estimated, and the job is not commercial.

**Obvious explanation:** regulatory drafting is easier to time than a sales result.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: last week's insight that numbers show up "where the job is a bounded check and a person keeps the hard cases." Primary: Use Case. Secondary: Operating Model.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** The buyer signed the number, and the team owning the output kept the decision.

**Pattern basis:** strengthening in narrow jobs (Tufts and Cigna last week, Takeda now), flat in commercial, where 93% adoption may just mean a required tool.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: A pilot's most useful finding is where not to use AI, and nobody publishes it.**
- Inferred: Lundbeck's private results likely include where AI added work or drifted. That is not shown at a vendor showcase.
- Why it matters: a use case is proven by where it stops as much as where it works. Scaling leaders need that "stop here" list.

**KEY INSIGHT: Even the best number rests on a guessed "before."**
- Takeda's AI times were measured; its manual times were estimates. Observed.
- Why it matters: if the cleanest case lacks a measured baseline, commercial teams almost certainly do too. Setting one before go-live is the unmet job.

**KEY INSIGHT: A tool that makes research cheap can starve the research it runs on.**
- Inferred: once testing feels free, real research budgets shrink, and the 90% decays with no alarm.
- Why it matters: this use case needs a named owner for the refresh.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE:** Three questions for any pilot readout. Where did it add work? What did it learn wrong, and who caught it? Was the "before" measured or guessed? No answers means the pilot tested the tool, not the use case.
- **DEEPER ASSET:** a one-page pilot readout: helped here, added work here, drifted here, output owner, baseline type. Test it in one conversation first.
- **LEARNING QUESTION:** When a commercial AI tool "learned the wrong things," who noticed, and how long did it run first?
- **LONG-TERM VALUE:** questions, then real readouts, then a map of where commercial AI adds work, then a pre-scale check.
