## Signal 1: A drugmaker pays for commercial work by results, not effort

**What happened**
- Observed: Indegene, which runs commercial services for drugmakers, said July 31 that one top client signed an omnichannel marketing deal paid purely on outcomes.
- Not known: the client, the outcome measured, the baseline, or what part AI plays.

**Unit of analysis:** one vendor's contract with one unnamed drugmaker, for omnichannel marketing work.

**Evidence**
- Chief executive Manish Gupta: "A pure outcome-based contract, wherein while the engagement has gone live, the revenue recognition is deferred by about three quarters... It's based on client acceptance of certain outcomes which are measured on a quarterly basis" ([Investing.com transcript, July 31](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-indegene-posts-strong-q1-2026-growth-stock-rises-93CH-4826762)). Claimed.
- Same call: "The client has been sharing the revenue upticks with us for now five months. Those numbers are very encouraging." And: "About 60% of revenue now comes from output- and outcome-based contracts." Claimed.
- The slides put the deal at "exceeding $10 million annually" and say "GenAI is not a standalone business line but embedded across all workflows" ([Investing.com slides summary, July 31](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/indegene-q1-fy27-slides-40-revenue-growth-client-base-expands-93CH-4826766)). GenAI means generative AI.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** One seller, one unnamed client. But it is budget and contract terms: the vendor waits for its revenue until the client accepts the outcome. That outweighs a press release.

**Obvious explanation:** a services firm using pricing to win share as AI pushes prices down. Gupta said "GenAI is driving price deflation."

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: the need hypothesis that leaders need "an evidence-backed choice," and the problem hypothesis that AI moves faster than value can be proven. Primary: Use Case. Secondary: Trust and Control.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** A buyer defined value in a contract. But the measure is private, and AI's share cannot be separated from the people's.

**Pattern basis**
- New. Rhymes with Doceree last week (value stated as a commercial outcome). The difference: Doceree graded its own test; here the client supplies the numbers. Too early to tell. One deal.

## Signal 2: Vendors disagree on how fast commercial AI is spreading

**What happened:** two vendors described client adoption on results calls, and told different stories. Observed.

**Unit of analysis:** two sellers' accounts of their own pharma clients.

**Evidence**
- IQVIA's Ari Bousbib: "19 of the top 20 pharma companies have already deployed IQVIA AI solutions in their workflow," clients are "moving beyond pilots and data foundation work," and one is expanding "its use of IQVIA AI across an immunology franchise in 95 countries" ([Investing.com transcript, July 28](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-iqvia-tops-q2-2026-estimates-and-raises-outlook-93CH-4817225)). The release credits only "increased adoption of our AI solutions" as one growth driver ([IQVIA release, July 28](https://ir.iqvia.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/IQVIA-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)). No outcome numbers.
- Indegene's Gupta: "while the enthusiasm is real, enterprise adoptions remain measured and the pace of change on the ground is slower." On one large engagement: "offtake on the existing scope has been measured" (Indegene transcript above). Claimed.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** Two independent sellers, both with reasons to spin. No usage or outcome number from either.

**Obvious explanation:** IQVIA counts software switched on. Indegene sees whether the work gets used.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Primary: Use Case. Secondary: Operating Model.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** "Deployed" is rising. "Used on the ground" lags, by one account. Both can be true.

**Pattern basis:** repeats the midweek pattern (named jobs, bigger scale claims, no results). New: the first counter-voice from a vendor. Stable.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: An outcome contract measures the work, not the AI**
- Indegene says AI is embedded in everything. So the deal pays for people, data, and AI together. Nobody can say what the AI added. Inferred.
- Why it matters: the first real proof of commercial value may arrive with AI's share unknowable. It answers "did the work pay off," not "is our AI working."

**KEY INSIGHT: The gap between "deployed" and "used" lands on the buyer**
- The gap is people and workflow, and it lands on the accountable commercial leader. Inferred. It fits the hypothesis that the leader "receives the uncertainty that upstream teams and systems did not resolve."

**KEY INSIGHT: Sanofi named the need without naming the tool**
- A new chief executive asked for fact-based decisions "at the commercial level" and said nothing about AI. Observed. The need may surface as governance first, not technology. Inferred. It sits squarely in Decision Integrity Under Complexity.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **What else could explain this?** The Indegene deal may be a pricing tactic with a loose outcome. "Measured" adoption may be one client's story, generalized.
- **Counterevidence:** IQVIA claims near-universal deployment at top drugmakers. If commercial AI is routine, silence on calls means nothing.
- **What would make us wrong:** the outcome turns out to be activity (reach, emails sent), not revenue or prescriptions. Or a drugmaker describes commercial AI at scale, with a result.

## Trigger

- A second vendor or agency reports a commercial deal paid on client outcomes, and names the outcome.
- Indegene or the client names the measure, or reports the full-year true-up.
- A drugmaker names a commercial AI job with a measured result.
- Doceree: a named customer confirms the lift. Otherwise drop it after one more quiet week.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE:** three questions for any outcome-based commercial deal. "What outcome, counted by whom?" "Against what baseline?" "If it works, can you tell whether the AI or the people did it?"
- **DEEPER ASSET:** not yet. One contract.
- **LEARNING QUESTION:** In outcome-based commercial contracts, is the outcome revenue, prescriptions, or activity? And who owns the number?
- **LONG-TERM VALUE:** if outcome pricing spreads, buyers need someone independent to check the measure. The three questions first, then a short contract review, then a standing service before a buyer signs.

## Decision

- **What changed?** One drugmaker pays for commercial work by results. One vendor says adoption is slow.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE**, scoped to the outcome-contract question.
- **Why this state?** Budget tied to an outcome weighs most here, and learning the measure is cheap.
- **What moves it next?** A second outcome-priced commercial deal moves it to TALK. Learning the outcome is only activity moves it back to MONITOR.
