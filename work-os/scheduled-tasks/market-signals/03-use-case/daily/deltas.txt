YOUR JOB TODAY: the Use Case Market Signals daily briefing

Use the fixed daily schema below. The structure, section names, and order match the other pillar briefs. Only the purpose, scope, selection rules, and Use Case content differ.

PURPOSE

Surface up to 5 high-value signals that could change how Venkat thinks, advises, creates, or acts around Use Cases for AI and customer-data capabilities in pharma.

Prioritize signals tied to what specific work, decision, behavior, or outcome AI or customer data is actually being used to improve, for whom, and under what conditions.

Do not turn this into a general AI news digest or a list of possible AI use cases.

SCOPE

Use asks: what specific work, decision, behavior, or outcome is the AI or customer-data capability supposed to improve, for whom, and under what conditions?

Having AI, data, models, or a platform does not establish a use case. A real use case connects the capability to: a specific problem or need; a real user or actor; a job to be done; a decision or piece of work that changes; a clear workflow and trigger; an output someone can act on; a defined role for AI and for people; clear constraints, exceptions, and boundaries; required data, knowledge, systems, and integrations; a measurable customer or business outcome; a baseline and a way to judge incremental value; ownership, feedback, and ongoing operation; conditions for repeatability and scale.

Scan across these Use areas: problem and outcome; user, work, and decision; interaction and workflow; AI role and authority; context and dependencies; constraints and boundaries; value and measurement; operating requirements; scale and repeatability; strategic leverage and capability reuse.

The key distinction: a feature or capability is not a use case. 'Predictive models' is a feature. 'Predict customer propensity' is a capability. A use case explains who uses that prediction, what decision changes, what happens next, and what outcome should improve.

SELECTION RULES

Choose the highest-value Use Case signals, up to 5.

Prioritize: recent and credible evidence; companies narrowing broad AI activity into specific decisions, workflows, or outcomes; new AI or customer-data uses tied to clear users, actions, and measurable business or customer outcomes; evidence showing which use cases are being funded, scaled, narrowed, redesigned, or stopped; changes in the role or authority given to AI, whether it assists, recommends, predicts, decides, executes, or adapts; evidence that workflow placement, timing, actionability, or user behavior determines whether a capability creates value; clear examples of dependencies, boundaries, failure states, or human judgment affecting a use case; measurable evidence that AI improves on the current process rather than merely adding activity; changes in how organizations prioritize use cases based on value, feasibility, risk, reuse, or economics; use cases that create reusable capabilities rather than isolated projects; pharma hiring or operating changes that reveal how AI or customer-data use is being embedded into real work; signals that strengthen, weaken, or complicate an existing Use belief, problem, hypothesis, or opportunity.

Avoid: duplicates; low-signal hype; generic AI use case lists; feature announcements with no clear user, decision, workflow, or outcome; pilots described only through usage or technical performance; vague personalization, automation, productivity, or copilot claims with no clear behavior or outcome change; unsupported return-on-investment claims; several stories about the same underlying event.

If several stories point to the same Use Case change, combine them into one signal. If fewer than 5 meet the bar, use fewer. Do not pad the brief.
