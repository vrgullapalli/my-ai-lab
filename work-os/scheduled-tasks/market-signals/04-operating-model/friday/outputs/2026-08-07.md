## Signal 1: No step in agent building assigns an owner

**What happened**
- Observed: one vendor measured live systems and found most agents built by business staff, with wide access.
- Interpretation: ownership used to pass at a handoff. Business-built agents skip it, so no one is asked to own them. Inferred.

**Unit of analysis.** Agents at companies Opsin monitors, across eight industries, plus SAP's survey. Not pharma. Read-across is my inference.

**Evidence**
- [Opsin Labs, via Yahoo Finance, August 5](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/opsin-labs-report-60-enterprise-162100301.html). Method: "enterprise production environments across eight verticals between March 2025 and June 2026." Measured by the vendor.
  - "67% of agents are being built by employees without engineering background."
  - "60% of agents provisioned beyond default settings were granted allow-all access."
  - "most of the associated risk traces back to provisioning choices that were never revisited." That is a maintenance gap, not only a setup gap.
- [SAP News Center, August 3](https://news.sap.com/2026/08/agent-sprawl-why-ai-governance-is-now-board-level-issue/), Paul Taylor: "less than half of the organizations surveyed have visibility into an inventory of AI agents." Vendor blog, no sample size.
- Counterevidence: Opsin counts agents "live or in draft mode," so many may do little. Nothing shows a named owner failing.

**Evidence strength:** MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR. First measured data in this series, but from a vendor that sells the fix, with the method only in a press release.

**Obvious explanation.** Easy tools, lagging rules, as with spreadsheets before.

**Hypothesis affected.** "The pieces have owners. The connections often do not." Also the audience condition: the leader is accountable but does not implement. Here the builder may sit in the leader's own team. Territory: Human-Governed Agentic Operating Systems. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.

**Direction:** STRENGTHENS. The gap is not a missing name. It is a missing moment where anyone would write one down.

**Pattern basis.** Repeating for a fourth week. Eight sponsors across three weeks agree. New: measured data, and business builders. Stable. Still no pharma source.

**Recommended state:** INVESTIGATE.

## Signal 2: Leaders say the blocker is authority, not accuracy

**What happened**
- Observed: on Thursday, a survey found nearly all leaders would let agents act alone in live systems, under conditions.
- Interpretation: the question is shifting from "does it work" to "who can let it act." But the conditions named are guardrails and fast approval, not an owner of results. Inferred.

**Unit of analysis.** 200 senior leaders at firms with 1,000 or more staff in the United States and Canada. About agents changing live technical systems, not business workflows.

**Evidence**
- [Caylent, via PR Newswire, August 6](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/98-of-enterprise-leaders-would-let-ai-agents-run-production-under-the-right-conditions-caylent-survey-reveals-302844574.html). Run by Censuswide for Caylent, a cloud services firm.
  - "98% of enterprise leaders say there are specific conditions under which they would allow AI agents to autonomously execute changes in production."
  - "83% place guardrails on equal or higher footing than model intelligence."
  - The release: "What's left is authority, not accuracy. Velocity comes from allowing humans in the loop to rapidly approve agentic behaviors."
- Counterevidence: self-reported, from a seller of agent operations. It does not say who approves.

**Evidence strength:** LOW. EARLY INDICATOR. One vendor survey, no pharma.

**Obvious explanation.** Vendors name the barrier they remove.

**Hypothesis affected.** "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Territory: Decision Integrity Under Complexity. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.

**Direction:** COMPLICATES. It supports authority as the real question, but treats human approval as a speed tool. If approval is fast by design, what judgment does the human add?

**Pattern basis.** Isolated. It links to Signal 1: agents built without an owner, then cleared by approval built for speed.

**Recommended state:** MONITOR.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: Ownership used to come from the handoff.** A builder handed a tool to an operator, and that moment forced someone to own it. When a business user builds and runs the agent, no one is asked. Opsin's "never revisited" line shows the cost: nobody's job is to go back. Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: Fast approval is not judgment.** If approval is built for speed, the approver may be a rubber stamp with a name on it. Human review needs a defined question, or it moves blame, not judgment. Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: The commercial leader inherits both ends.** Finance sets the AI savings. Business staff build the agents. Neither link has an owner, and the leader answers for the result. Inferred.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **Strongest alternative:** this is access control first. With well-scoped permissions, who built the agent matters less. That makes it Trust and Control.
- **Counterevidence:** every agent source sells a fix. Pharma's medical, legal, and regulatory review may already assign sign-off.
- **What would make us wrong:** a pharma company requiring a named owner and a scope check before any agent goes live, and showing it works.

## Trigger

- A pharma source saying who approves agents that commercial or medical staff build.
- A second measured source, not a survey, on who builds agents.
- A drugmaker naming who owns AI in commercial work, with power to stop it.
- Anyone defining what a fast approver must check.

## Value

- **Fast value:** "Who built this agent, and when did anyone last check what it can reach?" If the builder moved on, or the answer is "never," there is no owner.
- **Deeper asset:** not yet.
- **Learning question:** do pharma field or marketing staff build their own agents, and does medical, legal, and regulatory review ever see them?
- **Long-term value:** if pharma shows the missing handoff, a check for "owner, access, last review" opens his bounded diagnostic, and answers across companies become proof.

## Decision

- **What changed:** ownership fails at a missing handoff, not only a missing name. Pharma AI targets spread, still without owners.
- **Recommended state:** INVESTIGATE.
- **Why:** measured data and a fourth week, but no pharma evidence on agent ownership.
- **What moves it next:** pharma business-built agents with no owner moves it to TALK. Existing review catching them moves it to MONITOR.
