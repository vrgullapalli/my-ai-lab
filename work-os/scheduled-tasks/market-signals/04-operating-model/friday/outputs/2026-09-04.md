## Signal 2: Human review is the named safeguard, and it is getting thinner

**What happened**
- Observed: advisers keep proposing review gates, while new evidence shows bad data and labels weaken reviewers.
- Interpretation: review is added by default, without defining the judgment it supplies. Inferred.

**Unit of analysis.** Biopharma commercial analytics, sales leaders, managers in general. No pharma reviewer.

**Evidence**
- [BioProcess International, September 2](https://www.bioprocessintl.com/information-technology/agentic-ai-in-biopharmaceutical-commercial-analytics-benefits-risks-and-use-cases): "Outputs should pass a defined review gate before broader distribution."
- [PharosBio, September 4](https://pharos.bio/blog/ai-governance-pharma), a company blog: "nobody is ever punished for adding a review," and "tier drift is how governance fails after go-live."
- [Digital Journal, September 3](https://www.digitaljournal.com/article/checking-checking-is-human-oversight-of-ai-becoming-a-false-comfort/), on a Salesloft survey of sales leaders: "If human reviewers are working with flawed information, can they realistically provide effective oversight?" Not pharma.
- [GRC Report, September 3](https://www.grcreport.com/post/the-ai-employee-is-a-governance-failure-waiting-to-happen), a governance, risk, and compliance site, says people "caught 18 percent fewer errors" when AI was framed as an employee. The source, a [Boston University and Boston Consulting Group paper, July 17 draft](https://emmawiles.github.io/storage/ai_employee.pdf) (background), is narrower: "Average effects to error catching are small." But where companies already list "AI employees," the label "reduces managers' monitoring intensity by 16%" and "shifts their perceived accountability away from themselves."
- [pharmaphorum, September 4](https://pharmaphorum.com/sales-marketing/one-year-later-eversana-plans-go-beyond-ai-agency), EVERSANA's technology head: the tool "can be used as an MLR accelerator by the MLR teams, should we ever evolve to that," MLR meaning medical, legal, and regulatory review.

**Evidence strength:** LOW. REINFORCING INDICATOR. Advice and non-pharma surveys.

**Obvious explanation.** Regulators want a named human, so everyone adds one.

**Hypothesis affected.** "Reliance should be proportional to evidence." Territory: Decision Integrity Under Complexity. Secondary: Trust and Control.

**Direction:** COMPLICATES. A named reviewer is not review. What the human is shown, and what the AI is called, change how hard they look.

**Pattern basis.** Fifth week, stable. New: the first experiment on labels.

## Signal 3: Accountability lands on "the company," but no one has the list

**What happened**
- Observed: commentators say pharma cannot name, per AI system, who is accountable. Board policy lags adoption.
- Interpretation: accountability is assigned at the top without the inventory to use it. Inferred.

**Unit of analysis.** Large pharma's regulated processes (one editorial) and large companies in general.

**Evidence**
- [Pharma Focus Europe, August 31](https://www.pharmafocuseurope.com/articles/ai-governance-in-pharma-managing-trust-transparency-and-regulatory-expectations), date from the search index: "Few executive teams could produce on demand a complete list of the AI systems influencing decisions in their regulated processes, together with the named individual accountable for each one." Its list includes "commercial targeting."
- [Forbes Technology Council, August 28](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbestechcouncil/2026/08/28/adding-a-caio-heres-how-to-avoid-role-overlap-and-friction): "Most organizations define who owns AI strategy but not who owns AI failure."
- [Grant Thornton, September 3](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/articles/advisory/2026/how-boards-govern-agentic-ai-risk), its own survey: only 52 percent of boards have AI policies, while 74 percent of organizations pilot or use agentic AI.

**Evidence strength:** LOW. EARLY INDICATOR. Commentary and a consultant's survey.

**Obvious explanation.** Governance advice always says "make a list."

**Hypothesis affected.** Need: leaders need a fast, bounded view of what their foundation can support. Secondary: Repeatability and Scale.

**Direction:** STRENGTHENS, as a stated problem only.

**Pattern basis.** Too early. The faded stop-switch pattern, reframed as ownership.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: The review gate checks outputs. Drift happens in the learning.** Lundbeck kept its review process. But an agent that "learned the wrong things" fails upstream; output review catches it piece by piece, after the fact. Approval of a learning system quietly expires, and the approver and the learning may sit in different companies. Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: Calling agents "labor" is a governance choice.** Last week Veeva priced agents as labor. The experiment suggests the "employee" label moves felt accountability toward the machine. Oversight may soften without anyone deciding it should. Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: The hidden work lands on review.** Review teams likely were not in the room when the platform was bought, yet they absorb the "more workload." Inferred.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **Strongest alternative:** ordinary tuning plus conference talk, reported by the vendor's own outlet.
- **Counterevidence:** the drift was caught. Lundbeck gates by pilot results. Its chief AI officer may be the unreported owner.
- **What would make us wrong:** a named person who caught the drift, approved the fix, and can roll back.

## Trigger

- A named person credited with catching or fixing Lundbeck's drift.
- A drugmaker naming an owner for an agent's ongoing behavior, with time or budget for it.
- Public terms on who keeps prompts, settings, and learned behavior when a vendor changes.
