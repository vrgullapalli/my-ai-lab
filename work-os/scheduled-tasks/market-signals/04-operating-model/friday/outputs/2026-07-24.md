## The short answer

One belief got stronger. Agents are going to work before anyone is named to answer for them, and blame lands on whoever approved the money. Three independent sources agree.

Two other patterns are early: vendors selling software into the gap, and know-how sitting outside the company.

None of it comes from pharma. Thursday and Friday added no pharma evidence. [Roche's half-year results on July 23](https://www.roche.com/media/releases/med-cor-2026-07-23) said nothing about it.

## Signal 1: Nobody answers for the agent, so blame rolls uphill

**What happened**
- Observed: two surveys released July 21 found about one in five organizations cannot say who is responsible when an agent makes a serious mistake.
- Observed: a July 22 survey found most chief executives have not tied AI work to a profit and loss owner.
- Interpretation: ownership of the tool is being settled. Ownership of the outcome is not.

**Unit of analysis.** Finance leaders in four countries, United States federal technology leaders, and chief executives of companies with at least $500 million in revenue. Not pharma. Any read-across to pharma is my inference.

**Evidence**
- [Avalara survey, July 21](https://newsroom.avalara.com/2026-07-21-Avalara-Survey-Finance-Leaders-are-Racing-to-Deploy-AI-Agents-Before-Governance-is-Ready), 1,505 finance leaders: "nearly one in four (23%) say accountability for a significant AI agent error would be unclear or sit with no one." And "16% believe the executive who approved the AI investment would ultimately be held personally accountable."
- [Booz Allen survey, July 21](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/booz-allen-survey-finds-federal-150000609.html), 105 federal leaders, fielded in April: "22% of respondents say their organizations have not clearly determined who bears responsibility when an AI agent causes a security incident or operational failure." [ExecutiveBiz, July 22](https://www.executivebiz.com/articles/booz-allen-federal-agentic-ai-survey) repeats it. That counts once.
- New since midweek: [BCG, July 22](https://www.bcg.com/press/22july2026-ceos-cost-revenue-benefits-ai-struggling-scale), 152 chief executives: "More than half cited the need to link AI initiatives to the P&L as a key barrier, but only 14% have clearly defined the P&L impact for all AI initiatives." P&L means profit and loss. The [full report](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/how-ceos-scale-ai-value) adds: "Individual projects need individual owners," and "fewer than 10% believe AI strategy should be outsourced to a chief AI officer."
- Closest to pharma: [Grant Thornton on life sciences, July 21](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/articles/life-sciences/2026/ai-across-life-sciences-demands-proof): "Much of the published guidance on AI assumes centralized ownership and mature governance structures. Yet audit and regulatory expectations remain high regardless of operating model."
- Counterevidence: about three in four respondents in both July 21 surveys say ownership is settled.

**Evidence strength:** MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR. Three sponsors, three groups of respondents, one direction. All are self-reported, and two sponsors sell help with this problem.

**Obvious explanation.** Agents are new, and org charts always lag new tools.

**Hypothesis affected.** "The pieces have owners. The connections often do not." Also: "The accountable leader receives the uncertainty that upstream teams and systems did not resolve." The 16% finding is that hypothesis in survey form. Territory: T01 Decision Integrity Under Complexity. Secondary dependency: Use, because no one can own a result nobody defined.

**Direction:** STRENGTHENS. The gap now reaches the chief executive's office.

**Pattern basis.** People running agents and people funding them point the same way. Steady, not speeding up. Not yet seen in pharma.

**Challenge**
- What else could explain it: a value-measurement gap. If no one defined what the agent should improve, no one can own its failure. That is a Use gap dressed as an ownership gap.
- What would make us wrong: pharma evidence of a smaller gap, because medical, legal, and regulatory review already assigns sign-off on customer-facing work.

**Recommended state:** INVESTIGATE.

## Signal 2: Governance is being sold as a product

**What happened**
- Observed: on July 22, ServiceNow tied its agent growth to its governance tool.
- Interpretation: companies that have not decided who runs their agents are buying a tool to watch them.

**Unit of analysis.** One enterprise software vendor's customers, across industries.

**Evidence**
- [ServiceNow results, July 22](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx): "With our AI Control Tower as the market standard, agentic deployments of ServiceNow AI increased ninefold in just nine months." A vendor claim.
- [Diginomica, July 22](https://diginomica.com/servicenow-q2-2026-ai-acv-passes-1-billion-zavery-says-governance-opening-new-doors), quoting president Amit Zavery: "people are realizing it isn't manageable, isn't tameable."
- [Okta on the Cloud Security Alliance blog, July 17](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2026/07/17/the-hidden-risks-of-the-agentic-enterprise-bridging-the-ai-governance-gap): "only 34% of organizations report applying the same rigorous security controls to their agentic labor force as they do to their human labor force." Vendor research.

**Evidence strength:** LOW. EARLY INDICATOR. Buying beats a survey answer, but every claim comes from a vendor that sells the fix.

**Obvious explanation.** A normal market for managing a new kind of software.

**Hypothesis affected.** "Organizations keep adding technology to a coordination and meaning problem." Territory: T06 Human-Governed Agentic Operating Systems. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.

**Direction:** COMPLICATES. A dashboard can show every agent. It cannot decide who answers for one.

**Pattern basis.** Too early. One vendor, one quarter, nothing from pharma.

**Challenge.** Security teams may want visibility, which is a Trust and Control need, not an ownership one. What would make us wrong: governance tools that require a named owner with stop rights before an agent goes live.

**Recommended state:** MONITOR.

## Signal 3: The know-how to run agents sits outside the company

**What happened.** Carried from midweek. Nothing new on Thursday or Friday.

**Unit of analysis.** Finance teams, and one pharma marketing vendor's product.

**Evidence**
- Avalara, July 21: "76% lack dedicated in-house expertise to understand how their AI agents work, relying on IT or vendors." IT means information technology.
- [Pharmaceutical Commerce on Doceree, July 21](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/inside-semmelweis-docerees-ai-bet-on-pharma-marketing): recommendations are "advisory, human-approved, auditable and designed to be ready for medical, legal and regulatory review." The article does not say who approves.

**Evidence strength:** LOW. EARLY INDICATOR. One survey line and one vendor story.

**Obvious explanation.** Early know-how always sits with the builders.

**Hypothesis affected.** "Agencies may need a credible client-facing capability without hiring a complete executive function or buying another disconnected tool." Secondary dependency: Meaning and Context.

**Direction:** COMPLICATES. The gap is real, but vendors are filling it first.

**Pattern basis.** No pattern yet.

**Challenge.** "Human-approved" may be a compliance phrase, not a designed role. What would make us wrong: pharma companies hiring their own agent operations roles.

**Recommended state:** MONITOR.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: Ownership is being settled at the wrong level.** Chief executives hand accountability down to profit and loss owners. Finance teams hand know-how out to vendors. Both skip the link between the agent and the result. The 16% who expect the approving executive to take the blame show where the gap lands: on the person who signed the budget, not the person who can change the system. That is ownership without authority, and it matches his audience condition exactly.

**KEY INSIGHT: A governance tool is the easy purchase that postpones the hard decision.** A control tower lets a company say its agents are governed without saying who runs them. If that spreads, buyers may ask "which dashboard?" instead of "who owns this?" That hides the gap rather than closing it.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **Strongest alternative:** an ordinary rollout. Visibility comes first, and ownership follows once incidents force it.
- **Counterevidence:** most respondents say ownership is settled. On July 24 the European Union [published AI Act changes](https://lawandtechnology.eu/en/digital-omnibus-on-ai-official-journal-regulation-2026-1744/) pushing high-risk obligations to "2 December 2027 for stand-alone systems and from 2 August 2028" for embedded ones. That eases outside pressure to name owners. On July 23, two members of Congress [introduced a bill](https://lieu.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/reps-lieu-and-moran-introduce-bill-require-kill-switch-ai-systems-can) requiring developers to "maintain the technical ability to throttle, suspend, or fully shut down a covered AI system." It puts stop authority on builders and government, not on companies using AI.
- **What would make us wrong:** a pharma survey showing clear agent ownership through existing review.

## Trigger

- A pharma or life sciences survey asking who answers for an agent's error.
- A named pharma company publishing who can stop an agent, not just who "governs AI."
- A governance vendor requiring a named owner with stop rights.
- A Doceree customer or agency saying who approves and who can override.

## Value

- **Fast value:** "Name the person who can switch this agent off today, and the person who answers if it is wrong. Are they the same? Can the second one change the system?"
- **Deeper asset:** not yet. The evidence supports a question, not a diagnostic.
- **Learning question:** in pharma, does medical, legal, and regulatory review settle who owns an agent's output, or only who approves content?
- **Long-term value:** if the gap holds in pharma, the question becomes a short ownership check whose answers feed his bounded diagnostic.

## Decision

- **What changed:** agent ownership fails at the link between tool and outcome, and blame rolls up to whoever signed the budget.
- **Recommended state:** INVESTIGATE.
- **Why:** three independent sources agree, but none is pharma.
- **What moves it next:** one pharma source showing the gap moves it to TALK. One showing no gap moves it back to MONITOR.
