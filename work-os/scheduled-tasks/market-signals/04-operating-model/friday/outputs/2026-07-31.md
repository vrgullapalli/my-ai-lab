## The short answer

The main belief got stronger. Agents go live before anyone answers for them, and the gap gets filled by whoever deployed the agent or granted its access. Neither owns the result. Many companies also let agents approve transactions they cannot trace.

Pharma showed up at the top, not in commercial work. Two of five large drugmakers tied AI to how work runs. Neither said who runs it.

## Signal 1: The default owner is whoever deployed the agent or granted its access

**What happened**
- Observed: surveys on July 29 and 30 found ownership forming by default, and most companies unable to check what agents do.
- Interpretation: ownership settles next to the tool. The link to the result stays unowned.

**Unit of analysis.** Security, engineering, information technology, and compliance staff at large companies, across industries. Not pharma. Any read-across is my inference.

**Evidence**
- [Okta, July 29](https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/global-ciso-insights-2026/), security chiefs in six countries: "Nearly the same amount (20%) leave agent management to the team that deployed them on an ad hoc basis." Observed, self-reported.
- [1Password, via Help Net Security, July 29](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/29/1password-ai-agent-governance/), 1,000 staff at large United States firms. A 1Password product leader: "Accountability should actually sit with whoever authorized the agent's access." A vendor's opinion.
- New since midweek: [Pathlock, July 30](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/enterprises-are-handing-ai-agents-financial-workflows-while-more-than-half-cant-fully-verify-their-actions-302838770.html), 286 information technology, compliance, and security decision makers: "79% of organizations have no dedicated AI governance team or officer" and "52% cannot verify actions AI agents execute across business systems." Only 13% could investigate a flagged action in real time, yet 28% let agents approve transactions. Self-reported, from a vendor that sells the fix.
- Counterevidence: most respondents say something is in place. No survey tests whether a named owner acts.

**Evidence strength:** MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR. Six independent sponsors in two weeks: Avalara, Booz Allen, BCG, 1Password, Okta, Pathlock. All self-reported. Most sell a fix.

**Obvious explanation.** Agents are new. Org charts lag new tools.

**Hypothesis affected.** "The pieces have owners. The connections often do not." Territory: T03 System Reconstruction, because no one can answer for what no one can trace. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.

**Direction:** STRENGTHENS. The gap is not only "no one named." Agents hold authority most companies cannot trace afterward.

**Pattern basis.** Repeating and broadening, not speeding up. Now compliance staff show it too. Still no pharma source.

**Recommended state:** INVESTIGATE.

**Hypothesis affected.** "Organizations keep adding technology to a coordination and meaning problem." Territory: T06 Human-Governed Agentic Operating Systems. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.

**Direction:** COMPLICATES. Software that records an owner and a stop switch closes part of the gap without an advisor. But it names the product owner, not the owner of the business result.

**Pattern basis.** Repeats last week's ServiceNow signal, with ownership now part of the pitch. Too early to call a market.

## Signal 3: Drugmakers write AI into how they operate, and do not say who runs it

**What happened**
- Observed: GSK and Bristol Myers Squibb tied AI to company-wide savings or "rewiring." AstraZeneca, Sanofi, and AbbVie did not.
- Interpretation: AI enters pharma plans at the top as a cost lever, before anyone says who owns the redesigned work.

**Unit of analysis.** Five large drugmakers' second-quarter results, July 27 to 31. Company level, not commercial teams.

**Evidence**
- [GSK, July 28](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/gsk--gsk/2nd-quarter-results/9691506): "Cost savings are expected to be enabled by technology and AI and generated by streamlining support services and process redesign." Target: £1.9 billion a year by 2029. [STAT, July 28](https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/28/gsk-earnings-report-luke-miels-strategy-late-stage-clinical-trials/): "Miels would not say Tuesday how many jobs would be cut." Luke Miels is GSK's chief executive.
- New since midweek: [Bristol Myers Squibb slides, July 30](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000014272/000001427226000018/q22026earningspresentati.htm): "Rewiring how we operate" and "Expanding AI to help teams move faster and operate more efficiently." Company's own words. A [call summary, July 30](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/bristol-myers-squibb-company-q2-031854025.html), adds nothing on who runs it.
- Counterevidence: [AstraZeneca, July 27](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/astrazeneca-results-h1-and-q2-2026), [Sanofi, July 30](https://www.sanofi.com/en/media-room/press-releases/2026/2026-07-30-05-30-00-3335767), and [AbbVie, July 31](https://news.abbvie.com/2026-07-31-AbbVie-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results) said nothing about AI in operations. [Sanofi's slides](https://www.sanofi.com/assets/dotcom/content-app/events/quaterly-results/2026/2026-q2-2026-results/2026_07_30_Sanofi_Q2_2026_Results.pdf) mention AI only as partner research platforms.

**Evidence strength:** LOW. EARLY INDICATOR. Two companies, a few lines each, written for investors.

**Obvious explanation.** Cost programs always name technology. "AI" may label an ordinary savings drive.

**Hypothesis affected.** "AI deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Territory: T01 Decision Integrity Under Complexity. Secondary dependency: Use.

**Direction:** COMPLICATES. The midweek trigger was met: a second drugmaker named AI. But three of five did not, and neither of the two described the workflow.

**Pattern basis.** Emerging. Two of five is a start, not a trend.

**Recommended state:** MONITOR.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: The default owners can change the system but do not own the result.** Last week, blame rolled up to the budget approver, who owns the result but cannot change the system. This week, ownership forms around the deployer or access-granter, who can change the system but does not own the result. Inferred. So "name an owner" is the wrong fix. Connect the two people.

**KEY INSIGHT: Ownership fails without a record behind it.** At Pathlock's respondents, 28% let agents approve transactions and 13% can investigate in real time. An owner who cannot find out what the agent did cannot answer for it. Tracing comes before accountability. For a pharma commercial leader: "If the agent changed a target list last week, can anyone show why?" Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: In pharma, three groups may each hold one piece.** Finance owns the AI savings. A vendor runs commercial agents (IQVIA claimed "294 agents deployed across 90 use cases" on [July 28](https://finance.biggo.com/news/US_IQV_2026-07-28)). The brand leader owns the result. Nothing says who connects them. Inferred.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **Strongest alternative:** vendors sponsor surveys showing gaps they sell tools for. The real problem may be Trust and Control, with ownership following once tools show what runs.
- **Counterevidence:** most respondents say ownership exists. Three of five drugmakers were silent. Pharma's medical, legal, and regulatory review may already assign sign-off.
- **What would make us wrong:** a pharma commercial agent with a named business owner who can see the trail and stop it.

## Trigger

- A pharma or life sciences survey asking who answers for an agent's error.
- A drugmaker naming an owner with authority over its AI-enabled redesign.
- An IQVIA client or agency saying who monitors vendor agents and who can stop them.
- A pharma customer saying a governance tool made it name an owner with stop rights.

## Value

- **Fast value:** "Three names and one clock. Who granted this agent its access? Who is listed as its owner? Who answers for the result? If it did something wrong yesterday, how long until you know what and why?" Three different names, or a long clock, show the gap.
- **Deeper asset:** not yet. The questions are sharper, but no pharma buyer has answered them.
- **Learning question:** when a vendor agent changes a commercial recommendation, is anyone at the pharma company named as its owner, and can that person see why and stop it?
- **Long-term value:** if pharma shows the gap, these questions open his bounded diagnostic, and answers across companies become his proof.

## Decision

- **What changed:** default owners can change the system but do not own the result, and pharma is writing AI into plans without naming owners.
- **Recommended state:** INVESTIGATE.
- **Why:** six sponsors agree and pharma is moving at the top, but no pharma evidence on agent ownership itself.
- **What moves it next:** a pharma source showing the gap moves it to TALK. One showing clear ownership through existing review moves it to MONITOR.
