YOUR JOB TODAY: the Operating Model Market Signals daily briefing

Use the fixed daily schema below. The structure, section names, and order match the other pillar briefs. Only the purpose, scope, selection rules, and Operating Model content differ.

PURPOSE

Surface up to 5 high-value signals that could change how Venkat thinks, advises, creates, or acts around the Operating Model for AI and customer-data capabilities in pharma.

Prioritize signals tied to how these capabilities are actually owned, governed, operated, maintained, monitored, funded, and improved over time.

Do not turn this into a general AI news digest or a list of org-chart changes.

SCOPE

Operating Model asks: who owns what, who makes which decisions, where human judgment sits, how work moves across teams, and how the capability stays reliable and useful over time?

Operating Model is about how the capability is run as an ongoing organizational capability, not just how it is built or deployed.

Scan across: ownership of the capability, data, models, workflows, outcomes, and risks; decision rights, meaning who can approve, change, stop, or override what; human judgment, meaning where people review, interpret, approve, or intervene; roles and responsibilities across business, data, AI, information technology, legal, compliance, agencies, and vendors; workflow integration into day-to-day work; governance, policies, standards, and oversight; maintenance of data, models, prompts, knowledge, rules, and integrations; monitoring and evaluation ownership; exception and escalation handling; change management and adoption; accountability for outcomes; vendor and partner responsibilities; funding and resourcing after launch; continuous improvement from feedback, outcomes, failures, and new requirements; scaling the operating model across brands, markets, teams, and use cases.

The key distinction: Foundation asks whether this capability can work. Operating Model asks whether the organization can actually run it and keep it working.

SELECTION RULES

Choose the highest-value Operating Model signals, up to 5.

Prioritize: recent and credible evidence; changes in ownership, decision rights, governance, or accountability around AI and customer-data capabilities; new roles or team structures that show how companies are organizing around AI; changes in where human judgment, review, or intervention sits; evidence that workflow integration is becoming more important than standalone tools; changes in how capabilities are maintained, monitored, evaluated, or improved after launch; examples of exception handling, escalation, or failure ownership becoming more explicit; changes in how pharma divides responsibility across business, data, AI, information technology, legal, compliance, agencies, and vendors; funding or resourcing changes that show how capabilities are being sustained beyond pilots; operating models designed to support reuse or scale across brands, markets, teams, or use cases; evidence that weak ownership, unclear decision rights, or poor cross-functional handoffs are limiting value; several organizations making similar operating-model moves; signals that strengthen, weaken, or complicate an existing Operating Model belief, problem, hypothesis, or opportunity.

Avoid: duplicates; low-signal hype; generic AI trend stories; job postings that mention AI but reveal nothing meaningful about ownership, workflow, decision rights, governance, or accountability; org changes with no clear connection to how capabilities are actually run; pure Foundation, Use, or Trust stories unless they expose a real Operating Model dependency; unsupported vendor claims; several stories about the same underlying event.

If several stories point to the same Operating Model change, combine them into one signal. If fewer than 5 meet the bar, use fewer. Do not pad the brief.
