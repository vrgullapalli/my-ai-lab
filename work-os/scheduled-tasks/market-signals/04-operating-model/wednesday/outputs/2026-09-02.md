## 1. The vendor runs the work. The drugmaker answers for it.

**What appears to be changing**
- Running the work and answering for it are splitting across two companies. The vendor operates the workflow; the drugmaker stays on the hook for what comes out. Inferred.
- Lundbeck appears to hold the decision to expand, one function at a time. Who can stop a live workflow is not said.

**Evidence so far**
- [MM+M (Medical Marketing and Media), August 31](https://www.mmm-online.com/news/eversana-expanded-ai-agency-platform-lundbeck-going-all-in/), interview with Lundbeck chief commercial officer Shae Maughmer. The reporter writes that the rollout "is stepwise, guided by results from a series of internal pilots run on the platform." Her test: "not, hey, where do we apply AI, but where can we use AI to accelerate our commercial model." On success a year out: "I go back to the North Star in terms of what the objective is: using AI to accelerate the commercial model." And: "Lundbeck has not disclosed performance data from those pilots." Observed.
- Same article: Apnimed's chief commercial officer told the vendor's client event, "With AI Agency, Eversana and Google have burned the boats." The article does not say Apnimed is a client.
- [Medical Affairs Professional Society, September 1](https://medicalaffairs.org/elevate-articles/ai-audit-framework-medical-affairs-professionals/), an audit framework written by compliance leaders: "Pharma companies remain accountable for AI outputs, even when AI systems or services are provided by third-party vendors and partners." It treats disclosure "across the company and its supply chain" as "a foundational control rather than an optional nicety." The date comes from the search index; the page shows none. The full framework is for members only, so any audit or exit terms are unknown.
- [SupportNinja, August 28](https://www.supportninja.com/articles/future-of-ai-needs-humans-hitl-manifesto), an outsourcing firm, not pharma, sells human review itself as a managed service. Its chief executive asks: "What authority do they have to act?" Even the human layer can be rented.
- Background, [Lundbeck, March 10](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lundbeck-appoints-chief-ai-officer-302709214.html): its chief AI officer reports to the chief executive and will "establish strong governance and responsible AI practices." How that role touches the EVERSANA work is not said.

**Unit of analysis**
One drugmaker's United States commercial organization and its vendor. One professional society's guidance for medical affairs. One non-pharma outsourcing firm.

**Hypothesis affected**
"The pieces have owners. The connections often do not." Territory: Human-Governed Agentic Operating Systems. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.

**Direction:** COMPLICATES. Friday's worry was that the vendor might own the connections. The picture is now sharper: the vendor operates them, the drugmaker owns the consequences, and no public source joins the two.

**Evidence strength:** MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR. A named buyer on the record, plus an independent professional body. No contract terms, pause holder, or outcome data.

**Is it a pattern?** Repeating for a second week: the same event, plus a new, independent voice on accountability. Counterevidence: Lundbeck has an inside AI chief and gates expansion itself. That may be the missing owner. Unconfirmed.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: drug law already puts accountability on the drugmaker. The society is restating it.
- What else could explain it: a careful rollout of regulated functions, not an ownership design.
- What would make us wrong: Lundbeck saying who can pause each workflow, and that review runs in its own system.
- Outside this lens? Partly Trust and Control: whether the review itself works.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- Any further write-up of the late-August EVERSANA and Google client event naming who at Lundbeck approves AI-made content. Background: [pharmaphorum, August 25](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/lundbeck-taps-eversanas-ai-agency-commercialisation), which EVERSANA owns, said more details would "likely be shared" there and that it would attend.
- Any sign that Lundbeck's chief AI officer governs the EVERSANA workflows.
- Apnimed confirmed or denied as a client.

**State:** MONITOR CLOSELY. The client event was only a week ago, and more accounts of it may land before Friday.

## 2. Small companies are pulling data and AI ownership inside

**What appears to be changing**
At two small companies, work moved from outside vendors to one inside owner. Interesting signal. No pattern yet.

**Evidence so far**
- [Immunic, September 1](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/immunic-appoints-chitrang-dave-as-chief-data-and-digital-officer), a late-stage biotech, created a chief data and digital officer who reports to the chief executive. The role will build "a unified organization spanning data science, data engineering, data management, and digital capabilities" to support "commercial readiness." Observed, as announced.
- [Tellius, September 1](https://www.tellius.com/resources/blog/how-agios-pharmaceuticals-runs-ai-native-commercial-analytics-at-launch-scale), a vendor's recap of a webinar with Agios. Before launch, its "handful of people" "relied on offshore vendor for answers a couple of days later," and dashboards "ran through a multi-vendor chain." Its analytics lead: "We wanted more control on the semantic layer" and "to build the business context into it." Claimed by the vendor that sold the tool. The team still uses a vendor; what moved inside is the definitions and the answering.
- [Forbes Technology Council, August 28](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbestechcouncil/2026/08/28/adding-a-caio-heres-how-to-avoid-role-overlap-and-friction), a practitioner panel: "Put data and AI under one mandate, or fund the overlap." And: "Money is the org chart." Opinion.
- Background, [Pfizer, August 6](https://www.pfizer.com/news/articles/how_pfizer_thinks_about_ai): the center runs the platforms, "while our leaders across the enterprise own the accountability to change how their teams work." A large-company counterpoint.

**Unit of analysis**
One biotech's executive structure. One small commercial analytics team. Not pharma in general.

**Hypothesis affected**
Audience: at small and mid biotech, the economic buyer is the chief executive. Need: shared definitions and ownership before more automation. Secondary dependency: Meaning and Context.

**Direction:** TOO EARLY TO TELL. Small companies may be building the owner Venkat would help supply. Or a new inside buyer now sits between the chief executive and the problem. Inferred.

**Evidence strength:** LOW. EARLY INDICATOR. One hire and one vendor-told story.

**Is it a pattern?** No. Two kinds of evidence, two companies. Pfizer points the other way at large scale.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: any company nearing launch hires a data chief. Nothing AI-specific about it.
- What would make us wrong: Immunic staffing the role mainly through agencies, or Agios's answers still routed through vendors.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A second small or mid biotech naming one executive over data and AI, reporting to the chief executive.
- A launch team saying it moved work back from an agency, with the budget moving too.

**State:** WAIT FOR FRIDAY. Two data points do not make a pattern.

## 3. Review and decision rights are being written down before launch, on paper

**What appears to be changing**
Last week, critics said the named human answers for AI but no one defines their role. This week, advisers proposed a design: a review gate, decision rights set before launch, a named owner for failure. No drugmaker has shown one in use.

**Evidence so far**
- [BioProcess International, September 2](https://www.bioprocessintl.com/information-technology/agentic-ai-in-biopharmaceutical-commercial-analytics-benefits-risks-and-use-cases), by four Ernst & Young consultants, on biopharma commercial analytics: "Outputs should pass a defined review gate before broader distribution." And: "Humans define the decision, scope success criteria, and acceptable evidence types."
- [ZDNET, August 28](https://www.zdnet.com/article/3-surveys-uncomfortable-adopting-agentic-ai/), written by Salesforce's chief digital evangelist, summarizing older studies. Accenture proposes "clear decision rights defined before agents ever go live, not after," and a "chief agentic resource officer." KPMG: leaders pull ahead on "clear accountability, stronger governance, and real visibility into what running AI at scale actually costs." Re-reporting, not new data.
- [Forbes Technology Council, August 28](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbestechcouncil/2026/08/28/adding-a-caio-heres-how-to-avoid-role-overlap-and-friction): "Most organizations define who owns AI strategy but not who owns AI failure."
- [Pharmaceutical Executive, August 31](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/future-relationship-between-ai-field-reps), Parth Khanna, chief executive of field AI seller ACTO: "there's a world where the sales rep and MSL roles become hybridized," MSL meaning medical science liaison, "though the regulatory environment will need to catch up to that possibility." A vendor's view.

**Unit of analysis**
Biopharma commercial analytics (a consultants' proposal), large companies in general, and one vendor's view of field roles.

**Hypothesis affected**
"Reliance should be proportional to evidence." Territory: Decision Integrity Under Complexity. Secondary dependency: Trust and Control.

**Direction:** STRENGTHENS, as advice. Everyone now says decision rights must come before scale. No one shows them working.

**Evidence strength:** LOW. REINFORCING INDICATOR. Every source advises or sells.

**Is it a pattern?** Repeating for a fourth week. New this week: the talk moved from diagnosis to proposed fixes. Duplicate risk: the ZDNET piece recycles studies from earlier in the year.

**Challenge**
- Most obvious explanation: consultants selling operating model redesign.
- What would make us wrong: a drugmaker showing its reviewers reject or change a real share of AI output, with time set aside to do it.
- Outside this lens? Partly Trust and Control: what evidence the gate should require.

**Next evidence before Friday**
- A drugmaker or agency publishing the review gate for one agent before launch.
- A medical, legal, and regulatory reviewer describing how the job changed.

**State:** WAIT FOR FRIDAY. No practice to weigh yet.
