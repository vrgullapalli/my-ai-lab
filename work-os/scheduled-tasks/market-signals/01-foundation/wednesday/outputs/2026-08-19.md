## Pattern 1: Customer relationship data in rented software is being taken from health companies

**What appears to be changing**
- Two weeks ago the risk was patient data in vendor-hosted clouds (Amgen). This week the target is the customer relationship management system: where commercial teams keep records of the clinicians, hospitals, and accounts they sell to. The company rents the software but owns its settings and access.

**Evidence so far**
- Baxter, August 13, one day before the window: it found "unauthorized activity involving certain third party applications," with "no impact to manufacturing operations, customer operations, patient services or business continuity" ([Baxter statement](https://www.baxter.com/statement-regarding-cybersecurity-incident)). From a search snippet, not verified at the source. Baxter did not name the application.
- The group's claim, posted by August 14: "Over 7.1M Salesforce records containing some PII [personally identifiable information] was compromised. This is a final warning to reach out by 17 Aug 2026 before we leak" ([HookPhish, August 14](https://www.hookphish.com/blog/ransomware-group-shinyhunters-hits-baxter-international-inc/)). Claimed by criminals. Not confirmed by Baxter.
- Same group, same day, on Sharecare: "Over 3.4 million Salesforce records containing some PII [personally identifiable information] and 28GB+ [gigabytes] of internal corporate data was compromised" ([HookPhish, August 14](https://www.hookphish.com/blog/ransomware-group-shinyhunters-hits-sharecare-inc/)). Claimed. I found no confirmation from Sharecare in sources dated by August 19.
- Cook Medical, a device maker, was listed too, but "no record counts or file details have been made public" ([Breach Letter, August 14](https://breachletter.com/healthcare/ransomware-cook-medical-llc-2026-08-14/)). A day earlier the group claimed "more than 182 GB [gigabytes] of compressed data" ([Breach News, August 13](https://breachnews.com/breaches/shinyhunters-claims-baxter-breach-publishes-cook-medical-carhartt-and-sharecare-data/)). Salesforce is not named for Cook.
- Background on the method: in July, Microsoft wrote that this group "socially engineered employees into authorizing attacker-controlled connected apps within their Salesforce tenant" ([Microsoft Security blog, July 13](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/13/defending-saas-based-applications-against-shinyhunters-oauth-abuse/)). In March, Salesforce said an earlier wave was not a "vulnerability inherent to our platform" but customer settings with excessive permissions ([IT Pro, March 10](https://www.itpro.com/security/cyber-attacks/salesforce-issues-customer-alert-as-shinyhunters-group-claims-experience-cloud-breach)). How Baxter was entered is not public in anything dated by August 19.

**Unit of analysis**
- Customer relationship data held in rented software at a medical products maker and a digital health company, plus an unnamed system at a device maker. Not drug companies. That it is Salesforce data rests on the group's word.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy," and last Friday's open question: who answers for what you rely on but do not run. Primary lens: Foundation (access, permissions, vendor dependence). Secondary: Operating Model, since the gap vendors describe is a setting or an approval someone inside the company owns.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS** for health companies. **TOO EARLY TO TELL** for drug companies.

**Evidence strength: LOW to MEDIUM. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- MEDIUM that Baxter had an incident: the company said so. LOW that customer relationship records were taken: that rests on a criminal group's word. Early, because the customer relationship angle is new this week.

**Pattern basis**
- Three companies, one group, one playbook, one week. That is one campaign, not three independent signals. It counts once.
- It joins the running vendor thread (Amgen's vendor-hosted clouds, last week's billing vendor). Different vendors, different systems, same condition: the data sits in software the owner does not run.
- Contradicting evidence: Baxter says operations were untouched. Salesforce says its platform was not broken.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: a known criminal group working through Salesforce customers in many industries. Health is incidental.
- Alternative: this is a people and settings problem (staff tricked, access left open), closer to Operating Model than Foundation. Fair. But access rights on rented software are part of the foundation a commercial team stands on.
- What would make us wrong: Baxter says the application was not its customer relationship system, or the records turn out to be old contact lists.

**Watch next**
- A drug company named on the group's leak site, or disclosing an incident in its customer relationship system, before Friday.
- Baxter naming the application or the way in.
- Salesforce or Veeva sending life sciences customers a specific warning.

**State: MONITOR CLOSELY.** The group is naming health companies this week with short deadlines, so a drug company could appear before Friday, but there is nothing to act on yet.

## Pattern 2: A health records vendor learned the true size of its loss months late

**What appears to be changing**
- The company holding the data could not say for five months how many people's records sat in the environment that was entered.

**Evidence so far**
- SecurityWeek, August 19: state reports in July showed "a total of roughly 350,000." The federal health department's tracker "on Monday showed 3,371,508 affected individuals, and on Tuesday the number was updated to 3,756,469." Attackers "gained access to one of CareCloud's AWS [Amazon Web Services] environments between March 10 and March 16" ([SecurityWeek, August 19](https://www.securityweek.com/carecloud-data-breach-impact-grows-to-3-7-million-individuals/)). Observed. The article says the department confirmed the larger figure is correct.
- TechCrunch, same day: CareCloud "provides electronic medical record storage to tens of thousands of healthcare providers around the United States" ([TechCrunch, August 19](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/19/carecloud-confirms-3-7m-patients-had-their-medical-records-stolen-in-data-breach/)). Same event, counts once.

**Unit of analysis**
- One electronic health record and billing vendor serving many providers. Not a drug company.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy." Primary lens: Foundation (traceability: knowing what data lives where). Secondary: Trust and Control, since the first public number was off by ten times.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS** slightly for health vendors. **TOO EARLY TO TELL** for pharma.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM for the event, LOW for pharma. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Federal filing numbers and two news outlets. But it is one vendor.

**Pattern basis**
- Repeats last week's billing vendor case: a break-in from October 2025, reported in August. Two vendors, same shape: months between entry and a real count.
- Amgen, the one drug company in this thread, has said nothing new that I found this window.
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet in pharma.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: breach counts often grow as notices go out state by state. The jump may be paperwork, not a failure to know what was held.
- What would make us wrong: CareCloud explains the gap as a filing issue, not a gap in knowing its own data.

**Watch next**
- A drug company, or a data vendor serving one, revising a breach count upward.
- Amgen naming how many people were affected or how its cloud was entered.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Nothing here reaches pharma before Friday.
