## Pattern 1: The customer relationship system at big drug companies is being replaced, with AI added in the same motion

**What appears to be changing**
- The customer relationship management system holds the doctor, account, call, and consent records commercial teams run on. At the largest drug companies it is moving to a new platform.
- AI agents are arriving on the new system mid-move. Two big changes to the same foundation at once.

**Evidence so far**
- Veeva's results release, August 26: "Vault CRM [customer relationship management] leadership grew with more than 180 customers live, including five top 20 biopharmas. In August, two top 20 biopharmas and one large enterprise biopharma committed to Vault CRM, bringing total top 20 commitments to 12 globally." ([Veeva, August 26](https://www.veeva.com/resources/veeva-announces-fiscal-2027-second-quarter-results/)). Claimed by the company in a public results filing. Revenue was "$928.0 million," up 18% from a year ago.
- Same release: "a top 20 biopharma deployed Vault CRM [customer relationship management] and the Agentic Call Report across its full U.S. [United States] field team in the quarter." Claimed. Company not named. If true, an AI agent is past the pilot, in daily use by a whole sales force.
- Veeva's call, as reported the same day, on its chief executive Peter Gassner: "Gassner said a handful of large customers selected Salesforce, in some cases about two years ago, but those implementations have encountered delays and challenges because of the complexity of the product." And: "'We want to be your plan B,' Gassner said... Veeva CRM [customer relationship management] remains available as a backstop through the end of 2029" ([MarketBeat via Yahoo Finance, August 26](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/veeva-systems-q2-earnings-call-230419426.html)). Claimed by a direct competitor. No buyer confirmed a delay.
- Counterweight, background: in June, Salesforce said "more than 140 life sciences organizations are using its competing Agentforce Life Sciences platform," and "some of its smaller life science customers have completed onboarding in about a month" ([Drug Discovery and Development, June 1](https://www.drugdiscoverytrends.com/salesforce-lands-140-life-sciences-clients-pitches-headless-ai-to-pharma/)). Before the window. Also a seller's word.

**Unit of analysis**
- Customer relationship platforms at the 20 largest drug companies, as counted by one vendor. Not biotech. Not agencies. Not any named buyer's experience.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Organizations keep adding technology to a coordination and meaning problem," and the trigger moment "AI is added before data, definitions, ownership, or workflows are ready." Also Continuity of Meaning (territory two): how a system can change without losing the shared meaning that makes its outputs trustworthy.
- Primary lens: Foundation (platform migration, vendor dependence, implementation conditions). Secondary: Meaning and Context, since every customer match, territory rule, and consent flag has to survive the move. That second part is inferred.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS** that the ground under commercial data is moving at big drug companies. **TOO EARLY TO TELL** whether the data holds through the move.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM that the switch is happening. LOW on who is struggling. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- Medium: a public company's results, with counts it can be held to. Low: the delay story comes from a rival. Early: first time these briefs have seen the switch itself.

**Pattern basis**
- Veeva's release and call are one event. They count once.
- It links to the vendor-dependence thread (Novo Nordisk's outside AI and cloud vendors; Merck and Google Cloud as background). This is the commercial version: which vendor holds the customer system of record.
- Simple math on Veeva's own numbers: 12 of the top 20 committed, five live. So up to seven of the biggest switches are still in progress or ahead. Inferred.
- A tension inside the same call: on Falcon, its AI agent product for clinical, regulatory, and safety work, Gassner said deployments "should not require the extensive data mapping, system cutovers or extract-transform-load work often associated with enterprise software implementations" (same MarketBeat report). Claimed, and about research work, not commercial. It is the opposite of the claim that AI needs the data work done first.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: Veeva had a good quarter and said so. Customers are moving because the old version has an end date, not because anything broke.
- Alternative: "delays" are normal for any large system change. The real risk may be who owns the migration, which is Operating Model.
- What would make us wrong: drug companies report clean switches with records, consent, and history intact. Or Salesforce shows large pharma customers live on time.

**Watch next**
- The full call transcript: any word on what data must be moved, cleaned, or rebuilt in a switch.
- A drug company or agency, not a vendor, naming a go-live, a delay, or a split between the two systems by region.
- Drug company job posts for customer relationship system migration or commercial data migration leads.
- Salesforce's next quarterly results: any count of large pharma customers actually live.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** The numbers will not change in two days, and the fuller call record should help Friday.

## Pattern 2: Health companies' business systems keep getting hit, and one hit stopped orders

**What appears to be changing**
- For three weeks the thread was data taken from systems health companies rent. This week a second kind of hit: a company's business applications went down, and it could not ship. Different shape, same weak spot: the systems the business runs on, not the products.

**Evidence so far**
- Boston Scientific, a medical device maker, in a filing reported August 26: the incident "caused, and is expected to continue to cause, disruptions and limitations of access to certain of the company's information systems and business applications that support aspects of the Company's operations, including the ability to process and ship customer orders." It said the "full scope, nature and impacts, including operational and financial impacts" are unknown ([The Register, August 26](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/26/boston-scientific-discloses-global-disruption-in-ongoing-cyberattack/5292641)). Observed. The Register reports no group had claimed it and the company had not said whether data was taken.
- Baxter, August 26 update: "Baxter has not confirmed the nature of the stolen data, only stating that the attack involved certain third-party applications." The same piece dates the public leak of the files to August 19 ([HIPAA Journal, named for the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), August 26](https://www.hipaajournal.com/shinyhunters-baxter-international-data-breach/)). No new facts, and still no confirmation that customer relationship records were the target.

**Unit of analysis**
- Business systems at one device maker (outage) and one medical products maker (data taken from rented applications). Not drug companies. I found no drug company named on a leak site, or disclosing an incident, inside the window.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy," and the open question from the last two weeks: who answers for what you rely on but do not run. Primary lens: Foundation (security, production reliability, dependence on business applications). Secondary: Operating Model.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS** for health companies. **TOO EARLY TO TELL** for drug companies.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Boston Scientific's words come from its own filing. But its cause is unknown, so it cannot yet be tied to rented software or stolen logins.

**Pattern basis**
- Fourth week running for health companies. Amgen's vendor-hosted clouds, a billing vendor, Baxter's third-party applications, now an outage at Boston Scientific.
- Do not merge them. Baxter is a claimed theft through rented apps. Boston Scientific is an outage with no known cause. Same industry, no proven shared method.
- Contradicting evidence: still no drug company in the thread since Amgen.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: health companies are big targets, like every other large company. A busy month, not a shift.
- Alternative: Boston Scientific is a plain ransomware case in its own network, saying nothing about rented systems or customer data.
- What would make us wrong: that alternative proves true, and Baxter says its customer records were untouched.

**Watch next**
- Boston Scientific naming the cause, and whether a rented application or a stolen login was involved.
- A group claiming the Boston Scientific attack, or posting its data.
- Baxter naming the application.
- A drug company named on a leak site, or disclosing an incident in a customer relationship or data platform.

**State: MONITOR CLOSELY.** Boston Scientific's cause could come out any day, and if it runs through rented software the thread widens from theft to outage.
