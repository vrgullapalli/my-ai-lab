## Pattern 1: Trusted access keeps reaching health companies' rented systems, and the kinds of access are multiplying

**What appears to be changing**
- Last week: one group, one phone trick, one sign-on system. This week: a second group uses the trick, and a vendor's stored key opened a data connection. The weak point is any trusted access, human or machine, that reaches customer data in rented software.

**Evidence so far**
- Arctic Wolf, a security firm, on a cluster it calls PREY-0058. Its bulletin is dated September 3, one day before this window, so it is background: attackers get in by phone "without exploiting any software vulnerability," then steal data, "often completing the full intrusion-to-extortion cycle before security teams detect initial access" ([Arctic Wolf, September 3](https://arcticwolf.com/resources/blog/security-bulletin-active-cloud-data-theft-and-extortion-campaign-targeting-microsoft-365-and-saas-platforms/)).
- Inside the window: "These attacks most frequently target Directors, Vice Presidents, and other executive staff." Targets cluster in sectors including "healthcare and pharmaceuticals," ending in "bulk collection and exfiltration from SharePoint, OneDrive, Exchange and other SaaS [rented software] providers such as Box" ([Help Net Security, September 8](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/08/vishing-microsoft-365-data-theft-extortion/)). The Hacker News adds "the absence of endpoint malware deployment," and says the group names "do not correspond to a single proven actor identity" ([The Hacker News, September 7](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/microsoft-365-attackers-use-help-desk.html)). Observed by an independent firm. The sector list comes from fake login addresses copying real company names, not confirmed victims.
- Veradigm, formerly Allscripts, in a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission on September 8: "an unauthorized party obtained credentials from the vendor's environment to a Company application programming interface used by the vendor to provide services." The thief downloaded patient personal data, in some cases Social Security numbers ([Severity Daily, quoting the filing, September 8](https://severitydaily.com/veradigm-8-k-item-8-01-vendor-api-credentials-social-security-numbers/)). Observed. A criminal group, The Gentlemen, claimed it on September 5 and says it holds 3.5 million patient records ([BleepingComputer, September 9](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/veradigm-discloses-patient-data-breach-after-gentlemen-gang-claims-attack/)). Claimed. The same article lists "biopharmaceutical firms" among Veradigm's users.
- Medela, a Swiss maker of breastfeeding and wound care products: ShinyHunters listed it September 6 with a September 8 deadline, but "has not disclosed what it allegedly stole" ([BreachNews, September 6](https://breachnews.com/breaches/shinyhunters-names-medela-in-data-breach-and-extortion-claim/)). Claimed only.
**Unit of analysis**
- Access to rented business software at health companies: staff sign-ins to email and file stores, and a vendor's machine key to one data connection (Veradigm). Not a drug maker's customer relationship system. No drug maker named.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy." Primary: Foundation (who and what holds access, how far one key reaches, whether a bulk download gets noticed). Secondary: Operating Model (who owns vendor keys and the help desk).
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** Last Friday the thread moved to "no one sees it leave." Now more kinds of keys open the door, and an outside firm says the theft often ends before anyone notices.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** An independent research firm and a company filing, not just the criminals' word. But the pharma link is a sector label, and Veradigm's affected customers are not named.

**Pattern basis**
- Seventh week running in health. Arctic Wolf's many write-ups count once. So does Wednesday's re-coverage of last week's sector alert ([Industrial Cyber, September 9](https://industrialcyber.co/medical/health-isac-warns-shinyhunters-targets-health-sector-with-vishing-credential-theft-and-mfa-bypass-tactics/)). Veradigm is a new company and a new route.
- Against: Veradigm says the stolen key "provided access only through that limited interface" and not to its "broader network, servers, databases, or other systems" (BleepingComputer, above). A narrow key limited the damage. That cuts against "one key opens everything."
- Against: ShinyHunters also claims Florida's driver records, through "a password-reset flaw" ([BleepingComputer, September 8](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/shinyhunters-hackers-claim-breach-of-florida-david-dmv-database/)). The group hits every sector. Health is not special.
- Out, again: Boston Scientific's outage was its own systems (see below).

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: criminals go where logins are weak, in every industry.
- Alternative: this is a help desk and identity problem (Operating Model, Trust and Control). Fair. The Foundation part is narrower: what each key can reach, and whether anything flags a bulk download.
- What would make us wrong: a health company reports catching a bulk download as it happened, or the second group's pharma victims turn out few, with little taken.

**Watch next**
- A drug maker named on any of these groups' leak sites or in a filing.
- Veradigm naming the vendor, or its data appearing after the group's claimed Friday, September 11 deadline.
- A health company or security firm naming a customer relationship system, or a vendor's data connection key, as the way in.

**State: MONITOR CLOSELY.** A group aimed at pharma directors and vice presidents is active now; a named drug maker could surface any day.

## Pattern 2: New doors for agents into pharma customer systems, and the first buyer word that agents drift

**What appears to be changing**
- Sellers are building ways for agents to reach commercial data from outside the core system. One drug maker using agents widely says they need watching.

**Evidence so far**
- Salesforce, September 8: its new "Life Sciences MCP" (Model Context Protocol, a standard way for agents to connect to software) extends its life sciences system "beyond the Salesforce UI [screen] to any channel, app, or agent environment," naming "Slack, Microsoft Teams, WhatsApp, a CRO [contract research organization] partner portal, or Claude Code." It "will be available in 2027" ([Salesforce, September 8](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/proactive-intelligence-life-sciences/)). Claimed. A promise, not a deployment. The same post: "Until today, agents were simply layered on top of fragmented processes and data, resulting in compliance gaps and re-work." A seller naming the gap.
- Lundbeck's chief commercial officer, Shae Maughmer, at a client event run by Eversana, its agent vendor: "these agents, they're not always set it and forget it. And we've actually had a situation which, as the tool learned, it learned the wrong things." Done carelessly, "it can actually create a lot of inefficiency" ([pharmaphorum, September 4](https://pharmaphorum.com/sales-marketing/one-year-later-eversana-plans-go-beyond-ai-agency)). A buyer voice. Note: pharmaphorum says it is "owned by EVERSANA."
- Background, August 25, before the window: Lundbeck widened Eversana's platform into "marketing, medical communications, market access and media operations" ([Lundbeck, August 25](https://www.lundbeck.com/us/newsroom/2026/lundbeck-and-eversana-announce-strategic-partnership)).
- Background, August 25: the Model Context Protocol "standardizes how an agent discovers and requests metrics, but says nothing about whether governed metrics exist behind it" ([CTO Magazine, August 25](https://ctomagazine.com/semantic-layer-ai-agents-iceberg-mcp/)).

**Unit of analysis**
- One seller's planned agent connection to pharma commercial systems, and one drug maker's commercial agent rollout. Not the market.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "AI [artificial intelligence] deployment advances faster than the organization can define value, redesign the workflow, and prove the result." Primary: Foundation (each new connection is another key into customer data, and agents that learn need monitoring). Secondary: Trust and Control (what an agent has earned permission to do).
- Direction: **TOO EARLY TO TELL.** We do not know what Lundbeck's agent learned wrong, or how it was caught.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** One launch due in 2027. One sentence from a buyer at a vendor event, reported by the vendor's own outlet.

**Pattern basis**
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet. Neither piece shows an agent reaching customer data in live use.
- Link to Pattern 1, inferred: Veradigm shows what a vendor-held key to a data connection can pull. Every agent connection is that kind of key.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: a seller launching features, and a buyer candid about early bumps.
- Outside-lens check: "learned the wrong things" may be a use or workflow problem, not a data foundation one. A vendor blog cites a consulting firm's split of where value gaps come from: "10% algorithms, 20% technology and data, and 70% people, process, and workflow change" ([IQVIA, September 9](https://www.iqvia.com/blogs/2026/09/ai-readiness-and-operating-models)). Reported, not tested.
- What would make us wrong: Lundbeck explains the error as a content issue caught by normal review, with no data or monitoring gap.

**Watch next**
- Lundbeck or Eversana saying what went wrong, and whether a check or a person caught it.
- A drug maker naming an agent connection to its customer relationship system in live use.
- Salesforce or Veeva saying how agent connections are logged, limited, and shut off.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Nothing here moves in two days.
