## Pattern 1: Stolen logins keep reaching health companies' business data, and the companies learn of it from the thieves

**What appears to be changing**
- Two things got clearer this week. The data taken includes doctor records. And the theft is found after it ends, not while it runs.

**Evidence so far**
- McKesson named where it was hit: "Based on our investigation to date, we have confirmed the activity was associated with subsets of our Oncology & Multispecialty and Medical-Surgical business units" ([SecurityWeek, August 31, updated September 1](https://www.securityweek.com/mckesson-confirms-data-breach-as-attacker-deadline-looms/)). Observed.
- The timeline. McKesson's filing: "On August 25, 2026, McKesson Corporation discovered a cybersecurity incident" ([Form 8-K, a current-report filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission, August 28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927653/000092765326000247/mck-20260825.htm)). The group says it "contacted McKesson after completing the data theft on August 25 and demanded a $55,236,150 ransom" ([BleepingComputer, August 28](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/mckesson-discloses-breach-after-shinyhunters-claims-patient-data-theft/)). The dates match. That is observed. That the theft ran four days without being caught is inferred, and it rests on the group's word.
- The method, as claimed: the attackers "got in through vishing calls to McKesson employees, then used stolen credentials to take over Okta single sign-on accounts," then "moved into McKesson's Salesforce and Snowflake environments and pulled out about a terabyte of data over four days" ([Help Net Security, August 31](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/08/31/healthcare-company-mckesson-data-breach/)). "Vishing" means phone scams. Claimed. McKesson's own words, as quoted in the coverage I read, do not name these platforms.
- New: Novocure's filing, September 1. The exposed data included "internal Company patient ID numbers for over 1,400 U.S. [United States] patient records (these ID numbers are only used internally and no patient names or other identifying data for these was exposed)," plus "general contact information for healthcare providers we work with" and staff contact details ([Form 8-K, Securities and Exchange Commission, September 1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001645113/000164511326000065/nvcr-20260901.htm)). Observed.
- Same day, the group "claimed responsibility for the attack last month and leaked a 33GB [gigabyte] archive of files." Novocure "has yet to attribute this breach" ([BleepingComputer, September 1](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/novocure-data-breach-affects-more-than-1-400-cancer-patients/)). How they got in: unknown.
- A buyer-side voice, not an incident. Roche's head of cyber resilience, Jonathan Sinclair, and Ashish Gupta of the consulting firm PwC wrote for the World Economic Forum. They say cloud platforms, "consumer-led engagement and globally distributed suppliers are creating new value," but "also new dependencies and avenues for cyber disruption" ([Industrial Cyber, September 2](https://industrialcyber.co/medical/pharmaceutical-sector-urged-to-shift-cyber-resilience-focus-from-systems-to-medicine-value-chains/)). Their focus is keeping medicines moving, not customer data.

**Unit of analysis**
- Business systems at a drug distributor (McKesson) and a cancer therapy company (Novocure). The doctor records are their own customer lists. Not a drug maker. Novocure's system is unknown.

**Hypothesis affected**
- "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy," and the open question: who answers for what you rely on but do not run.
- Primary lens: Foundation (sign-on, access, and watching what data leaves a rented system). Secondary: Operating Model (who owns the help desk, and who owns the alert).
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.**

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.**
- Two companies confirmed in writing. The method, platforms, and timeline rest on the criminals' word. Novocure's method is not known.

**Pattern basis**
- Sixth week running for health companies. Novocure is a new company and a new filing, so it counts as new. This week's many McKesson stories are one event and count once.
- Different kinds of evidence point the same way: company filings, criminal claims, and a buyer-side essay on outside dependence.
- Counterevidence: Boston Scientific. SecurityWeek reported "the breach appears to be limited to some on-premises systems" ([SecurityWeek, August 31](https://www.securityweek.com/boston-scientific-still-recovering-from-cyberattack/)). That is its own network, not rented software. It stays out of this thread.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: one busy group running phone scams across every industry.
- Alternative: this is mainly an Operating Model problem, a help desk that resets a login over the phone. Fair. The Foundation part is narrower. Once one login worked, nothing in the stack seems to have flagged a terabyte leaving over four days. Inferred.
- What would make us wrong: McKesson says it caught the theft itself before the group called, or says the data came from somewhere other than these platforms.

**Watch next**
- Whether the group posts McKesson data. It threatened to publish "unless the company contacts them to start payment negotiations by September 1" (SecurityWeek, above). Reporting on a posting could show what kinds of records were taken.
- Novocure saying how access happened, or which system held the doctor contact list.
- A drug maker on the group's list, or in a filing.

**State: MONITOR CLOSELY.** The deadline has passed, and a leak could show what was taken any day.

## Pattern 2: Drug makers' shipment data can be legal but not usable, and the drug code is about to change everywhere

**What appears to be changing**
- A drug maker's data can meet the law and still fail the wholesaler who has to use it. Next, a new code format will run through nearly every system at once.

**Evidence so far**
- Cencora's Gregg Gorniak, vice president of manufacturer operations and data services, August 28. As the article puts it, Cencora uses "predictive alerts that flag incoming shipments lacking the EPCIS [Electronic Product Code Information Services, the standard shipment data] data required for receipt." That gives "a 12 to 24 hour warning window to resolve gaps with trading partners before a truck arrives at the dock" ([Pharmaceutical Commerce, August 28](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/cencoras-gregg-gorniak-on-using-ai-to-get-ahead-of-dscsa-data-gaps)). Claimed by Cencora.
- Part two, September 1. Posting data to a web portal "makes a company compliant, Gorniak notes, but not necessarily a good partner, and not operationally efficient for downstream partners who still can't get usable data into their workflows" ([Pharmaceutical Commerce, September 1](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/the-difference-between-meeting-dscsa-and-mastering-it)). The article's summary of his view.
- Cencora's Ameer Ali, August 31, on the Food and Drug Administration's move to 12-digit National Drug Codes. It affects "nearly every transaction, from ordering and shipping to receiving and billing, all at once." And: "Some manufacturers have completed their assessments and determined next steps, while others are still waiting on additional guidance before acting" ([Pharmaceutical Commerce, August 31](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/hda-2026-cencora-s-ameer-ali-on-readying-systems-for-ndc-12)). I could not confirm the date the new format takes effect.

**Unit of analysis**
- Product and shipment data sent from drug makers to one large wholesaler, as that wholesaler tells it. Not customer data. Not commercial systems.

**Hypothesis affected**
- Primary lens: Foundation (completeness, systems talking to each other, identifiers). It mirrors "customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy," but for product data.
- Secondary: Repeatability and Scale. A code change has to stay true across every system that uses it.
- Link to commercial data, inferred and not stated by the source: the drug code is also a key in the sales, claims, and specialty pharmacy data commercial teams run on. If its format changes, every match on it needs checking.
- Direction: **TOO EARLY TO TELL** for commercial data. **STRENGTHENS** the general point that "available" and "usable" are different.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.**
- A named executive at a major wholesaler. But one company, one outlet, one run-up to one conference (the Healthcare Distribution Alliance's Traceability Seminar). No drug maker spoke.

**Pattern basis**
- Interesting signal. No pattern yet. The three pieces come from one company in one interview series. They count as one voice.

**Challenge**
- Simplest reading: a company describing its own tools has reason to stress the gap.
- Outside-lens check: a portal is a choice about how to share, not a missing system. The gap may be Operating Model (who owns the feed) more than Foundation.
- What would make us wrong: a second wholesaler or a drug maker reports clean, usable feeds, or the code change proves a simple mapping vendors handle.

**Watch next**
- A second wholesaler, or a drug maker, describing the same gap in its own words.
- The confirmed start date for the 12-digit code, and whether commercial data vendors say their files will change.
- Drug maker job posts naming the code change in data or analytics roles.

**State: WAIT FOR FRIDAY.** Nothing here moves in two days.
