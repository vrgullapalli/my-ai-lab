## Signal 1: Stolen logins open health companies' rented systems, and the theft is seen only when the thief calls

**What happened**
- Observed: a sector body says the method has worked "in several recent cases," and two named experts say the theft is hard to see.
- Inferred: nothing seems to watch data leave a rented system under a valid login.

**Unit of analysis:** rented business software (customer relationship systems, data platforms) reached through single sign-on at health companies. Not a drug maker.

**Evidence**
- Health-ISAC (the Health Information Sharing and Analysis Center), sent to members August 28, posted September 3: the group "has been successful in several recent cases by bypassing multi-factor authentication (MFA) to rapidly pivot from single sign-on(SSO) platforms to connected SaaS [software as a service, meaning rented] applications" ([Health-ISAC, September 3](https://health-isac.org/urgent-threat-alert-shinyhunters-vishing-campaigns-and-domain-impersonation/)). Independent.
- Scott Gee, American Hospital Association: "An employee account moving around an environment looks 'normal' and often goes unnoticed" ([Healthcare Dive, September 2](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/mckesson-confirms-data-theft-cyberattack-involving-third-party-apps/829435/)). Independent.
- Ian Gray, Flashpoint, a threat research firm: "organizations often remain unaware of the breach until the extortionists make contact" ([CyberScoop, August 31](https://cyberscoop.com/mckesson-data-theft-extortion-attack-shinyhunters/)). Independent.
- McKesson, rechecked: it "discovered a cybersecurity incident" on August 25 ([filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission, August 28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927653/000092765326000247/mck-20260825.htm)). The group says it took data "between August 21 and August 25" via "Salesforce and Snowflake environments" ([BleepingComputer, August 28](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/mckesson-discloses-breach-after-shinyhunters-claims-patient-data-theft/)). Claimed.
- Novocure, rechecked: "general contact information for healthcare providers" was exposed ([filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission, September 1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001645113/000164511326000065/nvcr-20260901.htm)).
- New Thursday: BreachNews, a small tracking site, said the group "has now published downloadable data for McKesson" ([BreachNews, September 3](https://breachnews.com/breaches/shinyhunters-targets-mckesson-neogen-jack-henry-and-elekta-in-new-breach-claims/)). No larger outlet confirmed it by Friday.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. CONFIRMING EVIDENCE for the method.** A sector body and two experts now back what was the criminals' account. Platforms and posted files remain claims.

**Obvious explanation:** one busy group, one phone scam, many victims.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy." Primary: Foundation (sign-on, access, watching what leaves). Secondary: Operating Model (who owns the alert).
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS.** Last week: "data gets taken." Now: data can leave under a valid login, and no alarm fires.

**Pattern basis**
- Sixth week running in health. McKesson's many stories count once. The alert and experts are new, independent evidence.
- Against: Boston Scientific has "growing confidence" its intrusion "was limited to select internal-facing IT [information technology] infrastructure" ([Becker's Hospital Review, September 3](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/cybersecurity/boston-scientific-resumes-shipping-most-products-after-cyberattack)). Different path. Out.
- Against: the same group hit Jack Henry, a banking software firm; BreachNews reports "fewer than 10 clients" affected. Not health-only, and damage can be small.
- Stable, not accelerating.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: The alarm is the ransom note**
- A valid login pulling data looks like work. The gap is less "can someone get in" than "would anyone notice four days of data leaving." At McKesson, apparently not. Inferred, from the dates.
- Commercial owns the customer relationship system. Security owns alerts. Who watches the doctor file leave? Often no one. Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: The login is the foundation piece that matters most**
- If single sign-on is "the control plane," each agent added to that login widens what one phone call reaches. Last week's "one login, two doors" is now the sector's framing. Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: Before launch, the buyer hires a builder, not a check**
- Immunic hired someone to build its foundation. A bounded diagnostic may fit later, once there is something to test. One case. Inferred.

- **LONG-TERM VALUE:** the question, then a few answers, then a check a buyer runs before adding agents to the same login.
