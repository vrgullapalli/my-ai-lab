## Signal 1: Customer relationship data in rented software keeps being taken from health companies

**What happened**
- Observed: a medical products maker confirmed unauthorized activity in "third-party applications." Criminals say it was Salesforce records, and files are now posted.
- Inferred: the weak spot now reaches the system commercial teams use daily. Not yet at a drug company.

**Unit of analysis:** customer relationship records in rented software at Baxter (medical products) and Sharecare (digital health), plus data at Cook Medical (devices). Not drug companies.

**Evidence**
- Baxter, as reported August 14: "unauthorized activity involving certain third-party applications," and "no evidence that the activity affected products" ([24x7 Magazine, August 14](https://24x7mag.com/standards/safety/cybersecurity/baxter-identifies-cybersecurity-incident-involving-third-party-applications/)). Observed.
- The group, August 14: "Over 7.1M Salesforce records containing some PII [personally identifiable information] was compromised" ([HookPhish, August 14](https://www.hookphish.com/blog/ransomware-group-shinyhunters-hits-baxter-international-inc/)). On Sharecare: "Over 3.4 million Salesforce records containing some PII [personally identifiable information] and 28GB+ [gigabytes] of internal corporate data was compromised" ([HookPhish, August 14](https://www.hookphish.com/blog/ransomware-group-shinyhunters-hits-sharecare-inc/)). Claimed. Neither company confirmed the counts by August 21.
- August 18 update: "ShinyHunters has now published the alleged Baxter data." The same page says the group posted "more than 182 GB [gigabytes] of compressed data stolen from Cook Medical," and the claims "have not been independently verified" ([Breach News, updated August 18](https://breachnews.com/breaches/shinyhunters-claims-baxter-breach-publishes-cook-medical-carhartt-and-sharecare-data/)). Files posted, not proven real.
- Counterweight, Friday: the same group took "personal information from 1.6 million RingCentral accounts," a phone company ([Privacy Guides, August 21](https://www.privacyguides.org/news/2026/08/21/data-breach-roundup-august-14-20-2026/)). Health is one industry of many.
- Background, July 13: the group "socially engineered employees into authorizing attacker-controlled connected apps within their Salesforce tenant" ([Microsoft Security blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/13/defending-saas-based-applications-against-shinyhunters-oauth-abuse/)). Background, August 7: at Abbott, "The intrusion began with a vishing attack," a phone scam, and the group claims rows from Databricks, a data platform ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/08/07/shinyhunters-called-cancer-diagnostics-biz-and-tricked-staffers-into-giving-them-access-now-theyve-dumped-109m-email-addresses/5284857)).

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Baxter confirmed an incident, files were posted, and Microsoft documented the method. What was taken rests on the group's word.

**Obvious explanation:** a criminal group working through Salesforce customers in every industry.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: "Customer data can be technically available while remaining operationally untrustworthy," and last week's question: who answers for what you rely on but do not run. Primary: Foundation (access, permissions, vendor dependence). Secondary: Operating Model, since the gap is an approval someone inside owns.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS**, for health companies' commercial systems only, not drug companies.

**Pattern basis:** third week running. Amgen's vendor-hosted clouds, then a billing vendor, now customer relationship software. Same condition. Stable in pharma, not accelerating.

## Signal 2: Sellers say the identity match, not the doctor list, decides who a brand reaches

**What happened**
- Observed: an advertising technology test claims the same verified doctor list reached 32% more doctors when matched a different way.
- Observed: a second seller wrote that most brands cannot tell whether their ads reach real, on-target doctors.

**Unit of analysis:** the identity matching step in digital ads aimed at doctors for specialty drug brands.

**Evidence**
- Wrango and OpenX, August 19: "Creative, budget, flight dates, and bid strategy were held constant," and one path gave "a 32% increase in physician reach on identical budgets." Wrango's chief executive: "Multi-signal identity resolution at the SSP [supply-side platform] layer is what actually expands the reachable audience" ([ExchangeWire, August 19](https://www.exchangewire.com/blog/2026/08/19/wrango-finds-32-more-hcps-with-supply-side-targeting-via-openxselecttm/)). Claimed. No sample size or outside check.
- Doceree's co-founder, August 21: "Most organizations still don't actually know whether their programmatic impressions are reaching licensed, on-target physicians" ([HIT Consultant, August 21](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/08/21/kamya-elawadhi-doceree-why-pharma-hcp-marketing-requires-deterministic-standards/)). Claimed.
- Two companies, both selling a fix. They agree on the gap, not the answer.

**Evidence strength: LOW. EARLY INDICATOR.** A vendor's test of its own product, plus an opinion piece.

**Obvious explanation:** advertising technology firms marketing their own targeting.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: "Local definitions, tagging, mappings, scripts, and ownership choices make the same data support different answers." Primary: Foundation (identity resolution, customer data connectivity). Secondary: Use, since reach per dollar is what the brand pays for.
- Direction: **STRENGTHENS**, weakly. If it holds, the same list gives a third more reach depending on the match path. Only sellers are saying it.

**Pattern basis:** first time in these briefs. Isolated.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: Owning the list is not the same as reaching the people on it**
- Easy to miss: the Wrango test held the doctor list fixed. Only the matching step changed. Two brands with the same clean list could reach very different doctors and never know.
- Why it matters: readiness talk stops at "is our customer data clean." This points one step later: does the data survive the hand-off into the systems that act on it? Inferred.

**KEY INSIGHT: The weak spot is the connections, not the systems**
- Easy to miss: Baxter says its products were not affected, and nothing public says Salesforce itself was broken. The loss came through approvals and connected apps. At Abbott, one phone call led into a data platform.
- Why it matters: "The pieces have owners. The connections often do not." Reviews cover each system. Often nobody owns the list of what may connect to it. Inferred.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **What else could explain this?** Signal 1 is a crime wave across industries and a people problem, closer to Operating Model than Foundation. Signal 2 is marketing.
- **Counterevidence:** Salesforce changed its defaults in 2025 so "Users can no longer self-authorize uninstalled apps by default" ([Arkus, February 2](https://www.arkusinc.com/archive/2026/salesforce-connected-app-security-changes-what-actually-happened-and-what-you-need-to-do)). The rest may be customer settings. And buyers are not fully silent: in October 2025, a ZS survey of 115 pharma and biotech technology executives found "68% say to neglect data quality and governance early is the main reason AI initiatives fail" ([ZS](https://www.zs.com/insights/scaling-ai-in-pharma-cdio-2026)). Background, and talk.
- **What would make us wrong:** Baxter says the application was not its customer relationship system. The posted files are old contact lists. An outside test finds no reach gap between match paths.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE:** two questions for a commercial data leader. "Which outside apps can read your customer relationship system, and who approved each one?" And: "Of the doctors on your target list, what share did your ads actually reach last quarter, and who measured it?" Blank answers mean the gap sits in the connections.
- **DEEPER ASSET:** not yet. Later, a hand-off check in the diagnostic: follow one customer record from the master list into media and see what survives.
- **LEARNING QUESTION:** When a brand sends the same doctor list to two media partners, does anyone compare how many of those doctors each one reached? If not, who would own that?
- **LONG-TERM VALUE:** a hand-off test, then buyer answers, then a match-loss measure a buyer can take to procurement.
