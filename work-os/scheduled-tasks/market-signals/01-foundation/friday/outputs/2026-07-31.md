## Signal 2: Pharma is renting the data layer from its owners

**What happened**
- Observed: drug companies signed large data licensing deals with Tempus.
- Claimed in a call summary: clients build models inside Tempus's environment, which locks them in.
- Inferred: paying for the layer under AI is moving from owning compute to renting data and models.

**Unit of analysis:** one oncology data company and its drug company clients. Research data, not commercial customer data.

**Evidence**
- Tempus, July 30: "Signed large deals with BioNTech, Daiichi Sankyo, Level Set Bio, and Incyte Pharmaceuticals, contributing to ~$200 million in total bookings this quarter." Data licensing and modeling revenue rose 36%. It "Successfully delivered our first oncology foundation model to AstraZeneca" ([Tempus via BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/tempus-reports-second-quarter-2026-results)). Observed.
- Call summary, July 31: newer deals "add stickiness by locking clients into the ecosystem through long-term contracts and models built in Tempus's environment" ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/tempus-ai-inc-tem-q2-050243586.html)). Summary wording.
- IQVIA's chief executive, July 28: data must meet "interoperability, relevance, completeness, traceability, reliability, and linkability standards," and "That's proprietary healthcare data, and we've got that." The seller calls data the hard part, and says it owns it.
- Repeated, not new: Bristol Myers Squibb's July 30 filing restates its July NVIDIA deal for "training large AI models on BMS's own data" ([Securities and Exchange Commission filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000014272/000001427226000018/a2026q2ex991-filing.htm)). Counts once.

**Evidence strength: MEDIUM. REINFORCING INDICATOR.** Real bookings, named buyers. No buyer result.

**Obvious explanation:** a fast-growing data company in a hot market for AI training data.

**Hypothesis update**
- Affected: "Organizations keep adding technology to a coordination and meaning problem." Primary: Foundation. Secondary: Operating Model.
- Direction: **COMPLICATES.** Buying a finished foundation skips internal joining work but adds dependence on one vendor's data and terms.

**Pattern basis:** last week, four companies paid to own compute and data. This week, data owners report the buying from the other side. Repeating, changing shape. No production outcome.

## Non-obvious analysis

**KEY INSIGHT: "Past foundation work" may mean the foundation moved into the vendor**
- Easy to miss: a client running on IQVIA's data, or building models inside Tempus, did not finish its foundation. It traded the work for a dependence.
- Why it matters: the question changes to: can we check the vendor's data, trace what the model used, and leave if we must? The diagnostic may need to cover rented foundations. Inferred, not seen at any buyer.

**KEY INSIGHT: Only sellers are describing buyer readiness**
- Easy to miss: "clients are ready" and "data is the hard part" both came from companies selling data.
- Why it matters: vendor calls show what vendors want investors to believe. The buyer's real state has to come from buyers.

## Challenge the interpretation

- **What else could explain this?** Launches rose 45%, so vendors sold more of everything. AI may be riding along. Tempus's growth may be research demand only.
- **Counterevidence:** IQVIA may be right. If clients run agents on IQVIA's data, the vendor did the joining, and that may be fine.
- **What would make us wrong:** a named commercial team reporting an agent result on vendor data with no internal fix and no lock-in complaint.

## Trigger

- IQVIA or a named client reports a commercial AI result with a number.
- IQVIA separates AI revenue from launch demand.
- A pharma company names vendor lock-in or a vendor switch as a data problem.
- A second commercial data vendor says clients are past foundation work.

## Value

- **FAST VALUE:** when a vendor says "you're past foundation work," ask: "Whose foundation is it?" and "Could we reproduce this without them?" Or ask the team to show evidence on IQVIA's own six tests for the vendor's data.
- **DEEPER ASSET:** not yet. If Signal 2 holds two more weeks, a short "rented foundation" check could sit inside the diagnostic.
- **LEARNING QUESTION:** Did commercial teams running IQVIA agents finish their own data joins first, or skip them by using IQVIA's data?
- **LONG-TERM VALUE:** if foundations keep moving into vendors, the lasting need is checking vendor data. Two questions, then buyer answers, then a vendor part of the diagnostic.

## Decision

- **What changed?** We now take seriously that some pharma teams rent their foundation. We do not believe foundations are finished.
- **Recommended state: INVESTIGATE.**
- **Why?** IQVIA's line directly challenges the core need hypothesis. Weak evidence, cheap check.
- **What moves it next?** One buyer's answer to the learning question, or a named IQVIA commercial result.
